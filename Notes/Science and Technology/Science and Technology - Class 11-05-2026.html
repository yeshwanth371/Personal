54. IRNSS / NavIC (completes Section 54 of the 4 Nov note)

54.1 The programme and its constellation

  • IRNSS (Indian Regional Navigation Satellite System), operational name NavIC ("Navigation with Indian Constellation" - the name given when the PM dedicated the system to the nation on 28 Apr 2016, the day IRNSS-1G was launched on PSLV-C33) (ISRO; PIB, 28 Apr 2016).
  • Class: announced in 2006 with 7 satellites. Checked: the Government gave financial sanction in May 2006 for Rs 1,420 crore (CAG Report No. 2 of 2018). [Additional]
  • Design: 7 satellites - 3 in geostationary orbit (GEO) at 32.5, 83 and 129.5 degrees E, and 4 in inclined geosynchronous orbit (IGSO), inclination 29 degrees, crossing the equator at 55 and 111.75 degrees E, two satellites at each crossing longitude (ISRO). Seen from the ground, each IGSO satellite traces a figure-of-8 (Figure 51) (c39).
  • [Additional] Why this mix: GEO satellites stay fixed over the equator; IGSO satellites swing north and south of it (to about 29 degrees N and S), which spreads the satellites across India's sky and improves geometry (Section 54.5). With only 7 satellites, every one matters - losing one degrades service.

[Correction] "3 in geo-stationary earth orbit and 4 in geo synchronous orbit in 2 peers forming a figure of 8": write "4 in inclined geosynchronous orbit (29 degrees), two in each of the planes crossing the equator at 55 and 111.75 degrees E". A GEO satellite is itself geosynchronous (a special case with 0 degrees inclination); what distinguishes the other four is the inclination. The figure-of-8 is the ground track of each IGSO satellite, not a formation of the satellites (ISRO; figure-of-8 ground track as captioned in eoPortal). (The 4 Nov note's c34 gives the same slots.)

54.2 Coverage and accuracy

  • Primary service area: India and up to 1,500 km beyond its boundary. Extended service area: up to the rectangle 30 degrees S-50 degrees N, 30-130 degrees E (ISRO).
  • Class: accuracy 10 m over the Indian landmass and 20 m in adjoining areas. Checked: ISRO's current figure for the open service (SPS) is better than 20 m (2-sigma) over the whole primary service area (c40).
  • Class: timing accuracy 40-50 ns. Checked: "better than 40 ns (2-sigma)" on ISRO's NVS-02 page; "better than 50 ns" on the older Services page (c41).
  • Two services (see the 4 Nov note, Section 53.3): SPS (open, civil) and RS (Restricted Service, encrypted, for authorised users). NavIC also broadcasts short messages (for example, alerts to fishermen); five older satellites now serve only messaging (Section 54.7).

[Correction] "10 m over the Indian landmass, 20 m in adjoining areas": the official specification is a single figure - position accuracy better than 20 m (2-sigma) over the primary service area (India + 1,500 km) (ISRO, NVS-02 page, 24 Jan 2025). "Better than 10 m over India" was ISRO's expectation reported in 2013 (ET/PTI, 28 May 2013), and 10 m is sometimes quoted as a design goal in the press (4 Nov note, Indian Express). Write "better than 20 m" for the exam. "2-sigma" means about 95% of fixes fall within that error. The class sentence "the IRNSS is providing the ... over the Indian landmass" had a missing word; read "providing positioning, navigation and timing (PNT) services".

[Correction] Timing accuracy "40-50 ns": acceptable as a range, but the newer ISRO statement is better than 40 ns (2-sigma) (ISRO, NVS-02); the 50 ns figure is on ISRO's older Services page (ISRO). Sources disagree; quote "better than 40 ns" and note the older 50 ns figure if needed.

54.4 Atomic clocks - why they are needed and how they work

Principle: a receiver finds its distance from each satellite as distance = speed of light x signal travel time. Light covers about 300 km in 1 millisecond and about 30 cm in 1 nanosecond, so a tiny timing error becomes a large position error (NIST; NovAtel: 10 ns = 3 m).

  • Quartz clocks (watches, phones): a voltage makes a quartz crystal vibrate at a steady frequency (piezoelectric effect); the vibrations are counted to keep time. But quartz drifts with temperature and age. Class: quartz accumulates about 1 ms of error a day, equal to 300 km - so it cannot be the master clock on a navigation satellite (c42).
  • Atomic clock = quartz oscillator + atoms (caesium or rubidium) as a reference. Atoms of one element absorb microwaves at exactly the same frequency everywhere and always, so they can "discipline" the quartz (Figure 52).

9,192,631,770 cycles = 1 s
  • The SI second is defined as the duration of 9,192,631,770 periods of the radiation of the transition between the two hyperfine levels of the ground state of the caesium-133 atom (BIPM). The number is exact by definition.
  • Rubidium-87 clocks use a similar transition at about 6,834,682,610.904 Hz (6.83 GHz) (BIPM recommended value, 2025). They are smaller and lighter, so NavIC uses rubidium clocks.
  • Other atomic clocks (class list, corrected names): hydrogen maser (uses hydrogen's hyperfine transition at about 1.42 GHz; very stable over hours to days - flown on Galileo satellites) and optical clocks, which use a laser instead of microwaves to probe atoms of strontium, ytterbium, mercury or ions such as aluminium; their higher frequency makes them the most precise clocks built, and they are candidates for a future redefinition of the second (NIST) (c43).

[Correction] "Quartz accumulates error at 1 ms every day": the arithmetic is right (1 ms x 300,000 km/s = 300 km), but ordinary quartz is worse: NIST says a quartz wristwatch "may be accurate to within about 15 seconds per month" (about 0.5 s a day) (NIST). Read "1 ms per day" as an illustration of why even small drifts are unacceptable.

[Correction] "The quartz frequency is used for exciting the outermost electron of the caesium atom": the microwaves do not lift the electron to a higher orbit. They drive a hyperfine transition - a switch between two energy levels of the ground state that differ in how the outer electron's spin is aligned with the nucleus's spin (BIPM wording). The servo maximises the number of atoms that changed state. Also: "9192......... Hz" = 9,192,631,770 Hz; MASER = (hydrogen) maser, "microwave amplification by stimulated emission of radiation"; "attervium" = ytterbium. Hydrogen-maser frequency and the optical-clock species list are from standard references and were not re-read in this session [Verification Required].

54.6 Significance of IRNSS / NavIC

Economy
  • Positioning and timing underpin the gig and platform economy (ride-hailing, food and parcel delivery, logistics tracking). NITI Aayog: 77 lakh (7.7 million) gig workers in 2020-21, projected to reach 2.35 crore (23.5 million) by 2029-30 (PIB, 27 Jun 2022) (c45).
  • Indian start-ups and map companies can build services on NavIC's open (SPS) signal, whose interface specification ISRO publishes; for example, ISRO lists a MapmyIndia NavIC message-receiver app for fishermen (ISRO apps) (c46).
  • [Additional] Timing for telecom networks, banking time-stamps and power grids (see the 2018 GPS PYQ in the 4 Nov note).
Strategic and security
  • Strategic autonomy: the encrypted RS serves the armed forces without dependence on foreign systems (the 1999 Kargil GPS-denial lesson).
  • Supports India's aim to be a net security provider in the Indian Ocean Region / Indo-Pacific - the term used in the MoD's 2026 'RAKSHA' document (PIB, 3 Sep 2026) - and maritime domain awareness (MDA): tracking vessels, coastal security and messaging to fishing boats (c47).
  • Freedom of navigation in the South China Sea matters to India's Act East policy and trade; India supports it under UNCLOS (c48).
Governance and services
  • Border management, disaster management, search and rescue, terrestrial, aerial and marine navigation, fleet management, vehicle tracking.
  • Short messages to fishermen about weather, cyclones and potential fishing zones.
Diplomacy
  • Space diplomacy: NavIC's extended service area covers much of the Indian Ocean and neighbouring countries. When dedicating NavIC (28 Apr 2016) the PM said SAARC neighbours could use its services if they wished (PIB, PM's statement; PTI via Business Standard). No formal agreement to provide NavIC services to another country was found in this session [Verification Required].

56. Large Hadron Collider (LHC)

56.1 Hadrons and the machine

  • Class: "hadron" is a collective term for the proton and the neutron. Checked: a hadron is any composite particle made of quarks held together by the strong force - baryons (three quarks, e.g. proton, neutron) and mesons (a quark and an antiquark, e.g. pion) (CERN glossary) (c49).
  • The LHC, at CERN (European Organization for Nuclear Research), is the world's largest and most powerful particle accelerator: a 27 km ring of superconducting magnets in a tunnel about 100 m underground on the France-Switzerland border near Geneva. First started on 10 Sep 2008 (CERN).
  • How it works: two beams of protons travel in opposite directions in two separate beam pipes at ultra-high vacuum. 1,232 superconducting dipole magnets, cooled to -271.3 degrees C (1.9 K), bend the beams around the ring; accelerating cavities raise their energy. The beams are made to collide at four points, where the detectors ATLAS, CMS, ALICE and LHCb record the debris (CERN) (c50).
  • Energy: 13.6 TeV collisions (6.8 TeV per beam) in Run 3, from 5 Jul 2022 (CERN). At 6.8 TeV a proton moves at about 99.999999% of the speed of light (calculated: Lorentz factor about 7,250).
  • Besides protons, the LHC collides lead ions, creating a quark-gluon plasma - the state of matter thought to have filled the universe a few millionths of a second after the Big Bang (studied mainly by ALICE) (CERN).

[Correction] "Hadron = proton and neutron collectively": proton and neutron together are nucleons. Hadrons are the wider family of quark composites - baryons (proton, neutron and others) and mesons (CERN). The LHC is named for the protons (and lead nuclei) it accelerates; it does not accelerate neutrons, which have no charge.

[Correction] "27 km tunnel 100 m below the surface; proton beams released in opposite directions ... crashed into each other": correct in substance. Precision: depth varies from about 50 m to 175 m (deepest under the Jura) (ICE); beams run in separate pipes and cross only at the four detectors; superconducting magnets steer and focus the beams, while radio-frequency cavities accelerate them. "Conditions just after the Big Bang" applies most directly to lead-ion collisions (quark-gluon plasma).

56.2 Questions the LHC addresses

The three questions from class, with checked context
QuestionWhat is known (as of 7 Nov 2026)
A. How did mass originate?Higgs boson discovered by ATLAS and CMS, announced 4 Jul 2012 (mass about 125 GeV); Nobel Prize in Physics 2013 to Francois Englert and Peter Higgs (ATLAS/CERN). The Higgs field gives mass to elementary particles (W and Z bosons, quarks, electrons). But the up and down quark masses make up only about 1% of a proton's mass; the rest comes from the energy of the strong interaction binding quarks and gluons (APS Physics, 2018) (c51).
B. What are dark matter and dark energy?Dark matter is about 27% and dark energy about 68% of the universe; ordinary matter about 5% (CERN). Dark matter does not emit or absorb light; the LHC searches for particles that could make it up. No dark-matter particle has been found.
C. Why is there so little antimatter?Matter and antimatter should have formed in equal amounts at the Big Bang, yet the universe is made of matter. Physicists study CP violation (small differences between matter and antimatter). LHCb reported the first observation of CP violation in baryon decays (Nature, 16 Jul 2025; 5.2 sigma) - a piece of the puzzle, not the full answer (CERN) (c52).

[Correction] "The LHC will find how the origin of mass took place": write it as "how elementary particles acquire mass (the Higgs mechanism)". The Higgs boson was found in 2012; the LHC now measures its properties. Most of the mass of ordinary matter (protons and neutrons) comes from strong-force energy, not directly from the Higgs field (APS).

[Correction] "Anti-matter is not found in nature": antimatter does occur naturally, in tiny amounts - in cosmic rays and even in beams produced above thunderstorms (NASA Fermi) - but it is annihilated quickly on meeting matter. The real puzzle is why the universe is dominated by matter (CERN).

56.3 India, CERN and the LHC's status [Additional]

  • India became an Associate Member State of CERN on 16 Jan 2017; it had helped build the LHC and contributes to the CMS and ALICE experiments and the LHC Computing Grid (Tier-2 centres in Mumbai and Kolkata) (CERN). IIT Jodhpur joined CMS as a full member in July 2026 (ETV Bharat, news).
  • Status (as of 7 Nov 2026): Run 3 has ended. Last proton collisions 16 May 2026, last lead-lead collisions 14 Jun 2026, final beams 27 Jun 2026 (CERN Courier); on 29 Jun 2026 CERN switched the LHC off for Long Shutdown 3 (LS3) to install the High-Luminosity LHC (HL-LHC), due to begin operation in 2030 with up to ten times the luminosity (more collisions) (CERN). The LHC is therefore not running at present.
  • Other result in the news: ALICE showed in May 2025 that lead-lead near-miss collisions turn lead into gold nuclei briefly (about 89,000 nuclei per second at peak) (CERN, 8 May 2025).

Mains Points

  • Strategic autonomy in PNT: NavIC frees critical users (armed forces, power, telecom, banking) from dependence on foreign systems that can be denied or degraded in a crisis. But a 7-satellite regional system has no redundancy: clock failures, the 2017 PSLV-C39 loss and the 2025 NVS-02 failure left only 3 working satellites in 2026.
  • Self-reliance in critical components: the shift from imported to indigenous rubidium clocks (NVS-01 onward) is the key fix; the next steps are space-qualified clock reliability, in-orbit spares and faster replacement launches.
  • Adoption: a navigation system creates value only if receivers use it. L1 on NVS satellites, chipset support and a clear, notified policy for devices, vehicle trackers and fishing boats are needed; NavIC works best alongside GPS, Galileo and others (multi-GNSS), not as a replacement.
  • Economy and governance: location and time services enable the gig and logistics economy (23.5 million gig workers projected by 2029-30), precision agriculture, disaster response and maritime safety.
  • Foreign policy: NavIC's coverage over the Indian Ocean supports maritime domain awareness and India's role as a net security provider; the 2016 offer of NavIC services to SAARC neighbours is a space-diplomacy tool (no formal agreement found).
  • Big science (LHC): basic research has long payoffs - the World Wide Web (CERN), medical imaging and accelerator-based cancer therapy, computing grids. India's associate membership gives its scientists and industry access at a fraction of full-member cost; the issue is building domestic capacity (detectors, superconducting magnets, computing).
  • Way forward: launch NVS-04 and NVS-05 on a firm schedule and keep a spare; restore the full 7-satellite service before any expansion; notify device-support rules; encourage start-ups through IN-SPACe; and deepen India's role in international science projects such as CERN and the HL-LHC.

Common Traps / Confusions

  • NavIC's 4 non-GEO satellites are in inclined geosynchronous orbit; GEO is also geosynchronous. The figure-of-8 is the ground track.
  • NavIC coverage is 1,500 km beyond India (not "1,500 sq km" or "5,500 sq km" - see the 2018 PYQ).
  • Official accuracy is better than 20 m; "10 m" is a 2013 expectation / press figure.
  • Design 7, working 3 (2026). NVS-03 launched but is not yet operational.
  • Atomic clocks drive a hyperfine transition in the ground state, not an excitation of the electron to a higher orbit. Caesium defines the second; NavIC uses rubidium.
  • MASER = microwave amplification (hydrogen maser), not a laser; optical clocks use lasers.
  • Dilution of precision (not "of position"): satellite geometry, not signal quality.
  • Hadron is not only proton + neutron (that is a nucleon). LHC accelerates protons and lead ions, not neutrons.
  • The Higgs gives mass to elementary particles; most of a proton's mass is strong-force energy.
  • Antimatter exists in nature in tiny amounts; the puzzle is the imbalance.
  • The LHC is not running now (Long Shutdown 3 since Jun 2026).
  • BrahMos exports are not part of NavIC; the Philippines was the first export customer (2022).
