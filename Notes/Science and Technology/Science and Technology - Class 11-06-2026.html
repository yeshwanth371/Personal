57. Standard Model of Physics (completes Section 57 of the 7 Nov note)

57.1 What the Standard Model is

  • The Standard Model (SM) is the most successful theory of the building blocks of matter and the forces between them. It was assembled in the 1970s and every particle it predicted has since been found - the last being the Higgs boson, announced at CERN on 4 July 2012 (CERN; CERN Higgs page; 5 Nov note, Section 56.2).
  • Class: "12 fundamental particles and 4 fundamental forces". Checked: the SM has 12 matter particles (fermions) - 6 quarks + 6 leptons - plus the force-carrying bosons (gluon, photon, W and Z) and the Higgs boson. It explains 3 of the 4 forces; gravity is not included (c53; Section 57.4).
  • Each fermion has an antiparticle with the same mass and opposite charge (for example the positron for the electron; anti-quarks build the antiproton and antineutron).

[Correction] "12 fundamental particles and 4 fundamental forces": add that the 12 are the matter particles (fermions); the Standard Model also contains 5 kinds of bosons (gluon, photon, Z, W and the Higgs), and it describes only three forces - strong, electromagnetic and weak. Gravity is outside the SM (its carrier, the graviton, is hypothetical), which the class itself notes at the end (CERN: The Standard Model).

57.2 Quarks and the particles they build

  • Six quarks ("flavours") in three pairs (generations): up and down; charm and strange; top and bottom. Up-type quarks (u, c, t) carry charge +2/3; down-type (d, s, b) carry -1/3 †. The top is the heaviest known elementary particle (about 173 GeV, close to a gold atom) †.
  • Class: "up and down are most stable". Better: up and down are the lightest quarks; the heavier quarks decay quickly into them, which is why all ordinary matter is made of up and down quarks (c54).
  • Proton = 2 up + 1 down (uud), charge +2/3 +2/3 -1/3 = +1; neutron = 1 up + 2 down (udd), charge +2/3 -1/3 -1/3 = 0 (Figure 56).
  • Particles of three quarks are baryons (proton, neutron). [Additional] A quark + antiquark pair forms a meson (for example the pion). Baryons and mesons together are hadrons - the "H" of the LHC (5 Nov note, Section 56.1).
  • For every quark there is an antiquark; antiquarks build antiparticles, for example the antiproton (two anti-up + one anti-down, charge -1) and the antineutron.
  • Quarks carry electric charge, "colour" charge (the source of the strong force), weak charge and mass, so they experience all four fundamental forces (CERN). Quarks are never seen alone; the strong force confines them inside hadrons †.

[Correction] "Among them up and down are most stable": quarks are not "stable" or "unstable" on their own because they never exist freely. The precise statement is that up and down are the lightest quarks, so heavier quarks decay into them; hence protons and neutrons, and all ordinary matter, contain only up and down quarks. Note also that the proton is stable, but a free neutron decays in about 15 minutes (it is stable only inside nuclei) †.

57.3 Leptons

  • Six leptons: three negatively charged - electron, muon, tau - and three neutral partners, the neutrinos (electron-neutrino, muon-neutrino, tau-neutrino) (CERN).
  • Mass: electron lightest (0.511 MeV), muon about 105.7 MeV (about 207 times the electron), tau heaviest (about 1,777 MeV - heavier than a proton) (PDG).
  • Leptons have no colour charge, so they do not feel the strong force. Charged leptons feel electromagnetic, weak and gravitational forces; neutrinos feel only the weak force and gravity (Section 59.1).
  • [Additional] Muon and tau are unstable (the muon lives about 2.2 microseconds †). Cosmic-ray muons reach the ground and are a background that underground neutrino labs must shield against (Sections 59.3, 59.5).

57.4 The four fundamental forces and their carrier bosons (class discussed this after neutrinos)

  • Class order "gravitation, weak, electromagnetic, strong" is the order of increasing strength: gravity < weak < electromagnetic < strong. Approximate relative strengths: strong 1, electromagnetic about 1/137, weak about 10-6, gravity about 10-39 (c55).
  • In quantum field theory each force is carried by exchanging a boson: gluon (strong), photon (electromagnetic), W+, W- and Z0 (weak) and the hypothetical graviton (gravity).
  • Detection: photon (long known), gluon (three-jet events at DESY's PETRA collider, 1979), W and Z (CERN UA1/UA2, 1983; Nobel 1984 to Carlo Rubbia and Simon van der Meer) (CERN: Z boson). The graviton has never been detected. LIGO's detection of gravitational waves (14 Sep 2015, announced 11 Feb 2016; Nobel 2017) confirmed the waves, not the graviton (NobelPrize.org 2017).
  • So the class conclusion stands: the Standard Model cannot account for gravity; gravity is described separately by Einstein's general relativity, and the two have not been unified.
  • The Higgs boson is not a force carrier: it is the quantum of the Higgs field, which gives mass to the W and Z bosons, quarks and charged leptons (CERN).

[Correction] "4 fundamental forces in the following order of strength: Gravitation, weak, electromagnetic, strong" and "the origin of force is attributed to the particles called bosons": the order is right only if read as weakest to strongest; state it explicitly (Strong > EM > Weak > Gravity). And not every boson carries a force - the Higgs boson is a scalar boson linked to mass, not a force (CERN; CERN Higgs).

57.5 What the Standard Model does not explain [Additional]

Open questions
  • Gravity - no quantum theory, no graviton.
  • Neutrino mass - the original SM treats neutrinos as massless; oscillation proves they have mass (Section 59.2).
  • Dark matter and dark energy - about 27% and 68% of the universe's energy content, not explained (Section 60; 7 Nov note).
  • Matter-antimatter imbalance - SM CP violation is too small (5 Nov note, c52).
Where answers are sought
  • Colliders: LHC and the High-Luminosity LHC (from 2030).
  • Neutrino experiments: IceCube, JUNO, DUNE, Hyper-Kamiokande, KATRIN (Section 59.4).
  • Dark-matter searches and cosmology surveys (next class).
  • Gravitational-wave observatories (LIGO; LIGO-India, approved by the Union Cabinet in 2023 (LIGO, and LIGO-India under construction †)#8224;).

59. Neutrinos - the "Ghost Particle"

59.1 What a neutrino is and why it is a "ghost"

  • Name: Italian for "little neutral one", coined by Enrico Fermi; the particle was proposed by Wolfgang Pauli in 1930 to save energy conservation in beta decay (The Hindu; US DOE). Popularly the "ghost particle".
  • Neutrinos are the second most abundant particle in the universe, after photons (US DOE).
  • Sources - wherever there is a nuclear reaction or radioactive decay: the Sun and stars (fusion), supernovae, nuclear reactors (antineutrinos), cosmic rays striking the atmosphere ("atmospheric neutrinos"), the Earth itself (geoneutrinos from uranium, thorium and potassium decay) and relics of the Big Bang (US DOE). Class: "planets" - the verified example is the Earth's geoneutrinos.
  • Why a "ghost": neutrinos have no electric charge (so no electromagnetic force) and no colour charge (so no strong force). They feel only the weak force and gravity, and the weak force acts only over about 10-18 m, so a neutrino almost never hits anything (c56).
  • Numbers: about 65 billion (6.5 x 1010) solar neutrinos cross every square centimetre of the Earth's surface - and of your body - every second (radioactivity.eu.com). Over a whole human lifetime, only about one (popular estimates: one to a few) is expected to interact with an atom of the body (neutrino-science.org; c57).

[Correction] "They rarely interact with matter as they do not experience strong force": incomplete. Electrons also do not feel the strong force, yet they interact readily. The real reason is that neutrinos are electrically neutral and colourless, so they feel neither the electromagnetic nor the strong force; only the extremely short-range weak force (and negligible gravity) remains - as the class's next line correctly says (US DOE).

[Correction] "65 billion neutrinos pass per cm2 of human body ... in the entire life span ... they can interact with one atom": the 65 billion figure is the solar neutrino flux per cm2 per second at Earth (true for any surface, including the body). The lifetime figure is an order-of-magnitude popular estimate - sources say "perhaps only one" or "one to a few"; write "roughly one" and do not treat it as a measured value (radioactivity.eu.com; neutrino-science.org; both secondary).

59.2 Three flavours and neutrino oscillation

  • Three flavours: electron-neutrino, muon-neutrino, tau-neutrino (one partner for each charged lepton, Section 57.3).
  • Neutrino oscillation: a neutrino born as one flavour can be detected as another after travelling some distance (Figure 58). Each flavour is a quantum mixture of three mass states; because these have slightly different masses, the mixture changes as the neutrino travels.
  • Key inference: oscillation is possible only if neutrinos have mass. The original Standard Model treated them as massless, so this is evidence of physics beyond the Standard Model.
  • Nobel Prize in Physics 2015: Takaaki Kajita (Super-Kamiokande, Japan - atmospheric neutrinos, 1998) and Arthur B. McDonald (Sudbury Neutrino Observatory, Canada - solar neutrinos, 2001) "for the discovery of neutrino oscillations, which shows that neutrinos have mass" (NobelPrize.org).
  • Still unknown [Additional]: the absolute masses (best direct limit: below 0.45 eV, KATRIN, April 2025, about a million times lighter than the electron) and the mass ordering (whether the third mass state is the heaviest - "normal" - or the lightest - "inverted") (KIT; Section 59.4).

59.3 IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics

In the news (6 October 2026): the Nobel Prize in Physics 2026 was awarded to Francis Halzen (University of Wisconsin-Madison; born 1944, Tienen, Belgium), sole laureate, "for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin" (NobelPrize.org press release). Halzen first proposed a South Pole neutrino detector in 1988. He also received the 2026 APS Medal for Exceptional Achievement in Research (announced December 2025) (APS). (c58)

  • Where: at the Amundsen-Scott South Pole Station, Antarctica; run by UW-Madison with US National Science Foundation funding and an international collaboration (IceCube).
  • Design (class verified): about one cubic kilometre of ice instrumented with 86 strings (cables), each with 60 Digital Optical Modules (DOMs) = 5,160 sensors, placed between 1,450 m and 2,450 m deep. The last string went in in December 2010; full physics running began in May 2011 (IceCube).
  • IceCube Upgrade [Additional]: 5 new, denser strings with more than 600 new and enhanced sensors were installed at the bottom centre in the 2025-26 polar season (release, 12 Feb 2026) - descoped from the 7 strings planned earlier (IceCube, 2026).
  • Operating principle (Cherenkov radiation):
    1. A neutrino, very rarely, interacts (weak force) with a nucleus of an atom in the ice (frozen water).
    2. This produces a charged lepton (electron, muon or tau) and a spray of particles.
    3. If the charged particle moves faster than light travels in ice (about 0.76 c; nothing beats light in vacuum, c), it emits a cone of faint blue Cherenkov light - the optical equivalent of a sonic boom †.
    4. The DOMs record the light and its arrival time; the cone's axis follows the lepton, which follows the neutrino's direction, so the source can be located in the sky; the amount of light gives the energy (IceCube, 2023).
  • This makes neutrino astronomy possible: unlike light, neutrinos cross dust, gas and magnetic fields unchanged and point straight back to their sources.

59.5 India-based Neutrino Observatory (INO)

  • What: a multi-institutional underground laboratory led by TIFR, Mumbai, jointly funded by the Department of Atomic Energy and the Department of Science and Technology; sanctioned cost Rs 1,583 crore; Union Cabinet approval in 2015 (PIB; ThePrint, 2025).
  • Site: Pottipuram, Bodi West Hills, Theni district, Tamil Nadu - a cavern under about 1,200 m of rock (to block cosmic-ray muons), reached by a tunnel of about 1,900 m (PIB; ThePrint; c61).
  • Detector - Iron Calorimeter (ICAL): about 51,000 tonnes of magnetised iron in three modules, 151 layers of 5.6 cm iron plates, with about 28,800 glass Resistive Plate Chambers (RPCs) in the 4 cm gaps; copper coils give a field of about 1.3-1.5 tesla, so the bending of a muon's track shows whether it came from a neutrino or an antineutrino - the key to the mass ordering (Figure 60) (INO collaboration, arXiv 2023; ICHEP 2018; c62). It would have been the world's largest electromagnet (TIFR / IEEE 2018).
  • What exists: an 85-tonne prototype, mini-ICAL, operating at Madurai; INO trained about 42 PhD students (arXiv; Times of India, 8 Oct 2025).
  • Status (8 Nov 2026): stalled - no construction at the site. Reasons reported: local and political opposition, fears (unfounded) of radiation or nuclear waste because DAE is a funder, Western Ghats ecology and tiger-corridor concerns, dam-safety worries, and weak public outreach. No official closure order was found; "shelved" is the press description (Times of India, 2025; ThePrint, 2025; c63).

Key Facts for Prelims

  • Standard Model: 12 fermions = 6 quarks (up/down, charm/strange, top/bottom) + 6 leptons (electron, muon, tau + 3 neutrinos); gauge bosons gluon, photon, W, Z; Higgs (4 Jul 2012, CERN). Explains 3 forces, not gravity.
  • Quark charges +2/3 (u, c, t) and -1/3 (d, s, b). Proton uud (+1), neutron udd (0). Baryon = 3 quarks; meson = quark + antiquark; both = hadrons. Top quark heaviest elementary particle.
  • Tau heaviest lepton (about 1,777 MeV), electron lightest (0.511 MeV). Leptons do not feel the strong force.
  • Forces, weakest to strongest: gravity < weak < electromagnetic < strong. Carriers: graviton (hypothetical), W± and Z (CERN 1983), photon, gluon (DESY 1979).
  • Neutrino: "little neutral one" (Fermi); proposed by Pauli 1930; second most abundant particle after the photon; feels only weak force and gravity; about 65 billion solar neutrinos per cm2 per second at Earth.
  • Three flavours; oscillation proves mass; Nobel 2015: Takaaki Kajita (Super-Kamiokande) and Arthur B. McDonald (SNO). KATRIN mass limit < 0.45 eV (2025).
  • Nobel Physics 2026: Francis Halzen (sole) - IceCube and high-energy astrophysical neutrinos (6 Oct 2026).
  • IceCube: South Pole; about 1 km3 ice; 86 strings x 60 DOMs = 5,160; 1,450-2,450 m deep; completed Dec 2010; detects Cherenkov light; Upgrade of 5 strings (2025-26).
  • Cherenkov radiation: light emitted when a charged particle moves faster than light's speed in that medium (never faster than c).
  • Detectors: Baikal-GVD (Russia, lake water, 0.8 km3 in 2026), JUNO (China, 20 kt liquid scintillator, mass ordering), NOvA (USA, 810 km beam), OPERA (Gran Sasso, Italy; tau appearance), KATRIN (Germany, mass), DUNE (USA), Hyper-K (Japan, 2028).
  • INO: Bodi West Hills, Pottipuram, Theni, Tamil Nadu; TIFR-led, DAE + DST; Rs 1,583 crore, approved 2015; ICAL 51 kt magnetised iron + about 28,800 RPCs; about 1,200 m rock cover; mini-ICAL (85 t) at Madurai; stalled.
  • Kolar Gold Fields (Karnataka): first detection of atmospheric neutrinos, 1965 (TIFR-Osaka-Durham).
  • Geoneutrinos: from U, Th, K decay; KamLAND, Borexino; not an earthquake signal.

Mains Points

  • Basic research as national capability: the Standard Model, the Higgs and neutrino oscillation came from decades-long "big science" investments; the 2026 Nobel rewards an idea first proposed in 1988. Spin-offs include detectors, cryogenics, fast electronics, computing grids and trained people - INO alone produced about 42 PhDs even without its main detector.
  • India's position: a pioneer (KGF, 1965) that lost its underground lab when the mines closed; INO, approved in 2015, is stalled while China's JUNO (data since 2025) and Russia's Baikal-GVD move ahead. India participates through CERN associate membership (2017) and international collaborations, but without a domestic flagship it risks brain drain and a weaker voice in setting the research agenda.
  • Why INO stalled - governance lessons: fears of radiation (neutrinos are harmless and the lab is not nuclear), Western Ghats ecology, dam-safety worries, litigation and politics; scientists themselves admit weak outreach and not building a small demonstration tunnel. Lesson: early public consultation, independent environmental and geological studies, transparent communication and centre-state consensus are as important as technical design.
  • Applications - realistic view: neutrino astronomy and Earth-interior studies are demonstrated; reactor monitoring for non-proliferation is promising; communication and "faster internet" are speculative; earthquake prediction is not supported. Balanced answers should separate demonstrated, plausible and speculative uses.
  • Way forward: revive INO or relocate it with a consensus-based site process; consider shallow-depth or modular detectors (the mini-ICAL veto-wall studies); deepen roles in DUNE, Hyper-K and IceCube-Gen2; link the lab to multi-use research (dark matter, geophysics, low-background physics); and strengthen science communication through ANRF and DST outreach.

Common Traps / Confusions

  • "12 particles" are the fermions only; the SM also has bosons, and covers 3 forces, not 4.
  • The Higgs boson is not a force carrier; the graviton is undetected - gravitational waves (LIGO 2015) are not gravitons.
  • Gravity is the weakest force; the class list "gravity, weak, EM, strong" is in increasing order.
  • Neutron is neutral but made of charged quarks (udd); a neutrino is an elementary lepton. Do not mix them.
  • Neutrinos feel the weak force (that is how they are detected) - they are not "non-interacting".
  • Cherenkov light: faster than light in the medium (ice, water), never faster than c in vacuum.
  • IceCube is in ice at the South Pole; Baikal-GVD, KM3NeT and Super-/Hyper-K use water; JUNO uses liquid scintillator; INO would use iron + RPCs.
  • JUNO / NOvA measure mass ordering and mass differences; KATRIN measures absolute mass.
  • INO is in Theni (Tamil Nadu), not Thane; first site Singara (Nilgiris) was rejected. KGF is in Karnataka.
  • Nobel 2015 = oscillation (Kajita, McDonald); Nobel 2026 = Halzen (IceCube). Nobel 2013 = Higgs mechanism (Englert, Higgs).
  • Geoneutrinos come from radioactive decay, not from earthquakes.

30-Second Revision

Standard Model: 6 quarks (u d, c s, t b) + 6 leptons (e, mu, tau + 3 neutrinos) + gluon, photon, W, Z + Higgs (2012); no gravity. Proton uud, neutron udd; baryon 3q, meson q-qbar. Forces: gravity < weak < EM < strong; graviton never seen. Neutrino: Fermi's "little neutral one", 2nd most abundant, weak + gravity only, 65 billion/cm2/s, 3 flavours oscillate, so mass (Nobel 2015 Kajita, McDonald), < 0.45 eV (KATRIN). IceCube: South Pole, 1 km3, 86 x 60 = 5,160 DOMs, Cherenkov cone points to source; Nobel 2026 Francis Halzen. Others: Baikal-GVD (Russia), JUNO (China, ordering), NOvA (USA), OPERA (Italy), DUNE, Hyper-K. INO: Bodi West Hills, Theni, TN; ICAL 51 kt iron + RPCs; Rs 1,583 crore, 2015; stalled. KGF 1965 first atmospheric neutrinos. Uses: astronomy, Earth interior, reactor monitoring; communication speculative; no earthquake link.
