60. Dark Matter and Dark Energy (completes the Section 60 placeholder)

The universe's energy content has three parts: dark energy about 68%, dark matter about 27% and ordinary (baryonic) matter about 5% - everything ever seen with telescopes is in that last 5% (NASA: Dark matter; NASA: Dark energy). "Dark" means something different in each name: for dark matter it means invisible; for dark energy it means unknown.

60.1 Dark matter - what it is

  • Dark matter is matter that exerts gravity but does not emit, absorb or reflect light - it does not seem to interact with the electromagnetic spectrum at all, so telescopes cannot see it (NASA). Here dark = invisible.
  • Share: about 27% of the universe and about 85% of all matter (c67).
  • Origin of the idea [Additional]: Fritz Zwicky (1933) found that galaxies in the Coma Cluster moved too fast to be held by the gravity of their visible matter; Vera Rubin (1970s) found the same "missing mass" in spiral galaxies (NASA).

[Correction] "Einstein in general theory of relativity said emptiness is not nothing ... the invisible form of matter is dark matter": two ideas are mixed. (1) Dark matter was not proposed by Einstein or by general relativity; it came from observations by Zwicky (1933) and Rubin (1970s). (2) "Empty space is not nothing" is a NASA paraphrase ("Einstein was the first person to realize that empty space is not nothing"), not an Einstein quotation, and it refers to the energy of empty space - dark energy and the cosmological constant (Section 60.8), not to hidden matter (NASA-funded Universe Forum, archived; NASA: Dark matter).

[Correction] "80% of the total matter": NASA gives about 85% of all matter (27 / (27 + 5) = 84%); "about 27% of the universe" is correct (NASA).

[Correction] "Invisible because it is chargeless": the observed fact is that dark matter does not interact electromagnetically (no emission, absorption or reflection of light). Having no electric charge is consistent with this but is not the whole story - neutrons are chargeless yet are ordinary matter. What dark matter is made of is still unknown (NASA).

60.3 Role of dark matter - the "glue" or "skeleton" of the universe

  • Dark matter's extra gravity holds galaxies and clusters together; without it fast-moving stars would fly apart. The class image of a glue or endoskeleton of the galaxy is apt.
  • [Additional] It also acted as the scaffolding on which galaxies formed: dark matter clumped first, and ordinary gas fell into its gravity wells, giving the "cosmic web" of filaments and clusters. Each galaxy sits inside a much larger, roughly spherical dark-matter halo.

60.7 Dark energy - accelerating expansion

  • Older expectation: the expansion begun at the Big Bang would slow down under gravity and might stop.
  • 1998: two teams studying distant Type Ia supernovae found them dimmer than expected - farther away than a slowing universe allows. The expansion is accelerating: galaxies recede ever faster. Nobel Prize in Physics 2011: Saul Perlmutter, Brian Schmidt and Adam Riess (NASA: Dark energy).
  • Dark energy is the name for whatever causes this acceleration. "Dark" = unknown. It is about 68% of the universe (NASA: about 68.3-70%).
  • NASA describes one explanation: dark energy as a property of space. Because this energy belongs to space itself, more space brings more of it; its push therefore grows relative to gravity, and expansion accelerates (class point; Figure 65).

60.8 Cosmological constant (Lambda)

  • Einstein (1917) added a constant term, Lambda (Λ), to general relativity to balance gravity and keep the universe static. After Hubble showed the universe expands (1929), he dropped it, reportedly calling it his "biggest blunder" - a quote known only second-hand through George Gamow (NASA).
  • Today Λ has returned as the simplest explanation of dark energy: a constant energy density of empty space (the "ΛCDM" standard model of cosmology = Λ + cold dark matter) †.

[Correction] "Dark energy is derived from an unknown cosmological constant": the cosmological constant is the leading candidate explanation, not an established source. Dark energy might instead change over time. In the news [Additional]: DESI (Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument) results in March 2025 preferred evolving dark energy at 2.8-4.2 sigma depending on data combinations; its July 2026 update gave 2.7 sigma (DESI + CMB) and 3.2 sigma (with supernovae) - suggestive, below the 5-sigma discovery standard (Berkeley Lab, Mar 2025; arXiv, Jul 2026).

60.9 Evidence: expansion and its acceleration

[Correction] The three class items prove different things: (1) redshift shows the universe is expanding; (2) Type Ia supernovae show the expansion is accelerating - the direct evidence for dark energy; (3) the CMB is the afterglow of the hot early universe - evidence for the Big Bang, and its precise pattern, combined with other data, fixes the dark-energy share. Note the UPSC 2012 key counts microwaves and redshift, but not supernova explosions as such, as evidence for expansion (PYQ 2012).

1. Redshift (Doppler effect)

  • When a light source moves away, its wavelengths are stretched and spectral lines shift toward the red (longer-wavelength) end; approaching sources show blueshift.
  • Edwin Hubble (1929): more distant galaxies have larger redshifts - they recede faster (v = H0 × d, where v = recession speed, d = distance, H0 = Hubble constant). The IAU in Oct 2018 recommended calling it the Hubble-Lemaitre law (IAU).
  • [Additional] Strictly, cosmological redshift comes from space itself stretching the light while it travels; the Doppler picture is a useful approximation †.

2. Type Ia supernova - the "standard candle"

  • A Sun-like star ends as a white dwarf - a hot, Earth-sized carbon-oxygen core. Alone, it cools slowly toward a black dwarf.
  • In a close binary, the white dwarf pulls matter from a companion (or merges with another white dwarf) †. Near the Chandrasekhar limit (about 1.4 solar masses) it cannot support itself; runaway carbon fusion destroys it in a Type Ia supernova.
  • Because they explode at nearly the same mass, Type Ia supernovae reach a nearly uniform, calibratable peak luminosity - so they are standard candles. Comparing their known intrinsic brightness (luminosity) with the apparent brightness seen from Earth gives the distance, because apparent brightness falls with the square of distance (b = L / 4πd2; b = apparent brightness, L = luminosity, d = distance).
  • Cepheid variables are the other standard candle: pulsating stars whose period of brightening and dimming reveals their luminosity (period-luminosity law, Henrietta Swan Leavitt, 1912) (NASA; PYQ 2023).
  • Chandrasekhar: Indian-born astrophysicist, Nobel Prize in Physics 1983 (shared with William Fowler) for studies of stellar structure and evolution (NobelPrize.org).

[Correction] "If the white dwarf loses its energy it becomes a black dwarf": correct in theory, but black dwarfs are hypothetical - cooling takes far longer than the present age of the universe (about 13.8 billion years), so none exists yet †.

[Correction] "Chandrasekhar limit 1.41 times the Sun": the standard value is about 1.4 solar masses (often quoted as 1.44 †) (NobelPrize.org). It is the maximum mass of a white dwarf, not a property of the Sun.

[Correction] "In both (Type Ia and Cepheids) there is a peak level of luminosity": only Type Ia supernovae are used through their (calibrated) peak luminosity; Cepheids are used through the period-luminosity relation. Type Ia is evidence for acceleration because distant ones looked fainter than expected - the gap between intrinsic and apparent brightness showed they were farther away than a decelerating universe would put them.

3. Cosmic microwave background (CMB)

  • Faint microwave radiation filling all space - the cooled afterglow of the Big Bang, about 2.725 K today. Discovered by Arno Penzias and Robert Wilson (1965); Nobel 1978 †.
  • Satellites (COBE, WMAP, Planck) mapped its tiny ripples; the ripple pattern gives the universe's composition (Figure 61) †.

62. Higgs Boson - the "God Particle"

Builds on the 7 Nov note (LHC and Higgs, Section 56.2) and the Standard Model chart in the previous note, Figure 55.

62.1 Role: explaining the origin of mass

  • The Higgs field is an energy field that fills all space. A split second after the Big Bang it took a non-zero value everywhere ("switched on" in electroweak symmetry breaking †). Before that, the elementary particles were massless and moved at the speed of light.
  • Particles that interact (couple) with the field are slowed and acquire mass; the stronger the coupling, the heavier the particle (class: "intrinsic coupling constant"). The photon and gluon do not couple and stay massless (Figure 68).
  • The Higgs boson is the smallest possible excitation (a "ripple") of the Higgs field. Finding it proved the field exists (CERN).
  • Name: after Peter Higgs; "boson" honours Satyendra Nath Bose †. The nickname "God particle" comes from Leon Lederman's 1993 popular book; physicists generally dislike it †.

[Correction] "Higgs boson mediated the conversion of energy into mass": it is the Higgs field - not the boson - that gives mass, and it does so through interaction, not by converting energy into mass. It gives mass to the elementary particles (W and Z bosons, quarks, charged leptons). Most of the mass of a proton or neutron (about 99%) - and therefore of everyday matter - comes from the energy of the strong force binding quarks and gluons, not from the Higgs †. Whether neutrino masses come from the Higgs is unknown †.

62.3 Why Higgs research continues

  • How the Higgs gets its own mass (class point 1): the Higgs interacts with itself (self-coupling). Measuring this tests the shape of the Higgs field's energy; LHC Run 3 began exploring it with rare decays (CERN Courier, Jul 2026).
  • Will the universe stay as it is? (class point 2): with the measured Higgs and top-quark masses, our vacuum may be metastable - in theory it could one day settle into a more stable lower-energy state, on time-scales vastly longer than the age of the universe † [Verification Required: a hypothesis based on Standard Model extrapolation].
  • Beyond the Standard Model [Additional]: links to dark matter (Section 60) and the matter-antimatter imbalance (Section 63.2).
  • Next machine [Additional]: the LHC ended Run 3 on 27 June 2026 and entered Long Shutdown 3 for the upgrade to the High-Luminosity LHC (HL-LHC), due in June 2030 (CERN Courier; CERN schedule).
  • Indian link [Additional]: India has been an associate member of CERN since 2017, and Indian institutions take part in the CMS and ALICE experiments † (7 Nov note).

Exam point: UPSC 2013 asked the importance of the Higgs discovery - "understand why elementary particles have mass" is the only correct statement; teleportation and better fission fuels are distractors (PYQ 2013).

63. Antimatter

63.1 What antimatter is

  • Antimatter is made of antiparticles. An antiparticle has the same mass as its particle but opposite charge and other opposite quantum numbers. Electron (-1) ↔ positron (+1); proton (+1) ↔ antiproton (-1).
  • For the chargeless neutron, the antineutron has the same mass but opposite baryon number (and opposite magnetic moment), because it is built of antiquarks (class point) (c84).
  • When a particle meets its antiparticle they annihilate, turning their mass into energy (E = mc2; E = energy, m = total mass annihilated, c = speed of light).
  • History [Additional]: Paul Dirac predicted antimatter (1928); Carl Anderson found the positron (1932); antiproton at the Bevatron, Berkeley (1 Nov 1955); antineutron (3 Oct 1956) (CERN timeline).

[Correction] Definition: "same mass but opposite charge" is the textbook rule; the general statement is that all charge-like quantum numbers are reversed (electric charge, baryon number, lepton number). That is why a chargeless neutron still has a distinct antiparticle - opposite baryon (not "barionic") number. Note a photon is its own antiparticle †.

63.2 The puzzle: where did the antimatter go?

  • The Big Bang should have made matter and antimatter in equal amounts, which would have annihilated completely - yet the universe is made of matter. This matter-antimatter asymmetry is a key question at the LHC (class point).
  • [Additional] Andrei Sakharov (1967) listed the conditions needed; one is CP violation - a difference in how matter and antimatter behave. CP violation was first seen in 1964 in strange-meson (kaon) decays (Cronin and Fitch) and in baryon decays for the first time by LHCb (Nature, 16 Jul 2025). The amount observed so far is too small to explain the imbalance (Nature: LHCb 2025).

63.5 Antimatter in nature

  • Natural antimatter is rare (class point). Examples: positrons from radioactive (beta-plus) decay and in cosmic rays (measured by AMS-02 on the ISS †); a belt of antiprotons trapped in Earth's magnetic field (reported by the PAMELA satellite, 2011 †) (c89).
  • [Additional] NASA's Fermi telescope saw thunderstorms hurling beams of antimatter into space - detected by the 511 keV gamma-ray signature of electron-positron annihilation (NASA, 10 Jan 2011) (NASA).
  • No antimatter stars or galaxies have been found †.

[Correction] "Isolated instances of positrons and a layer of antiprotons trapped in Earth's magnetic field": correct in substance (PAMELA, 2011 †); add the thunderstorm positrons (Fermi, 2011) and cosmic-ray positrons. "Postrons" is positrons.

Key Facts for Prelims

  • Universe: dark energy ~68%, dark matter ~27%, ordinary matter ~5%; dark matter ~85% of all matter (NASA, Planck data).
  • Dark matter: no electromagnetic interaction; felt only through gravity so far. Proposed by Zwicky (1933, Coma Cluster); rotation curves by Vera Rubin (1970s); Bullet Cluster (2006) lensing evidence.
  • Neutrinos = hot (near-light speed); favoured dark matter = cold (slow). Neutrino mass < 0.45 eV.
  • MACHOs = dark ordinary objects in galactic halos (black holes, neutron stars, brown dwarfs), searched by microlensing. WIMPs = hypothetical heavy particles. Also axions, primordial black holes.
  • Projects: LZ (USA, SURF), XENONnT (Gran Sasso, Italy), XLZD (merger incl. DARWIN, proposed), DAMPE (China, space, 2015), Euclid (ESA, 1 Jul 2023, L2), InDEx (India, Jaduguda, Jharkhand).
  • Accelerating expansion: 1998, Type Ia supernovae; Nobel 2011 Perlmutter, Schmidt, Riess. Cosmological constant Λ - Einstein 1917.
  • Expansion evidence: redshift (Hubble 1929; Hubble-Lemaitre law, IAU 2018) and CMB (2.725 K, Penzias-Wilson 1965 †).
  • Chandrasekhar limit ~1.4 solar masses = maximum white-dwarf mass; Nobel 1983. Standard candles: Type Ia supernovae and Cepheids (period-luminosity, Leavitt 1912). Black dwarfs: hypothetical.
  • Higgs boson: ~125 GeV/c2 (PDG 125.20), charge 0, spin 0 (scalar), ~10-22 s; ATLAS + CMS, 4 Jul 2012; Nobel 2013 Englert and Higgs. Field gives mass to elementary particles; photon and gluon stay massless.
  • Antimatter: Dirac 1928; positron 1932 (Anderson); antiproton 1955; antineutron 1956. Antihydrogen 1995 (CERN); antihelium-4 (STAR 2011 †); antihyperhydrogen-4 (STAR 2024); antihyperhelium-4 evidence (ALICE 2024-25). Hyperon = baryon with a strange quark.
  • Annihilation converts 100% of mass to energy (fusion ~0.4%, fission ~0.1%). Natural antimatter: cosmic-ray positrons, PAMELA antiproton belt, thunderstorm positrons (Fermi 2011).

Mains Points

  • Why "dark sector" research matters: 95% of the universe's content is unexplained; resolving it tests general relativity and the Standard Model. It is basic research - payoffs are knowledge, instruments (ultra-low-background detectors, cryogenics, sensors, data science) and trained people, not near-term products.
  • Science method in action: dark matter and dark energy are inferred, not seen; claims need independent evidence (the Bullet Cluster, the 1998 two-team agreement) and high statistical thresholds - LZ's 2.6-sigma event and DESI's 2.7-3.2 sigma hints show why "hint" is not "discovery".
  • India's position: a strong intellectual legacy (S. N. Bose, Chandrasekhar), CERN associate membership (2017) and roles in CMS and ALICE †; but domestic underground physics is small (InDEx at JUSL) and INO is stalled (previous note). A national underground laboratory would serve dark-matter, neutrino and low-background research together.
  • Big-science collaboration: experiments now merge across nations (XLZD; Euclid with NASA); India gains from joining early, contributing hardware and hosting facilities, and from open data releases such as Euclid's.
  • Antimatter - realistic view: proven uses are medical (PET) and fundamental tests; propulsion and power are far from feasible because production costs far exceed the energy returned (NASA 1999 estimate US$ 62.5 trillion per gram).
  • Way forward: sustain long-term ANRF/DST and DAE funding for basic physics; build shared underground and computing facilities; deepen CERN, Euclid-type and next-generation (HL-LHC, XLZD) participation with Indian industry as supplier; and invest in science communication so that projects like INO do not stall on public mistrust.

Common Traps / Confusions

  • Dark matter = invisible, pulls (gravity), holds galaxies; dark energy = unknown, pushes, accelerates expansion. Do not swap their shares (27% versus 68%).
  • Dark matter was not proposed by Einstein; Einstein's link is the cosmological constant (dark energy).
  • Dark matter is ~27% of the universe but ~85% of matter.
  • MACHOs are ordinary matter candidates; WIMPs are new-particle candidates. Pulsar = rotating neutron star, not "neuron star".
  • Euclid and DAMPE are space missions; LZ, XENONnT and InDEx are underground detectors.
  • Redshift proves expansion; Type Ia supernovae prove acceleration; CMB supports the Big Bang.
  • Cepheids: period-luminosity, not "peak"; nebulae are gas clouds (PYQ 2023).
  • The Higgs field gives mass; the Higgs boson is its ripple. Most of the proton's mass comes from the strong force, not the Higgs. The Higgs is not a force carrier.
  • Nobel 2013 = Englert and Higgs (Brout died 2011); Nobel 2011 = accelerating universe; Nobel 1983 = Chandrasekhar.
  • Antineutron is chargeless like the neutron but differs in baryon number. Hyperon = baryon with a strange quark.
  • Antimatter "fuel" is an energy carrier at best, not a source.
