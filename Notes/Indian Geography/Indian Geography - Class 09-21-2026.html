2. Teesta: Course, GLOF and Water Sharing

2.1 Source and Direction

  • Headwaters: the high, glaciated northern Sikkim Himalaya. Sikkim's official geography page names Cho Lhamu / Tso Lhamo Lake; NRSC describes the Teesta Khangtse/Kangse glacier headwater region.
  • Glacial tributaries: Zemu glacier contributes through Zemu Chu to the upper Teesta system; the Lachen and Lachung streams meet at Chungthang.
  • Course: generally south through Sikkim, into northern West Bengal near Sevoke, then southeast towards Bangladesh and the Brahmaputra/Jamuna.
  • [Additional] Rangit / Rangeet, rising in western Sikkim, is an important Teesta tributary. This distinction is tested in the 2017 PYQ.
[Correction] "Westward river" should be a western tributary of the Brahmaputra basin, not a river flowing predominantly west. "Sikkim → Chicken's Neck → West Bengal" also treats a region within West Bengal as a separate state: locate the river near the Siliguri corridor and Sevoke foothills of northern West Bengal. "Siluguri" is Siliguri.
[Correction] "Khangse glacier and Zemu glacier" should not be memorised as two equivalent, directly joined sources. Distinguish the headwater lake/glacier description from Zemu Chu's tributary contribution. Official sources describe different levels of this headwater network; do not invent a single exact source coordinate. Sikkim geography; NRSC headwater study.

2.2 The 1787 Course Change

Historically, Teesta drainage reached the Ganga/Padma system. Around 1787, a major course change redirected it towards the Brahmaputra/Jamuna. Britannica's Teesta account supports this historical change.

[Correction] Retain the rough note's river-reorganisation idea, but do not treat the 1787 event as automatically proving classic headward-erosion river capture. Avulsion is a rapid shift to a new channel; river capture/piracy is diversion of drainage from one river system to another. Teesta is useful for discussing drainage diversion, but the precise mechanism needs geomorphological evidence.

2.3 Hydropower and the 2023 Disaster

The Teesta basin contains a series of hydropower schemes with numbered stages. The Teesta III dam at Chungthang was destroyed during the flood following the South Lhonak Lake outburst on the night of 3-4 October 2023.

[Correction] "Teesta 1 to 5" is not evidence that five dams were all completed or operating on the class date. These are separate project stages with different sites and histories, and the basin's project numbering also extends beyond V. [Verification Required] Check each stage's dated operator/CEA status before listing it as commissioned; Teesta III's pre-disaster operation is not its post-disaster status.
[Correction] "South Lohnak" is South Lhonak; GLOF expands to glacial lake outburst flood. The lake outburst and downstream dam failure are linked but distinct events. Do not describe the disaster simply as an ordinary release from a functioning dam. PIB, 30 July 2025.

2.4 India-Bangladesh Water Sharing

Teesta sharing concerns seasonal water availability, irrigation, downstream needs and cooperation between India and Bangladesh. West Bengal's interests are important within India's decision-making.

[Additional] Separate water allocation from hydrological information sharing. Rainfall, river-level, discharge and reservoir-operation information support flood warnings but do not by themselves create an agreed dry-season water allocation.

[Verification Required] The rough notes do not identify a signed agreement or negotiation date. Do not infer the existence or terms of a final Teesta-sharing treaty on 21 September 2026 from a proposal or an older political statement.

3.1 Environmental and Social Issues

[Additional] The following is a risk-assessment framework, not a claim that all listed impacts have already occurred.

  • Seismic and slope risk: earthquake-prone, steep terrain requires assessment of landslides, slope stability, construction spoil and cascading infrastructure failure.
  • Flow alteration: reservoir filling and operations can change seasonal flow and short-term fluctuations; assess downstream ecological flows and warning lead times.
  • Sediment continuity: trapping sediment can affect channel form, floodplain renewal, aquatic habitats and downstream delta processes.
  • Biodiversity and livelihoods: roads, tunnels, reservoirs and transmission lines may fragment habitats and affect fishing, farming, settlements and culturally important landscapes.
  • Cumulative impacts: assess the whole cascade together with other basin projects, not each structure in isolation.

3.2 Strategic and Diplomatic Issues

  • Upstream-downstream information asymmetry: India and Bangladesh need timely information about flows, construction and operations.
  • Trust and consultation: unilateral decisions in a transboundary basin can intensify uncertainty and diplomatic tensions.
  • Disaster preparedness: agreed warning protocols and joint technical exchanges can reduce downstream risk.
  • Avoid exaggerated certainty: upstream regulation can matter, but claims that one project can simply "stop all Brahmaputra water" ignore major monsoon and tributary inputs below Tibet. A precise effect needs seasonal water-balance and operating data.
[Correction] The note about a "hydrological data sharing proposal from India and Bangladesh" does not establish a three-country agreement. MEA's 7 August 2025 reply records India's request for resumption of Chinese hydrological-data provision and describes the India-China Expert Level Mechanism, established in 2006. India-Bangladesh cooperation is a separate bilateral channel. [Verification Required] Any specific joint proposal, renewal or later resumption requires its own dated confirmation.

4. Barak-Meghna and the Bengal Delta

Barak rises in the hills of Manipur, follows a course involving the Manipur-Mizoram region, and enters the Barak Valley of Assam. Near the Bangladesh frontier it divides into the Surma and Kushiyara; their downstream network contributes to the Meghna.

[Correction] "Manipur → Nagaland → Assam" is not a reliable simple mainstem itinerary. Nagaland contributes to the wider basin, while the route summary must include the Manipur-Mizoram reaches and Assam. Likewise, Barak does not merely change name to Meghna at the border: retain the Surma-Kushiyara branching and downstream network. Assam basin description; Barak course reference.
[Correction] The world's largest delta is the Ganga-Brahmaputra-Meghna (Bengal) delta. The Sundarbans is the tidal mangrove region within its lower part, not an exact synonym for the entire delta. The note's Barak connection is retained through the Meghna system. UNESCO's delta description.

6.2 Bhitarkanika, Gahirmatha and Olive Ridley Turtles

Bhitarkanika is a mangrove-wetland landscape in coastal Odisha's Brahmani-Baitarani deltaic region. The adjoining Gahirmatha coast is an important olive ridley turtle mass-nesting area. The Rushikulya mouth is another important nesting location farther south in Odisha.

[Correction] "BetharKanyika" is Bhitarkanika and "olive ridely" is olive ridley. Distinguish the mangrove/estuarine habitat from the turtles' sandy marine nesting beaches, especially Gahirmatha; they are associated landscapes, not interchangeable labels. Odisha Tourism.

[Additional] Mass nesting is called arribada. Conservation priorities include reducing fishing-net bycatch, protecting nesting beaches, managing artificial lighting and maintaining suitable coastal habitat. Nesting totals vary by season; no undated population figure is used here.

9. Kolleru Lake and the Godavari-Krishna Link

9.1 Kolleru: Location and Ecological Function

Kolleru is a natural, shallow, predominantly freshwater lake between the Krishna and Godavari deltas in Andhra Pradesh. It acts as a flood-balancing wetland and supports fisheries, agriculture and waterbirds.

[Additional] Ramsar designation: 19 August 2002. The Ramsar description calls it naturally eutrophic and notes monsoon flooding and seasonal drying; natural nutrient richness is not the same as unchecked pollution. Upputeru is its outlet towards the Bay of Bengal. The Ramsar entry's ecological account is based on 2002 information, not a 2026 water-quality survey. Ramsar site record.

9.3 Polavaram Canal, Pattiseema and Rayalaseema

The Polavaram Right Main Canal provides the route for transferring Godavari water towards the Krishna upstream of Prakasam Barrage, using the Budameru Diversion Channel. The project provision cited by PIB is 80 TMC (thousand million cubic feet).

[Additional] Pattiseema is a separate lift scheme that pumps Godavari water into this canal. PIB, 4 January 2018, states that the scheme was completed and water had been drawn through the canal from 2015-16 onwards. That is historical evidence of this link, not proof that the entire Polavaram dam project was complete.

[Correction] Do not equate Polavaram dam, Polavaram Right Main Canal and Pattiseema lift scheme. Also, Godavari water entering the Krishna delta is not a direct canal delivery to every part of Rayalaseema. The proposed water-security logic is that supplying some delta demand may free Krishna-system water for other needs, subject to allocations, available water, storage and separate conveyance schemes. PIB technical explanation, 2018.

[Additional] River linking alone cannot guarantee an end to Rayalaseema drought. Assess reliable donor-basin water, dry-year demand, pumping costs, distribution, displacement, environmental flows, groundwater recharge and efficient cropping.

12. Cauvery / Kaveri River System

12.1 Source, Course and Monsoon Dependence

  • Source: Talakaveri in the Brahmagiri hills, Kodagu (Coorg), Karnataka, in the Western Ghats.
  • Main course: Karnataka → Tamil Nadu → Bay of Bengal; important downstream areas include Tiruchirappalli (Trichy) and the Thanjavur delta.
  • [Additional] About 800 km in NCERT/CWC. Mainstem course is distinct from the broader basin and water-sharing interests, which also involve Kerala and Puducherry.
  • [Additional] The upper catchment benefits strongly from the southwest monsoon; the lower Tamil Nadu basin also receives the northeast monsoon. This helps sustain flow across seasons but does not remove drought variability.

[Correction] Standardise Talakaveri, Kodagu/Coorg, Tiruchirappalli and Thanjavur. "Karnataka-Tamil Nadu" is a useful mainstem summary, not the complete list of basin-sharing parties. CWC; Cauvery institutions.

Common Traps / Confusions

  • Western tributary is not west-flowing river: Teesta generally flows south/southeast.
  • Basin state is not mainstem state; project beneficiary is not a river-course state.
  • Pranahita is not Purna + all neighbouring rivers; retain the actual hierarchy.
  • Chitrakote is not Chitrakoot; Bhima's Chandrabhaga is not Chenab's Chandra-Bhaga.
  • Kolleru is freshwater-dominated; Sundarbans is a tidal mangrove landscape; Gahirmatha is a marine nesting coast.
  • Kallanai is an anicut; Shivanasamudra is a hydropower station; Mettur is a storage dam. They are not interchangeable project types.
  • Normal-year allocation is not a drought-year guarantee; MW is not annual generation; announced capacity is not commissioned capacity.
  • "Largest" needs a metric; "oldest" needs a structure category; river length needs a measurement convention.
  • A DPR, an interstate agreement and an inaugurated component are not proof that a whole project is complete.

  4. 1, 2 and 3
