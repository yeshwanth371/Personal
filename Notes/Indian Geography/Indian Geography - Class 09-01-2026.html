1. Course Framework: Reading Indian Geography

Geography comes from the Greek ge (Earth) and graphia (description or writing). Indian geography is best understood as a spatial discipline: learn facts through location, distribution, relationship and regional variation.

2. Locational Aspects of India

A precise location is expressed through latitude and longitude. Latitude helps explain solar angle, day length and broad climatic patterns; longitude helps identify east-west position and calculate local time.

2.1 Latitude: North-South Position

Latitude is the angular distance of a place north or south of the Equator, measured as an angle at the Earth's centre. The Equator is 0 degrees latitude. One degree is divided into 60 minutes, and one minute into 60 seconds.

ReferenceCoordinateMeaning
Northern limit of mainland India37 degrees 6 minutes NIn the Ladakh sector; use the coordinate rather than attaching it casually to a town.
Southern limit of mainland India8 degrees 4 minutes NNear Kanyakumari, Tamil Nadu. Cape Comorin is the historical English name for Kanyakumari.
Southernmost point of Indian territory6 degrees 45 minutes NIndira Point on Great Nicobar Island, Andaman and Nicobar Islands.
Tropic of Cancer23 degrees 30 minutes NCrosses India roughly through its middle and separates the tropical south from the subtropical north in the strict latitudinal sense.
[Correction] India's mainland latitudinal extent is 8 degrees 4 minutes N to 37 degrees 6 minutes N. Including islands, Indian territory extends south to 6 degrees 45 minutes N. The north-south distance of the mainland is about 3,214 km; it should not be called the latitudinal extent "in continuity." One degree of latitude averages about 111 km, so degree conversion gives only an approximation.
[Correction] Indira Point is not presently submerged as a whole. The 26 December 2004 earthquake caused land subsidence and inundated parts of the coast; the lighthouse area was affected, but Indira Point remains India's southernmost territorial point.

2.2 Why 23 degrees 30 minutes N Matters

  • The Earth's rotational axis is tilted about 23.5 degrees relative to the perpendicular to its orbital plane.
  • Near 21 June, the subsolar point reaches the Tropic of Cancer: the Sun can be overhead at local noon there.
  • Near 22-23 September, the subsolar point crosses the Equator southward.
  • Near 21-22 December, it reaches the Tropic of Capricorn (23 degrees 30 minutes S).
  • The Tropic of Cancer passes through eight states: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura and Mizoram.
[Correction] The December solstice is generally dated 21 or 22 December, not fixed at 23 December. Equinox and solstice dates can vary slightly by year and time zone.
[Correction] Southern India does not receive vertical rays throughout the year, nor does all northern India always receive only slanting rays. The overhead Sun is possible only within the tropics and shifts seasonally between the two tropics.
[Correction] "Latitude is inversely proportional to temperature" (L ~ 1/T) is not a physical law. Temperature generally decreases poleward because the average solar angle declines, but altitude, season, cloud, winds, ocean currents, distance from sea and relief can override the trend.
[Additional] India's tropical-subtropical position helps produce strong monsoon seasonality. However, the Himalayas and altitude make northern climatic conditions much more complex than latitude alone suggests.

2.3 Longitude: East-West Position and Time

Longitude is the angular distance east or west of the Prime Meridian (0 degrees), which passes through Greenwich. The Prime Meridian divides the globe into Eastern and Western Hemispheres. India lies entirely in the Eastern Hemisphere.

ReferenceCoordinateExam use
Western extent68 degrees 7 minutes EGujarat sector; Guhar Moti is commonly used as the nearby mapped locality.
Eastern extent97 degrees 25 minutes EArunachal Pradesh sector; do not treat Kibithu and Dong as interchangeable labels without consulting an authoritative large-scale map.
Standard Meridian82 degrees 30 minutes EBasis of Indian Standard Time; commonly referenced near Mirzapur, Uttar Pradesh.
[Verification Required] Small-scale textbooks securely establish the extreme coordinates, but locality labels such as Kibithu/Kibithoo, Dong and Guhar Moti vary by whether the question asks for a settlement, inhabited village or precise territorial extremity. Use the latest Survey of India sheet for point-level claims.
[Correction] Although India's latitude and longitude spans are both about 29 degrees, they do not represent equal ground distances. One degree of longitude equals about 111 x cos(latitude) km and therefore becomes shorter with latitude. India's east-west distance is about 2,933 km, while its north-south distance is about 3,214 km.
[Correction] 82 degrees 30 minutes E is the chosen Standard Meridian, not the exact arithmetic midpoint of 68 degrees 7 minutes E and 97 degrees 25 minutes E (about 82 degrees 46 minutes E). It lies close to the country's longitudinal centre and gives an offset of 5 hours 30 minutes from UTC.

2.4 From Longitude to Indian Standard Time

Earth rotates 360 degrees→24 hours→15 degrees per hour→1 degree per 4 minutes

India's longitude span is 29 degrees 18 minutes. Its theoretical local solar-time difference is about 117 minutes, commonly rounded to two hours. Sunrise therefore occurs much earlier in far-eastern India than in far-western India, while clocks show the same IST.

IST = UTC + 5 hours 30 minutes. The Standard Meridian passes through Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Odisha and Andhra Pradesh. CSIR-National Physical Laboratory maintains and disseminates Indian Standard Time.

[Correction] Use UTC for the modern civil-time reference. GMT is still commonly stated, but it is not identical in technical definition. The offset is written UTC+05:30, not "+5.30 minutes." India adopted a single standard time based on 82 degrees 30 minutes E in 1906; post-Independence IST continued this basis.

2.5 Single Time Zone: Relevance and Debate

Advantages of one ISTChallenges, especially in the Northeast
Uniform administration and national schedulingClock time is poorly aligned with early sunrise and sunset
Simpler railway, aviation and logistics timetablesUseful morning daylight may be lost under later work/school schedules
Common financial-market and communication hoursArtificial lighting may increase evening electricity demand
No internal time boundary or conversion errorsPotential effects on sleep, circadian rhythm, safety and winter tourism
Easy domestic trade and interstate coordinationA two-zone system would itself create boundary, transport and coordination costs
[Additional] Assam tea gardens have historically used an informal "chaibagaan time", generally one hour ahead of IST (equivalent to UTC+06:30), to align work with daylight. It is not an official civil time zone.
[Correction] Do not attribute a single settled proposal to "NIAS recommendation - GMT+6" without a document and year. Research proposals differ: some favour advancing work hours, while a CSIR-NPL study proposed IST-I (UTC+05:30) and IST-II (UTC+06:30). India officially retains one time zone as of 2026.
Critical analysis: A second zone could improve daylight use and social timing in the Northeast, but gains depend on behaviour and regional load patterns. Before changing civil time, India can test staggered school, office and market hours. Any two-zone decision must solve the boundary location, railway/aviation safety, digital systems and public communication.

3. Physiographic Regions of India

India has highly varied relief: young fold mountains, extensive alluvial plains, an ancient plateau, arid terrain, coastal plains and island groups. Physiography influences agriculture, drainage, groundwater, minerals, biodiversity, settlement, transport, hazards and regional planning.

[Correction] A five-part scheme is useful for these class notes, but NCERT commonly presents six physiographic divisions: (1) Northern and Northeastern Mountains, (2) Northern Plain, (3) Peninsular Plateau, (4) Indian Desert, (5) Coastal Plains and (6) Islands. Combining Coastal Plains and Islands produces the five-part classroom scheme.
RegionBasic characterHuman-geography link
1. Northern and Northeastern MountainsYoung fold mountains; high relief; glaciers and major river headwatersHydropower, horticulture, tourism, difficult transport and high hazard sensitivity
2. Great/Northern PlainsDeep alluvium deposited mainly by Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra systemsIntensive agriculture, dense population, groundwater use, floods and major cities
3. Peninsular PlateauAncient, stable crystalline block with plateaus, uplands and rift valleysMineral concentration, industries, rain-shadow contrasts and hard-rock groundwater limits
4. Coastal Plains and IslandsNarrower west coast, broader depositional east coast; continental and oceanic islandsPorts, fisheries, deltas, tourism, cyclones, coastal erosion and strategic value
5. Indian/Thar DesertArid terrain west of the Aravallis; dunes, ephemeral drainage and saline depressionsPastoralism, irrigation, wind/solar energy, water scarcity and desertification risk

4. Regional Planning Lens

Himalayas

Prioritise slope stability, seismic safety, spring-shed management, carrying capacity and low-impact connectivity.

Plains

Coordinate floodplain zoning, groundwater recharge, crop diversification, air quality and urban expansion.

Plateau and Desert

Match mining and industry with water budgets, land restoration, drought resilience and pastoral mobility.

Coasts and Islands

Integrate cyclone resilience, coastal regulation, mangroves/coral ecosystems, freshwater security and strategic needs.

Mains link: India's physiographic diversity makes place-based planning essential. The same infrastructure standard, cropping package or settlement model cannot be transferred unchanged across ecologically and geomorphologically different regions.

Key Facts for Prelims

  • Mainland latitude: 8 degrees 4 minutes N to 37 degrees 6 minutes N.
  • Territorial south: Indira Point, 6 degrees 45 minutes N.
  • Longitude: 68 degrees 7 minutes E to 97 degrees 25 minutes E.
  • North-south distance: about 3,214 km.
  • East-west distance: about 2,933 km.
  • Tropic of Cancer: 23 degrees 30 minutes N; crosses eight states.
  • Standard Meridian: 82 degrees 30 minutes E.
  • IST: UTC+05:30; one official time zone.
  • Local solar-time gap across India: about 117 minutes.
  • NCERT commonly uses six physiographic divisions.

Mains Points

  • Location-climate link: tropical-subtropical span, seasonal solar migration, Himalayan barrier and oceanic setting together shape the monsoon.
  • Location-strategy link: India's central position in the northern Indian Ocean connects West Asia, Africa, Southeast Asia and major sea lanes.
  • Time-zone debate: balance daylight efficiency and circadian alignment against administrative, transport and digital coordination costs.
  • Physiography-development link: relief controls drainage, soil, resources, accessibility, hazards and settlement density.
  • Planning argument: adopt region-specific norms based on ecological capacity and hazard exposure rather than uniform territorial schemes.

TrapCorrect position

Indira Point disappeared in 2004.Parts subsided and were inundated; it remains the southernmost point.
One degree always equals 111 km.Approximately true for latitude; longitude distance varies with latitude.

30-Second Revision

India lies in the Northern and Eastern Hemispheres. Mainland: 8 degrees 4 minutes N-37 degrees 6 minutes N and 68 degrees 7 minutes E-97 degrees 25 minutes E. The Tropic of Cancer divides tropical south from subtropical north. IST uses 82 degrees 30 minutes E and equals UTC+05:30; local solar time differs by nearly two hours across the country. Relief is organised into six standard NCERT divisions: mountains, plain, plateau, desert, coastal plains and islands. These regional contrasts demand region-specific planning.

Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

UPSC CSE Prelims 2015 - General Studies Paper I

Which one of the following pairs of States of India indicates the easternmost and westernmost State?

  1. Assam and Rajasthan
  2. Arunachal Pradesh and Rajasthan
  3. Assam and Gujarat
  4. Arunachal Pradesh and Gujarat

Correct answer: (d) Arunachal Pradesh and Gujarat. Arunachal Pradesh contains India's eastern territorial extent, while Gujarat contains its western extent.

UPSC CSE Mains 2021 - General Studies Paper I

Differentiate the causes of landslides in the Himalayan region and Western Ghats.

Answer approach: Contrast the young, tectonically active, highly fractured and steep Himalayas (earthquakes, river undercutting, intense rain/snow processes and road cutting) with the older but deeply weathered Western Ghats (intense orographic rain, saturated regolith, escarpment slopes, quarrying and land-use change). Connect both to physiography and human disturbance.

UPSC CSE Mains 2016 - General Studies Paper I

"The Himalayas are highly prone to landslides." Discuss the causes and suggest suitable measures of mitigation.

Answer approach: Causes include active tectonics, weak/folded rocks, steep slopes, seismicity, intense rainfall, glacial and river erosion, deforestation, blasting and unplanned construction. Mitigation includes susceptibility zonation, slope drainage, bio-engineering, retaining structures, regulated construction/quarrying, monitoring, early warning and community preparedness.
