1. Himalayan Drainage: Three Great Systems

The Himalayan drainage is commonly organised into the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra systems. These are transboundary, largely perennial systems supplied by monsoon rain, snowmelt, glacier melt and groundwater.

SystemBroad direction and outletCountries in the wider basin
IndusTibet and Ladakh, then mainly south through Pakistan to the Arabian Sea.China, India, Pakistan and Afghanistan through the Kabul sub-basin.
GangaUttarakhand to the northern plains, then east into Bangladesh and the Bay of Bengal.India, Nepal, China and Bangladesh.
BrahmaputraTibet to Arunachal Pradesh and Assam, then Bangladesh and the Bay of Bengal through the Meghna estuary.China, India, Bhutan and Bangladesh.
[Additional] Riparian position: an upper-riparian state lies upstream and can affect timing or quantity of flow; a lower-riparian state lies downstream and depends on water arriving from above. The description can change between tributaries: Afghanistan is upstream of Pakistan on the Kabul River, for example.

2. Indus River: Source, Course and Basin

The Indus (Sindhu) rises in south-western Tibet near the Lake Manasarovar-Kailash region. NCERT describes its source near the Bokhar Chu glacier at about 4,164 m. It flows north-west through Tibet, enters Ladakh, passes the Leh region and receives the Zanskar and Shyok systems. Farther west it bends around the Nanga Parbat massif through deep gorges, crosses Pakistan and builds a distributary delta south-east of Karachi before reaching the Arabian Sea.

Tibetan Plateau→Ladakh→Gilgit-Baltistan region→Pakistan Punjab and Sindh→Indus delta→Arabian Sea

LengthAbout 3,180-3,200 km; published estimates differ with source definition and measurement method.
River countriesThe main stem flows through China, India and Pakistan.
Basin countriesThe wider basin also includes Afghanistan through the Kabul River system.
ReliefDeep antecedent gorges occur around the Nanga Parbat massif; the river does not simply cut through the summit.

[Correction] “Bokhar Chu glaciers” is better written as the Bokhar Chu glacier/source area. Sources differ in how they identify the ultimate headstream, so “near the Manasarovar-Kailash region” is safer than saying the river rises directly from Lake Manasarovar.
[Correction] The Indus skirts and deeply incises the Nanga Parbat massif; “cuts Nanga Parbat mountain” is an overstatement. Its sea outlet is in the delta south-east of Karachi, not at Karachi city itself.
[Correction] A fixed “India 20%, Pakistan 80%” basin split and the unexplained “Indus 9.4%” are not retained. Basin shares vary by dataset and definition. NCERT's India-focused figure is that the Indus basin covers about 3.21 lakh sq km in India, roughly 9.8% of India's geographical area.

3. Punjab Rivers and the Panjnad Network

Punjab means the land of five waters/rivers. From west to east the five rivers are Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas and Sutlej.

Modern nameAncient/Sanskrit associationCourse and confluence
JhelumVitastaVerinag spring → Kashmir Valley and Srinagar → Wular Lake → Pakistan → Chenab.
ChenabAsikni / ChandrabhagaChandra and Bhaga meet at Tandi → Himachal Pradesh → Jammu and Kashmir → Pakistan; largest Indus tributary by discharge.
RaviParushniRises west of Rohtang Pass → Himachal Pradesh → along part of the Jammu-Punjab boundary → Pakistan → Chenab.
BeasVipashaBeas Kund near Rohtang Pass → Himachal Pradesh → Punjab → Sutlej at Harike.
SutlejShutudri / ShatadruNear Rakshastal in Tibet → Shipki La sector → Himachal Pradesh → Punjab → Pakistan → Chenab/Panjnad system.
[Correction] “All five rivers are collectively called Panjnad” needs precision. Panjnad is the combined lower channel formed after the five Punjab rivers have merged. The treaty legally deems the Chenab to include the Panjnad; this is not a competing British claim that the final channel is the Sutlej.

3.1 River-Specific Corrections

  • Jhelum: it flows through Srinagar and then Wular Lake. Dal Lake is connected to the Jhelum by channels but the river does not flow through Dal Lake. Karewas are older lacustrine and fluvio-glacial deposits of the Kashmir Valley; their well-drained terraces support saffron near Pampore.
  • Chenab: the Chandra rises near Baralacha La and the Bhaga near Suraj Tal; they meet at Tandi. Bara Shigri Glacier feeds the Chandra system but is not the simple source of both headstreams. Chandratal is associated with the Chandra headwater region.
  • Ravi: it does not run through Jammu and Kashmir as a normal state-length course; it forms part of the boundary between Jammu and Kashmir and Punjab before entering Pakistan.
  • Sutlej: it is the longest of the five Punjab tributaries and enters India through the Shipki La sector.

4. Indus Waters Treaty (IWT), 1960

The treaty was signed at Karachi on 19 September 1960 by Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru and Pakistan President Mohammad Ayub Khan. The International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (World Bank) signed for the limited purposes specified in the treaty.

River groupBasic allocationImportant qualification
Eastern Rivers: Ravi, Beas, SutlejWaters are available for India's unrestricted use, subject to express treaty provisions.Water that India releases after final crossing may be used by Pakistan without creating a continuing right to future releases.
Western Rivers: Indus, Jhelum, ChenabPakistan receives waters that India is obliged to let flow.India retains treaty-defined domestic, non-consumptive and agricultural uses, hydroelectric generation and specified storage rights.
[Correction] India is not limited only to drinking water, minor irrigation and zero-storage, low-height dams on the Western Rivers. Annexures C, D and E permit defined agricultural use, run-of-river hydropower subject to design rules, and limited categories/volumes of storage. Dam height is not the treaty's simple deciding test.

4.1 Cooperation and Dispute Settlement

  1. Question: first examined by the Permanent Indus Commission (PIC), consisting of one commissioner from each country.
  2. Difference: specified technical matters may go to a Neutral Expert.
  3. Dispute: may proceed through inter-government negotiation/mediation and then an ad hoc seven-member Court of Arbitration under Annexure G.
[Correction] The PIC must meet at least once a year, not generally twice by treaty. A treaty Court of Arbitration is ad hoc; it is not the same institution as the Permanent Court of Arbitration, even though the PCA may provide registry support and is based in The Hague. The World Bank's role is limited and procedural, not a general appellate role.

4.3 Status after the 2025 Pahalgam Attack

Time-sensitive position: On 23 April 2025, India announced that the IWT would be held in abeyance with immediate effect until Pakistan credibly and irrevocably abjured support for cross-border terrorism. This followed the 22 April Pahalgam terrorist attack. [Verification Required] for any later formal change to this position after the latest official statement available during preparation.
[Correction] “Blood and water cannot flow together” is a political formulation associated with India's post-Uri position, not a treaty clause. Uri (2016), Pulwama (2019) and Pahalgam (2025) must not be merged into one decision: the formal announcement to hold the treaty in abeyance followed Pahalgam.
[Additional] India's case for treaty review has cited changed population, clean-energy needs, climate variability, glacier and hydrological uncertainty, sedimentation and security conditions. Treaty Article XII says modification or termination requires a duly ratified treaty between both governments; the legal effects of unilateral “abeyance” are therefore contested internationally.

[Correction] “Most populous basin in the world; second Yangtze; 9% of world population” is not retained as a fixed ranking. Basin boundaries and population years differ, and the Ganga is often combined with Brahmaputra-Meghna in global datasets. Use a named dataset and census year before comparing basins.
[Additional] The Ganga basin's dense settlement reflects fertile alluvium, extensive groundwater and surface-water use, monsoon agriculture, navigable reaches, historic cities and religious-cultural importance. These advantages also create severe pollution, floodplain encroachment and water-allocation pressures.

6.1 Headstreams and Main Course

The Bhagirathi emerges at Gaumukh from the Gangotri Glacier. The Alaknanda, hydrologically the longer headstream, receives the Dhauliganga, Nandakini, Pindar and Mandakini along the sequence of Uttarakhand's major prayags. Bhagirathi and Alaknanda meet at Devprayag; downstream the river is called the Ganga.

Gaumukh-Gangotri: Bhagirathi+Alaknanda→Devprayag: Ganga→Rishikesh-Haridwar→northern plains→Farakka and Bengal delta
ReachPlaces to map
UttarakhandGaumukh/Gangotri, Tehri, Devprayag, Rishikesh and Haridwar.
Uttar PradeshKanpur, Prayagraj and Varanasi.
BiharPatna.
JharkhandSahibganj/Rajmahal reach.
West Bengal and deltaFarakka, Bhagirathi-Hooghly, Kolkata, Haldia and Gangasagar; principal Padma channel toward Bangladesh.
[Correction] The Ganga does not simply “divide into Hooghly and Padma at Farakka”. Near the delta head, the Bhagirathi-Hooghly distributary branches from the Ganga; Farakka Barrage diverts water through a feeder canal to the Bhagirathi-Hooghly. The main channel enters Bangladesh as the Padma.
[Correction] Gangotri should not be called India's “second-largest glacier” without a specified inventory and metric. It is one of the largest Himalayan glaciers and the source glacier of the Bhagirathi. Alaknanda is more accurately a headstream than merely a head tributary.
[Additional] Tehri Dam on the Bhagirathi is conventionally listed as India's highest dam (260.5 m). The Ganga's 2,525 km figure refers to the recognised main river course, not a simple straight-line Gaumukh-Gangasagar distance.

Key Facts for Prelims

  • Himalayan drainage has three major systems: Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra.
  • The Indus main stem crosses China, India and Pakistan; Afghanistan lies in the wider basin through the Kabul River.
  • Upper-Indus sequence: Zanskar joins near Nimmu; Nubra and Galwan feed Shyok; Hunza feeds Gilgit.
  • Punjab rivers west-east: Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej.
  • Jhelum-Vitasta; Chenab-Asikni/Chandrabhaga; Ravi-Parushni; Beas-Vipasha; Sutlej-Shutudri/Shatadru.
  • Beas joins Sutlej at Harike; Jhelum and Ravi join Chenab; the Panjnad eventually joins the Indus.
  • IWT Western Rivers: Indus, Jhelum, Chenab. Eastern Rivers: Ravi, Beas, Sutlej.
  • Questions go first to PIC; specified differences to a Neutral Expert; disputes may go to an ad hoc Court of Arbitration.
  • Bhagirathi and Alaknanda meet at Devprayag, downstream of which the river is named Ganga.
  • Ganga basin: about 26.3% of India's area; Ganga length: about 2,525 km.

Mains Points

  • Hydropolitics: upstream infrastructure, downstream dependence, data exchange, climate variability and security make the Indus a coupled water-diplomacy problem.
  • Treaty design: durable river agreements need technical rules, regular institutions, staged dispute settlement and procedures for changed circumstances.
  • Climate adaptation: glacier change, extreme precipitation, sediment and seasonal-flow shifts require joint monitoring rather than reliance on historical averages alone.
  • Eastern-river use: storage, canal efficiency and project completion can improve utilisation, but ecological flows and downstream impacts remain relevant.
  • Ganga governance: pollution control, groundwater management, floodplain zoning, environmental flows and interstate coordination must be handled at basin scale.

30-Second Revision

The three Himalayan systems are Indus-Ganga-Brahmaputra. Indus flows from the Kailash-Manasarovar region through Ladakh and Pakistan to the Arabian Sea; Kabul brings Afghanistan into its wider basin. Remember J-C-R-B-S west-east and the merge pattern: Jhelum and Ravi into Chenab, Beas into Sutlej, Panjnad into Indus. IWT 1960 assigns Western Rivers mainly to Pakistan and Eastern Rivers to India but preserves defined cross-uses; India announced abeyance after Pahalgam in April 2025. The Ganga begins by name where Bhagirathi and Alaknanda meet at Devprayag, runs about 2,525 km, drains about 26.3% of India and continues toward the Padma while the Bhagirathi-Hooghly distributary serves West Bengal.

Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

UPSC CSE Prelims 2021 - General Studies Paper I

With reference to the Indus river system, of the following four rivers, three of them pour into one of them which joins the Indus direct. Among the following, which one is such river that joins the Indus direct?

  1. Chenab
  2. Jhelum
  3. Ravi
  4. Sutlej

Correct answer: (a) Chenab. Jhelum and Ravi join the Chenab, while Beas-fed Sutlej also becomes part of the combined Panjnad/Chenab system before that channel joins the Indus. The treaty definition explicitly deems Chenab to include Panjnad.
