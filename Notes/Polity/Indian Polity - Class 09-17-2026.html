1. Supreme Court on Mercy Powers

[Additional] Constitutional setting: Article 72 gives the President clemency powers in court-martial cases, offences within the Union's executive sphere, and all death-sentence cases. Read it with Article 74: the President acts on the aid and advice of the Union Council of Ministers. Article 161 concerns the Governor's constitutional clemency power.

[Correction] "Maru Ram case 1981" uses the reporting year; the judgment was delivered in 1980. "Keher Singh" is Kehar Singh, decided in 1988 and commonly reported as a 1989 case. "Shatrugan Chauhan" is Shatrughan Chauhan.
[Correction] "Mercy powers are not a right of a petitioner" is too broad. There is no entitlement to a favourable pardon, and Kehar Singh denies a right to insist on an oral hearing. But the right to seek mercy and have the petition lawfully considered is a constitutional safeguard, expressly recognised in Shatrughan Chauhan. An oral hearing is not prohibited; the executive may provide one.

1.2 Epuru Sudhakar: Grounds of Limited Review

  1. Non-application of mind.
  2. Mala fides: exercise in bad faith.
  3. Reliance on extraneous or wholly irrelevant considerations.
  4. Exclusion of relevant material.
  5. Arbitrariness.

The Court checks legality and the decision-making process, not whether it would have made the same mercy decision. Political loyalty, caste or religion cannot replace constitutionally relevant considerations.

[Correction] "Courts cannot be approached if the petition was considered rationally" confuses access to review with grounds for interference. A challenge may be brought alleging a recognised legal defect. If none is established, courts will not substitute their view on the merits. Use limited judicial review, not an undefined "partial review".
[Correction] Epuru Sudhakar does not impose a universal duty to communicate a detailed reasoned order to the affected petitioner. Its discussion of reasons distinguishes legitimate, relevant reasons underlying the decision from an obligation to convey those reasons. Courts may examine the underlying material; withholding relevant material in litigation may have consequences. Article 74(2) protects ministerial advice from inquiry, not automatically every document on which that advice was based.

1.3 Delay and Article 21

  • A death-row prisoner retains Article 21 protection until the end; a lawful sentence does not justify an unlawful execution process.
  • Administrative delay must be assessed for its duration, explanation and responsibility. Not every lapse of time automatically results in commutation.
  • [Additional] Relevant supervening circumstances can include established serious mental illness and procedural violations, depending on the facts.
  • [Additional] Shatrughan Chauhan requires written communication of mercy rejection, access to legal aid and a minimum 14-day interval between receipt of the rejection communication and scheduled execution.
[Correction] The rough statement that "delay is a violation of Fundamental Rights" needs the qualification undue, unexplained/inordinate and unreasonable executive delay. Shatrughan Chauhan did not prescribe a universal numerical deadline for the President's decision or an automatic commutation rule after a fixed period.

1.4 December 2024 Follow-up

[Additional] Identified judgment: State of Maharashtra & Others v. Pradeep Yashwant Kokade & Another, 9 December 2024, 2024 INSC 947. The Supreme Court reaffirmed the Article 21 safeguards and the duty to process mercy petitions promptly. It also held that an inordinate, unexplained delay by the Sessions Court in issuing an execution warrant can justify commutation.

  • [Additional] States/Union Territories must establish a dedicated cell in the Home or Prison Department, with a designated officer and legal-department support, to process mercy petitions within the timeframe laid down by the respective government.
  • [Additional] Prison authorities must promptly send relevant records, judgments, information about the convict and prison-conduct reports; electronic correspondence should be used as far as possible, subject to confidentiality.
  • [Additional] Sessions Courts must track pending remedies, give the convict notice and a hearing before issuing the execution warrant, and ensure legal aid where required.
  • [Additional] The operative direction requires 15 clear days between the convict's receipt of the order and execution warrant and actual execution. This has a different starting event from the 14-day mercy-rejection communication safeguard in Shatrughan Chauhan; retain both distinctions.
[Additional] Exam Point: The three-month period in paragraph 43(iii) of the 2024 judgment was for the States/UTs to report compliance with its directions. It was not a universal three-month deadline for the President to decide every mercy petition. Paragraph 42(v) rejects a mathematical, hard-and-fast test of what delay is inordinate.
[Verification Required] The original December 2024 sentence ends at "expeditiously and generall". Its intended final words cannot be reconstructed from the supplied notes. The verified ruling is recorded above without inventing the missing continuation.

2. Pardoning Power of the US President

Article II, Section 2, Clause 1 of the US Constitution authorises the President to grant reprieves and pardons for offences against the United States, except in cases of impeachment.

2.1 What Is a Pre-emptive Pardon?

A pre-emptive pardon is a pardon for past conduct granted before a prosecution or conviction. It prevents possible future prosecution for the covered past federal offences; it is not permission to commit future crimes.

Offence already committed → US pardon may be granted
No charge yet / proceedings pending / after conviction
Future offences → not covered merely by an earlier pardon
[Correction] Replace "after the commencement of an offence" with after its commission. The controlling distinction is past offence versus future offence, not simply that some preparatory activity has begun. Ex parte Garland, 71 U.S. 333 (1867), recognises exercise after commission, including before legal proceedings.
[Correction] "There cannot be judicial review" is an overstatement. In Schick v. Reed, 419 U.S. 256 (1974), the US Supreme Court examined and upheld a condition attached to presidential commutation, explaining that conditions must not themselves offend the Constitution. No ordinary appeal on clemency merits does not mean no judicial determination of legal limits.
[Not mentioned in the Constitution] Pre-emptive pardon is a modern descriptive label, not an expression in the US Pardon Clause; it is also not a named category in India's Article 72. [Additional] Neither the US rule nor India's rule makes a pardon an ordinary appellate judgment acquitting the accused.

3. Constitutional Position of the President

3.1 Constitutional Head and Ministerial Advice

India follows a parliamentary system. The President is the head of State and the formal constitutional executive; the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister exercises the political executive authority and is accountable to Lok Sabha.

Provision / caseCore position
Article 74(1)A Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at its head aids and advises the President, who acts in accordance with that advice.
[Additional] Article 74 provisoThe President may require reconsideration of advice, but must act on the advice tendered after reconsideration.
Article 75(3)The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the House of the People: Lok Sabha.
Shamsher Singh v. State of Punjab
23 August 1974		Presidential or gubernatorial "satisfaction" ordinarily means constitutional satisfaction under the Cabinet system, not personal satisfaction independent of ministerial advice.
U. N. R. Rao v. Indira Gandhi
17 March 1971		Article 74(1) is mandatory. Dissolution of Lok Sabha does not make the Prime Minister and ministers automatically cease to hold office or allow the President to govern without them.
[Correction] "The President has no constitutional discretion" means there is no general discretionary exception comparable to Article 163 for the Governor. It should not erase the express power to seek reconsideration under Article 74 or the limited judgment needed in government-formation situations. The rough case name "Samsher" is standardised to Shamsher Singh.

[Additional] The 42nd Amendment Act, 1976 made the duty to act on advice explicit; the 44th Amendment Act, 1978 added the reconsideration proviso. A special rule can prescribe a different binding input: for example, under Article 103(2), the President acts according to the Election Commission's opinion on the specified MP-disqualification questions. That is not personal discretion.

3.2 Limited Situational Judgment

[Not mentioned in the Constitution] Nominal executive, real executive, situational discretion, hung Parliament and floor test are explanatory or political/parliamentary labels, not named constitutional categories. The underlying rules come from Articles 74, 75 and 85 and responsible-government principles.

[Correction] A hung Lok Sabha means no party or pre-existing alliance has a clear majority; it does not necessarily mean that no government can form. A coalition or a minority ministry with outside support may command confidence. Dissolution is therefore not automatic, and appointment is not confined only to a single majority party.

4. Vice-President of India

Article 63 provides for the office. The Vice-President is commonly described as India's second-highest constitutional office, after the President. [Not mentioned in the Constitution] "Second-highest office" is a description of institutional standing/protocol, not wording in Article 63.

4.1 Qualifications: Article 66(3)-(4)

  1. Must be a citizen of India.
  2. Must have completed 35 years of age.
  3. Must be qualified for election as a member of Rajya Sabha; need not already be an MP.
  4. Must not hold an office of profit under the Union or a State government, or a local/other authority subject to their control.

The explanation to Article 66 exempts a person from being treated as holding an office of profit merely because the person is the President, Vice-President, Governor, or a Union/State Minister. These are the same categories of constitutional exemption used for presidential-election eligibility.

[Correction] "50% + 1 valid votes" is shorthand, not precise arithmetic when the number of valid votes is odd. With 599 valid votes, the quota is 300, not 300.5. Count valid votes cast, not the total sanctioned strength of Parliament.

[Additional] Electors indicate preferences. If no candidate reaches the quota, the lowest candidate is excluded and ballots are transferred according to the next available preferences under the counting rules. The Constitution expressly uses proportional representation and single transferable vote, even though only one office is being filled; these terms must not be tagged as absent from the Constitution.

[Additional] Article 66 provides an electoral college, not a joint parliamentary sitting. The 11th Amendment Act, 1961 replaced the earlier requirement that members assemble at a joint meeting.

[Correction] "Forfeited if less than one-sixth of valid votes received" has the wrong denominator and misses equality. An unsuccessful candidate with 50 votes or fewer forfeits the deposit under Section 20A(4); 51 votes avoids forfeiture on that ground. These are hypothetical figures, not the results of a particular election.

4.4 Oath and Conditions of Office

  • Article 69: the oath/affirmation is made before the President or a person appointed by the President. It promises true faith and allegiance to the Constitution and faithful discharge of duty.
  • Article 66(2): the Vice-President cannot be a member of either House of Parliament or either House of a State legislature. An elected legislator vacates that seat on entering the Vice-President's office.
  • Article 64: the Vice-President shall not hold any other office of profit.
[Correction] The heading "Conditions of office of president" in this part of the rough notes belongs to the Vice-President. The relevant provisions are Articles 64 and 66(2). The oath is in Article 69 itself, not the Third Schedule.

4.5 Term and Re-election: Article 67

The term is five years from entering office, subject to earlier resignation, removal or another vacancy. Under Article 67(c), the Vice-President continues after expiry until the successor enters office.

Re-election is permitted; there is no constitutional two-term limit. [Additional] Verified examples are Dr. S. Radhakrishnan, 1952-1962, and M. Hamid Ansari, 2007-2017, each serving two successive terms.

[Correction] "Re-election not mentioned" means there is no separate express provision identical to the President's Article 57; it does not mean re-election is prohibited. "Two times" should not be memorised as a constitutional maximum.

  • The Vice-President cannot preside at a Rajya Sabha sitting while the resolution for removal is under consideration.
  • [Additional] The Vice-President may speak and otherwise take part in those proceedings.
  • The Vice-President cannot vote at all on the removal resolution or on any other matter during those proceedings, including a casting vote.
[Correction] "Cannot preside when the resolution is pending" is too broad if it includes the whole notice period. Article 92 uses under consideration at a sitting. Also, the absence of ordinary membership alone is not the full explanation for no vote: the Chairman normally has a tie-breaking vote, but Article 92(2) expressly excludes it during removal proceedings.
[Correction] "Notice is given to the Deputy Chairman" compresses different roles. Notice is submitted through the Rajya Sabha Secretariat/Secretary-General; the Deputy Chairman's role in handling the matter is distinct. In the verified December 2024 example, the notice was submitted to the Secretary-General's office, and Parliament's official session record says that the Secretary-General reported the Deputy Chairman's ruling on 19 December. 4.10 Salary and Allowances

The Vice-President receives remuneration in the capacity of Chairman of Rajya Sabha, governed by Article 97 and parliamentary law. While acting as President or discharging presidential functions, the Vice-President receives the relevant presidential emoluments, allowances and privileges under Article 65(3), and not the Chairman's salary/allowances: Article 64 proviso.

[Correction] "No constitutional provision for salary" is too broad: the Constitution provides the framework through Articles 64, 65(3) and 97 and the Second Schedule. There is no separate salary simply for the title of Vice-President. Chairman remuneration is attached to that office, not payable only on days personally presiding; the two offices' remuneration is not drawn together while acting as President.

5. Prime Minister

5.1 Position and Appointment

The Prime Minister is the head of government, heads the Council of Ministers, and leads the real/political executive. The President remains the head of State. India's parliamentary executive draws on the British model, adapted to India's written Constitution.

  • Article 75(1): the President appoints the Prime Minister.
  • Normally, this is the leader of the party/alliance with majority support in Lok Sabha. The controlling test is the ability to command the House's confidence.
  • If no clear majority or successor exists, the limited situational role described in section 3 applies. A minority-party leader can head a government supported by other parties.
[Correction] "The Constitution has no specific PM provisions and only Article 75 mentions appointment" is incorrect. Article 74 places the PM at the head of the Council; Article 75 governs appointment and related ministerial matters; Article 78 imposes specific duties to the President. Also, "political head of India" means the government's political leader, not head of State.

5.4 Tenure, Resignation and Vacancy

Under Article 75(2), ministers hold office during the pleasure of the President. For the PM and ministry as a whole, read this with Article 75(3): continuation depends on Lok Sabha confidence, not presidential personal preference. The PM has no independently guaranteed five-year term.

5.5 Powers and Functions of the PM

  1. Leads government and the Council: sets political direction, coordinates ministers and leads Cabinet decision-making.
  2. Selects ministers: the President appoints other ministers on the PM's advice, under Article 75(1), subject to constitutional eligibility.
  3. Maintains ministerial unity: may seek a minister's resignation or advise the President to remove that minister.
  4. Allocates portfolios: determines distribution and reshuffling of ministerial responsibilities; the formal allocation/business framework is under Article 77(3).
  5. Chief political spokesperson: explains and defends the government's main policies. This does not mean only the PM may speak for the government.
  6. Parliamentary leadership: normally Leader of the House of which the PM is a member; nominates a minister who belongs to the other House to lead government business there.
  7. Advice on Lok Sabha dissolution: advises the President, subject to the constitutional position on confidence and viable alternatives.
  8. Institutional leadership: chairs NITI Aayog, the Inter-State Council and NDMA, under their respective constituting arrangements.
  9. Link with the President: fulfils the three express duties in Article 78, below.
[Not mentioned in the Constitution] Leader of the House is a role defined by parliamentary rules, not a constitutional office named in Articles 74-78. [Additional] It must not be confused with the Speaker/Chairman, who presides. A newly appointed non-MP PM does not yet have a House of membership.

Mains Points

  • Mercy and rule of law: clemency can correct harshness and account for humanitarian circumstances without becoming another judicial appeal. Ministerial advice and limited review constrain arbitrary use.
  • Dignity survives conviction: Shatrughan Chauhan and Kokade connect timely processing and lawful execution procedure with Article 21.
  • Constitutional head, responsible executive: Articles 74-75 reconcile formal presidential powers with political accountability. Situational judgment should restore a confidence-backed government, not create a rival executive.
  • Vice-President's dual role: Rajya Sabha chairmanship supports parliamentary deliberation; Article 65 supports executive continuity. The two functions are not performed simultaneously.
  • Impartial presiding: neutrality, fair opportunities to government and opposition, and consistent application of rules strengthen the credibility of the Upper House.
  • PM's leadership and collective responsibility: portfolio distribution and control over the team must operate with Cabinet coordination and Lok Sabha accountability, including when the PM belongs to Rajya Sabha.
  • Size is not efficiency: the 15% ceiling restrains expansion, but competence, coherent portfolios, coordination and accountability determine governmental effectiveness.
  • Article 78 preserves informed constitutional oversight: the President can receive decisions and information and require collective consideration without taking over ministerial administration.
  • [Additional] Reform arguments: transparent mercy tracking, complete records, explanations for administrative delay, impartial parliamentary procedure and coherent ministerial allocation are useful recommendations; do not describe every proposed safeguard as an existing constitutional command.

4. UPSC CSE Mains 2025 - General Studies Paper II

Compare and contrast the President's power to pardon in India and in the USA. Are there any limits to it in both the countries? What are 'preemptive pardons'? (Answer in 150 words)

Suggested answer points:

  • India: Article 72 covers court-martial cases, the Union executive sphere and all death sentences; Article 74 makes its exercise advice-bound.
  • USA: Article II, Section 2 covers federal offences, excludes impeachment and does not extend to State offences. It is an independently exercised presidential power.
  • Limits: India permits limited review under Epuru Sudhakar and Article 21 safeguards. US courts may determine constitutional scope and the validity of conditions, as Schick v. Reed illustrates; neither is an ordinary judicial merits appeal.
  • Pre-emptive pardon: in the US, relief for an offence already committed before prosecution/conviction, not advance permission for future crime. India's Article 72 uses the language of conviction.

Source: Drishti IAS: Polity Mains PYQs, 2025 section.

5. UPSC CSE Mains 2022 - General Studies Paper II

Discuss the role of the Vice-President of India as the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.

Suggested answer points:

  • Constitutional basis: Articles 64 and 89(1) make the VP ex officio Chairman, though not a member of Rajya Sabha.
  • Presiding role: maintains order, interprets rules and facilitates orderly debate and parliamentary scrutiny. Fair treatment of government and opposition strengthens the House's credibility.
  • Voting and removal: only a casting vote on a tie under Article 100; under Article 92, no presiding or voting while own removal is under consideration, but participation is permitted.
  • Continuity: while exercising presidential functions under Article 65, does not perform Chairman duties; Article 91 provides substitutes.
  • Assessment: constitutional authority is effective when exercised impartially, protecting deliberation in the chamber representing the States.

Source: Drishti IAS: Polity Mains PYQs, 2022 section.

6. UPSC CSE Mains 2014 - General Studies Paper II

Instances of President's delay in commuting death sentences has come under public debate as denial of justice. Should there be a time limit specified for the President to accept/reject such petitions? Analyse.

Suggested answer points:

  • Case for timely disposal: prolonged uncertainty undermines dignity and Article 21; unexplained administrative delay is incompatible with humane justice.
  • Judicial position: Shatrughan Chauhan (2014) permits commutation for undue, inordinate and unreasonable delay on the facts, without fixing a universal presidential deadline.
  • Balance: irreversible punishment requires careful examination of records and humanitarian grounds; automatic rejection on expiry of a deadline would defeat clemency's purpose.
  • Recommendation: accountable processing schedules, complete records, tracking and explanations for exceptional delay, with access to constitutional remedies.

[Additional] Later update: Kokade (2024) adds concrete administrative and execution-warrant safeguards. It post-dates the question and must not be presented as a judgment available to a candidate in 2014.

Source: Drishti IAS: Polity Mains PYQs, 2014 section.

7. UPSC CSE Mains 2014 - General Studies Paper II

The size of the cabinet should be as big as governmental work justifies and as big as the Prime Minister can manage as a team. How far is the efficacy of a government then inversely related to the size of the cabinet? Discuss.

Suggested answer points:

  • Legal context: Article 75(1A)'s 15% ceiling applies to the whole Council of Ministers, including the PM, not only the Cabinet.
  • Costs of excessive size: fragmented portfolios, coordination burdens, overlapping responsibilities and patronage can reduce efficiency.
  • Why smaller is not always better: an excessively small team can overload ministers and neglect the breadth of work or representation needs.
  • Conclusion: no mechanical inverse relationship; appropriate size, capable ministers, coherent allocation, PM-led coordination and collective accountability matter together.

No presidential discretion in any sense; hung Parliament means no government.No general discretionary domain, but express reconsideration and limited situational judgment exist. Coalitions/outside support can produce a confidence-backed government.
20 proposers and 20 proposers; deposit only in RBI.20 proposers + 20 seconders. Section 5C also permits cash with the Returning Officer or deposit in a Government Treasury with receipt.
Forfeiture below one-sixth of all valid votes.For an unsuccessful candidate, does not exceed one-sixth of the votes necessary for election, subject to Section 20A's provisions.
Quota simply 50% + 1.Discard the fraction after division by two, then add one; 599 valid votes gives quota 300.
VP material headed Conditions of office of President.Vice-President: Articles 64 and 66(2); oath under Article 69, not the Third Schedule.
Re-election not mentioned; two times.No separate Article 57-like clause for VP, but re-election is permitted without a constitutional two-term maximum.
No provision to fill a casual VP vacancy.Article 68(2) requires an election ASAP; no designated acting VP. Chairman duties have an Article 91 mechanism.
Incumbent continues only until a successor is elected.Continues until the successor enters office. A casual-vacancy winner gets a fresh five-year term.
VP becomes President on vacancy/absence.Acts as President during a vacancy or discharges functions during temporary inability; not automatic full-term succession.
Removal may start in either House; one-tenth requirement.Starts only in RS; one-tenth is quorum, not sponsorship/passage. RS needs then-members majority; LS needs present-and-voting majority.
Cannot preside while notice is pending; no vote only because not an MP.Bar applies during consideration at a sitting. Article 92(2) expressly removes even the ordinary casting vote during those proceedings.
Notice simply given to Deputy Chairman.Secretariat/Secretary-General submission and processing are distinct from the Deputy Chairman's role; Article 67 names no administrative recipient.
No constitutional salary provision; pay only while presiding.Articles 64, 65(3), 97 and Second Schedule provide the framework. Chairman remuneration attaches to the office; no dual pay while acting as President.
PM has no specific constitutional provisions apart from appointment.Articles 74, 75 and 78 expressly regulate the PM/Council and the PM's information duties.
PM is political head of India; oath by President or nominee.PM heads government, not the State. Article 75(4) specifies the President for office and secrecy oaths.
UK PM must already be a Commons member; distinct minimum age 18.Modern membership convention admits the recorded appointment exception; 18 is the MP-candidacy threshold, not a separate premiership-age clause.
Lok Sabha's end automatically creates PM/Council vacancies.It does not automatically terminate ministers. PM departure brings down the ministry, with reconstitution and transitional continuity.
Ministers must be more than 15%; PM additional to count.Not more than 15%, including PM; 543 gives maximum 81. The 91st Amendment Act's year is 2003; this insertion commenced in 2004.
Separate constitutional pleasure of PM; vacancies filled by PM or President alone.PM's pleasure is political shorthand. Portfolio reassignment differs from a new minister's appointment, formally made by President on PM's advice.
