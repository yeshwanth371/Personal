Indian Polity - Class Notes | 15 September 2026

Topics: President's conditions of office, immunity, term and re-election; vacancies and acting arrangements; impeachment in India and the United States; executive and legislative powers.

Labels used: [Correction] explains a substantive correction to the rough notes. [Additional] marks relevant supplementary material. [Verification Required] identifies an unresolved claim. [Not mentioned in the Constitution] distinguishes explanatory classifications from constitutional wording.

1. Conditions of Office: Article 59

  • The President cannot be a member of either House of Parliament or a House of a State Legislature.
  • If such a member is elected President, the legislative seat is deemed vacated on the date of entering presidential office.
  • The President cannot hold any other office of profit.
  • The President is entitled to the use of official residences without payment of rent, and to emoluments, allowances and privileges determined by Parliament by law.
  • Emoluments and allowances cannot be diminished during the term.
[Correction] A separate resignation from the legislative seat is not required to make Article 59 operate. A member may resign earlier, but otherwise the seat is automatically deemed vacated on entering office, not merely on winning the election.

1.1 Charged Expenditure

The President's emoluments and allowances, and other expenditure relating to the office, are charged on the Consolidated Fund of India (CFI) under Article 112(3)(a).

[Correction] The rough note grouped salary determination and the CFI charge entirely under Article 59. Article 59 governs the entitlement and parliamentary determination; Article 112(3)(a) provides the constitutional charge.
[Additional] Under Article 113(1), charged expenditure is not submitted to Parliament's vote, but either House may discuss it. "Charged" does not mean exempt from all parliamentary scrutiny.

3. Term and Re-election: Articles 56-57

  • Article 56(1): five years from the date the President enters office, subject to resignation or removal.
  • Resignation: writing addressed to the Vice-President. Under Article 56(2), the Vice-President must forthwith communicate it to the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
  • Continuity: notwithstanding expiry of the term, the incumbent continues until the successor enters office.
  • Article 57: a current or former President is eligible for re-election, subject to other constitutional provisions. There is no constitutional limit on the number of terms.
[Correction] "No President contested more than two terms" must not be used as a two-term rule. Elections are contested; terms are served. Article 57 imposes no two-term ceiling.
[Verification Required] The historical assertion that no person who held the presidency contested more than two presidential elections was not independently established from a complete official election history. Do not extend it to unsuccessful repeat candidates.
[Additional] The official presidential profile confirms that Dr. Rajendra Prasad served two terms, holding office from 26 January 1950 to 13 May 1962. This historical fact is not a legal term limit.

4. Vacancies, Election Timing and Continuity

4.1 Causes and Deadlines

SituationWho performs presidential functions?Election requirement

Resignation, death, removal by impeachment, or other vacancy such as election declared voidThe Vice-President acts as President under Article 65(1), until the newly elected President enters office.As soon as possible, and not later than six months from the vacancy: Article 62(2).

[Correction] The six-month deadline is not a grace period for every presidential election. Normal-expiry elections must be completed before the term ends; the six-month outer limit applies to casual vacancies under Article 62(2).
[Correction] To avoid an interregnum, the incumbent continues until the successor enters office, not merely until the successor is elected. This continuity rule does not cancel the duty to complete the election before normal expiry.
[Additional] A President elected to fill a casual vacancy receives a full five-year term from entering office, subject to Article 56, not just the predecessor's unexpired term. "Interregnum" and "casual vacancy" are useful explanatory labels [Not mentioned in the Constitution] in these provisions.

4.2 Election Declared Void: Article 71

  • Article 71(1): doubts and disputes connected with the election of the President or Vice-President are decided by the Supreme Court; its decision is final.
  • Article 71(2): if the election is declared void, official acts performed on or before the date of the Supreme Court's decision are not invalidated by reason of that declaration.
  • Article 71(4): the election cannot be questioned on the ground of any vacancy, for whatever reason, among members of the electoral college.
[Correction] The unfinished line "Incomplete electoral college is not" means that electoral-college vacancies alone are not a ground for challenging the election. It does not excuse other election-law violations.
[Correction] "All earlier actions remain valid" is too broad. Article 71(2) prevents invalidity merely because the election was void; it does not cure other legal defects in an official act. Its wording includes acts on the decision date.

4.4 Oath, Conditions, Powers and Immunities

Article 60 requires every President, and every person acting as President or discharging presidential functions, to make and subscribe the oath or affirmation before entering office. The oath commits the person to faithful performance, preserving, protecting and defending the Constitution and the law, and the service and well-being of the people.

The office remains governed by Article 59's conditions. Under Article 65(3), the Vice-President acting as President or discharging the functions has all presidential powers and immunities and the prescribed emoluments, allowances and privileges. Section 3(4) of the 1969 Act makes corresponding provision for its substitute.

[Correction] The rough statement about anyone "holding the office" is better separated into its legal sources: oath: Article 60; conditions: Article 59; acting Vice-President's powers and immunities: Article 65(3); statutory substitute: section 3(4). Do not assume that the acting arrangement is a fresh election.

5. Impeachment of the President: Article 61

5.1 Ground and Procedure

The constitutional ground is "violation of the Constitution". The expression is expressly mentioned in Article 61. [Additional] The Constitution does not define it.

[Correction] One-fourth is the signature requirement for written notice, not the voting threshold for passing the initiating resolution. The rough "14 days' notice to the President" summary should not replace Article 61(2)(a)'s precise requirement: a resolution moved after at least fourteen days' written notice, signed by the required members, of the intention to move it.
Exam Point: Both Houses use two-thirds of total membership, not two-thirds of members present and voting. The Houses vote separately; there is no joint sitting for impeachment. For a hypothetical total membership of 240, at least 60 notice-signatories and 160 votes for the resolution are required.

5.2 Who Participates?

  • Both elected and nominated MPs participate: Article 61 refers to House membership, without the elected-only limitation used in Article 54.
  • State Legislative Assemblies and Councils do not participate. Delhi and Puducherry Assembly members also have no separate role in impeachment.
  • The process is described as a quasi-judicial function of Parliament because it includes charges, investigation, representation and a finding. [Not mentioned in the Constitution] "Quasi-judicial" is an analytical description, not Article 61's label.
[Verification Required] The rough notes state that no President of India has been impeached. This historical claim was not independently confirmed against a current official parliamentary record in this research; it is retained here as a claim requiring verification, not as a checked current fact.

5.3 Why Do States Not Participate?

Legal answer: Article 54 establishes a federal electoral college for election, while Article 61 expressly assigns the charge and investigation to the two Houses of Parliament. Election and removal are different constitutional procedures; membership of the electoral college does not confer a right to impeach.

[Additional] Institutional explanation, not an express constitutional reason: impeachment concerns accountability of the Union head of State through a national parliamentary process with high thresholds and an investigation. The Council of States supplies a federal element within Parliament, but State legislatures do not separately vote or ratify the removal. Avoid claiming that the Constitution itself states a single reason for excluding them.

6. United States: Impeachment and Removal

Article II, Section 4 of the US Constitution specifies removal upon impeachment and conviction for "Treason, Bribery, or other high Crimes and Misdemeanors". The allocation of functions and trial rules appear in Article I, Sections 2-3.

  1. The House of Representatives alone impeaches: it approves articles of impeachment by a simple majority vote.
  2. Impeachment is the formal accusation. It does not by itself remove the President.
  3. The Senate tries the impeachment. When the US President is tried, the Chief Justice of the United States presides.
  4. Conviction requires two-thirds of the Senators present; conviction results in removal from office.
[Correction] The US Senate threshold is two-thirds of members present, not two-thirds of total membership. Article II, Section 4 supplies the grounds, but Article I provides the House/Senate procedure.
[Additional] "Simple majority" is a procedural classification [Not mentioned in the Constitution] in the US impeachment clauses; the official Senate explanation uses this voting rule for the House. Do not substitute it for the express Senate conviction threshold.

7. Executive Powers and Government Business

7.1 Articles 53, 74 and 75

Under Article 53, Union executive power is vested in the President and is exercised directly or through subordinate officers in accordance with the Constitution.

  • The President appoints the Prime Minister; other Ministers are appointed by the President on the Prime Minister's advice: Article 75(1).
  • Ministers hold office during the pleasure of the President: Article 75(2).
[Additional] Read these formal powers with Article 74: the President acts on the Council of Ministers' advice, may require reconsideration once, but must act on advice tendered after reconsideration. Under Article 75(3), the Council is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. Pleasure tenure is not a general personal power to dismiss a ministry enjoying Lok Sabha confidence.

7.2 Article 77: Three Rules

ClauseRule
77(1)All executive action of the Government of India is expressed to be taken in the President's name.
77(2)Orders and instruments made and executed in that name are authenticated in the manner prescribed by rules made by the President.
77(3)The President makes rules for the more convenient transaction of government business and its allocation among Ministers.
[Additional] Proper authentication prevents a challenge on the specified ground that the order was not personally made or executed by the President. It does not protect the order from every constitutional or legal challenge.

7.4 Information and Reference to the Council: Article 78

[Additional] Article 78 makes it the Prime Minister's duty to keep the President informed and provides the following constitutional channels:
  • 78(a): communicate all Council of Ministers decisions relating to Union administration and proposals for legislation.
  • 78(b): furnish information about Union administration and legislative proposals that the President calls for.
  • 78(c): if the President requires, submit to the Council any matter decided by an individual Minister but not considered by the Council.
Exam Point: Article 78(c) is a reference of an individual Minister's decision to the Council. It is distinct from requiring reconsideration of ministerial advice under Article 74; neither creates a general presidential power to govern independently.

9. Legislative Powers and Functions

9.1 President as Part of Parliament

Article 79: Parliament consists of the President, Council of States (Rajya Sabha) and House of the People (Lok Sabha).

[Correction] The rough notes cited Article 59 for the President's membership of Parliament's constitutional structure. The correct provision is Article 79. The President is a component of Parliament, not a member of either House.

9.2 Summoning, Prorogation and Dissolution: Article 85

AdjournmentEnding/suspending a sitting until a specified time.Presiding officer of the House

[Additional] Article 85 permits prorogation of either or both Houses, but dissolution of Lok Sabha only; Rajya Sabha is not subject to dissolution. No more than six months may intervene between the last sitting in one session and the first sitting in the next. "Adjournment sine die" is parliamentary terminology [Not mentioned in the Constitution].
[Additional] Prorogation is generally after adjournment sine die, but a House can be prorogued while in session. These powers ordinarily operate on ministerial advice; dissolution can involve limited situational discretion in exceptional circumstances.

9.3 Ordinances: Article 123

The President may promulgate an Ordinance when circumstances require immediate action, except when both Houses of Parliament are in session.

[Additional] An Ordinance has the same force and effect as an Act of Parliament, subject to constitutional limits. It must be laid before both Houses and ceases six weeks after reassembly, unless ended earlier by disapproval of both Houses or withdrawn. If the Houses reassemble on different dates, count six weeks from the later date. One House being out of session can satisfy the session condition; both need not be in recess.

9.4 Nominations: Article 80

The rough reference "President nominates" concerns, in this parliamentary context, 12 members of Rajya Sabha having special knowledge or practical experience in literature, science, art and social service, under Article 80(1)(a) and 80(3).

[Additional] Do not add two nominated Anglo-Indian members to the current Lok Sabha. Anglo-Indian nomination ceased under Article 334's time limit as altered by the 104th Amendment. Rajya Sabha nomination continues.

9.5 Special Address: Article 87

The President addresses both Houses assembled together at the commencement of:

  1. the first session after each general election to the Lok Sabha; and
  2. the first session of each year.
[Correction] The constitutional wording is the commencement of the first session, not an address separately at each House's first sitting, nor an address after every kind of general election. Article 87 specifically refers to a Lok Sabha general election.
[Additional] Article 86 separately permits the President to address either House or both Houses together and to send messages. Article 87's special address is not the same as a joint sitting to resolve a legislative deadlock under Article 108.

[Additional] Textbook vocabulary: absolute veto describes withholding assent; suspensive veto describes returning a Bill for reconsideration; pocket veto describes keeping a Bill pending without a decision. [Not mentioned in the Constitution] These are analytical labels, not expressions in Article 111.

Exam Point: Article 111 contains no numerical assent deadline and uses "as soon as possible" for return. Do not turn the textbook label "pocket veto" into a claim of an express, unrestricted constitutional entitlement to indefinite inaction. Read presidential action with Article 74.

9.7 State Bills Reserved for the President: Article 201

[Additional] When a Governor reserves a State Bill for the President:
  • The President may assent or withhold assent.
  • For a non-Money Bill, the President may direct the Governor to return it to the State Legislature with a reconsideration message.
  • The State Legislature must reconsider it within six months from receipt of the message. If passed again, with or without amendment, it is presented again for the President's consideration.
  • Unlike Article 111, Article 201 does not say the President must assent after repassage.
Exam Point: Article 201's six months concerns the State Legislature's reconsideration, not a presidential decision deadline. These are the textual rules; a fixed judicial deadline or a rule of deemed assent should not be assumed without checking the applicable judgments.

9.8 Joint Sitting to Resolve Deadlock: Article 108

[Additional] After a Bill passes one House and goes to the other, the President may initiate a joint sitting where:
  1. the other House rejects it;
  2. the Houses finally disagree about amendments; or
  3. more than six months pass after the other House receives it without passing it, subject to Article 108's exclusions when counting time.

The President notifies the intention to summon a joint sitting and may then summon it. The Bill is decided by a majority of members of both Houses present and voting, subject to the Article's amendment rules.

  • No joint sitting for Money Bills; Constitution Amendment Bills require separate passage under Article 368 and likewise cannot use this route.
  • The Speaker of Lok Sabha ordinarily presides, not the President. Alternative presiding arrangements follow Article 118 and the joint-sitting rules.
  • The President cannot revive a Bill already lapsed on dissolution through this procedure. However, if the intention was notified before dissolution, Article 108(5) permits the joint sitting and passage afterward.
[Additional] In calculating the six months for non-passage, exclude periods during which the receiving House is prorogued or adjourned for more than four consecutive days. This is different from Article 85's maximum interval between sessions.

9.10 Decisions on MP Disqualification: Article 103

[Additional] A question whether an MP has become subject to a disqualification under Article 102(1) is referred to the President. Before deciding, the President must obtain the Election Commission's opinion and act according to it.
Exam Point: This does not cover defection under the Tenth Schedule, which is linked to Article 102(2) and ordinarily decided by the Speaker or Chairman. Article 103 is not a general presidential power to remove MPs at will.

9.12 Temporary Presiding Arrangements

[Additional] Under Article 95(1), when both Speaker and Deputy Speaker offices are vacant, the President appoints a Lok Sabha member to perform the Speaker's duties. Under Article 91(1), when the Chairman's office is vacant or the Vice-President acts as President/discharges presidential functions, and the Deputy Chairman's office is also vacant, the President appoints a Rajya Sabha member to perform the Chairman's duties.
Distinction: These are prescribed vacancy arrangements, not a general presidential choice of presiding officer whenever the regular officer is absent from a sitting.

9.13 Regulations for Specified UTs: Article 240

[Additional] Article 240 empowers the President to make regulations for the peace, progress and good government of the Union Territories specified in that Article. This is not a power applicable to every UT.
  • For Puducherry, the power is restricted once its Article 239A Legislature first meets; it may operate during the Legislature's dissolution or suspension as provided in Article 240.
  • A regulation may repeal or amend an applicable parliamentary Act or other applicable law and, when promulgated, has the same force and effect as an Act of Parliament applying to that UT.
  • Distinguish Article 239 administration, Article 240 regulations and Article 123 Ordinances. They have different constitutional conditions.

10. Key Facts for Prelims

  • 59: legislative seat vacated on entering office; no other office of profit; emoluments/allowances cannot be diminished.
  • 112(3)(a): presidential expenditure charged on CFI; 113(1): discussable, not voted.
  • 361: official non-answerability; no criminal proceedings or court-issued arrest process during term; two-month notice for institution of personal civil proceedings.
  • 56-57: five years from entering office; resignation to Vice-President; re-election without a constitutional term limit.
  • 62: normal election before expiry; casual vacancy as soon as possible and within six months; new President gets a full five years.
  • 65: Vice-President acts during vacancy or discharges functions during temporary inability; 1969 Act: CJI/senior-most available SC Judge in prescribed contingencies.
  • 71: Supreme Court decides election disputes; prior acts not invalidated merely by election being void; electoral-college vacancies are not a challenge ground.
  • 61: one-fourth notice-signatories + 14 days; two-thirds total membership in each House; nominated MPs included, State legislatures excluded.
  • US: House impeaches by simple majority; Senate convicts by two-thirds present.
  • 53/74/75/77: formal executive power, binding advice, ministerial appointments and responsibility, government business.
  • 341/342: initial SC/ST notification by President; later inclusion/exclusion by Parliament by law.
  • 79/85/87/123: Parliament's composition; sessions/dissolution; special address; Ordinances.
  • [Additional] 78: PM furnishes information; President can require an individual Minister's decision to be considered by the Council.
  • [Additional] 111/201/368: distinguish parliamentary Bills, reserved State Bills and mandatory assent to constitutional amendments.
  • [Additional] 108: joint sitting for specified deadlocks; no Money Bills or Constitution Amendment Bills; President summons, Speaker ordinarily presides.

11. Mains Points

  • Continuity without permanent succession: holdover at normal expiry and acting arrangements for casual vacancies prevent a gap while preserving the requirement of election.
  • Immunity versus accountability: Article 361 protects the office-holder from specified proceedings; impeachment and legal proceedings against government action preserve constitutional accountability.
  • High-threshold removal: Article 61 combines a stated constitutional ground, advance notice, investigation, representation and separate supermajorities to prevent routine partisan removal.
  • Federal distinction: State participation in election under Article 54 does not imply participation in removal under Article 61. Each procedure has its own institutional design.
  • India-US comparison: initiation, voting denominators and the meaning of "impeachment" differ; the American accusation stage must be distinguished from conviction and removal.
  • Parliamentary executive: Articles 53 and 77 supply the formal framework; Articles 74-75 connect its exercise with ministerial advice and Lok Sabha accountability.
  • Targeted constitutional safeguards: distinguish territorial Scheduled Area orders from community lists; Articles 341-342 reserve subsequent list changes to legislation.

Article 361 makes every official act legally unchallengeable.Personal non-answerability does not erase remedies against the Government.
Criminal immunity is lifelong.The court-proceedings bar in Article 361(2) applies during the term.
India limits Presidents to two terms.Article 57 imposes no such limit.
Every presidential election can wait six months.Normal-expiry election must finish before expiry; the six-month limit concerns casual vacancies.
The successor to a casual vacancy serves only the balance.A newly elected President receives a full five-year term.
Nominated MPs cannot impeach because they cannot elect the President.Election and impeachment use different membership rules.
Two-thirds means the same denominator in India and the US.India: total membership of each House. US conviction: Senators present.
The President can amend SC/ST lists by a fresh notification.Later inclusion/exclusion needs Parliament's law.
Prorogation ends Lok Sabha; sine die ends the session.Dissolution ends Lok Sabha; prorogation ends a session; sine die adjournment leaves the next sitting undated.
The President is part of Parliament under Article 59.Article 79; Article 59 concerns conditions of office.

13. 30-Second Revision

Office: Art. 59 = no legislative seat/other office of profit; protected emoluments. CFI charge = Art. 112.
Immunity: Art. 361 = official non-answerability, term-bound criminal-process protection, two-month notice for personal civil suits.
Term: five years; resignation to VP; re-election allowed; incumbent until successor enters office.
Vacancy: VP acts; double vacancy = CJI or senior-most available SC Judge under 1969 Act. Election within six months; full new term.
Impeachment: Art. 61 = violation of Constitution; 1/4 notice-signatories, 14 days, 2/3 total in each House; nominated MPs yes, MLAs no.
US: House accusation by simple majority; Senate conviction by 2/3 present.
Powers: 53 executive; 74 advice; 75 Ministers; 77 business; 79 Parliament; 85 sessions; 87 address; 123 Ordinances.
[Additional] Executive: 78 information; 239 UT administration; 263 Inter-State Council; 280/340/344 commissions.
[Additional] Legislative: 111 assent; 201 reserved State Bills; 368 mandatory amendment assent; 108 joint sitting; 103 MP disqualification on ECI opinion; 240 specified-UT regulations.
Lists: SC/ST initial notification by President; subsequent changes by Parliament. Scheduled Areas = Fifth Schedule.

Q1. UPSC CSE Prelims 2014, General Studies Paper I

Consider the following statements:

  1. The President shall make rules for the more convenient transaction of the business of the Government of India, and for the allocation among Ministers of the said business.
  2. All executive actions of the Government of India shall be expressed to be taken in the name of the Prime Minister.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only
(b) 2 only
(c) Both 1 and 2
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: (a) 1 only. Article 77(3) supports statement 1; Article 77(1) uses the President's name, not the Prime Minister's.

Q2. UPSC CSE Prelims 2024, General Studies Paper I

With reference to the Parliament of India, consider the following statements:

  1. Prorogation of a House by the President of India does not require the advice of the Council of Ministers.
  2. Prorogation of a House is generally done after the House is adjourned sine die but there is no bar to the President of India prorogating the House which is in session.
  3. Dissolution of the Lok Sabha is done by the President of India who, save in exceptional circumstances, does so on the advice of the Council of Ministers.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only
(b) 1 and 2
(c) 2 and 3
(d) 3 only

Answer: (c) 2 and 3. Read Article 85 with Article 74. Prorogation requires advice and can occur during a session. Dissolution ordinarily follows ministerial advice, subject to exceptional constitutional situations.

Q3. UPSC CSE Prelims 2018, General Studies Paper I

Consider the following statements:

  1. No criminal proceedings shall be instituted against the Governor of a State in any court during his term of office.
  2. The emoluments and allowances of the Governor of a State shall not be diminished during his term of office.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only
(b) 2 only
(c) Both 1 and 2
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: (c) Both 1 and 2. Article 361(2) bars criminal proceedings being instituted or continued during the term and applies to both President and Governor. Article 158(4) protects the Governor's emoluments and allowances; Article 59(4) is the presidential counterpart.

Q4. UPSC CSE Prelims 2023, General Studies Paper I

With reference to 'Scheduled Areas' in India, consider the following statements:

  1. Within a State, the notification of an area as Scheduled Area takes place through an Order of the President.
  2. The largest administrative unit forming the Scheduled Area is the District and the lowest is the cluster of villages in the Block.
  3. The Chief Ministers of the concerned States are required to submit annual reports to the Union Home Ministry on the administration of Scheduled Areas in the States.

How many of the above statements are correct?

(a) Only one
(b) Only two
(c) All three
(d) None

Answer: (b) Only two. Statements 1 and 2 are correct. The presidential order is provided for in Fifth Schedule paragraph 6. The administrative-unit description in statement 2 is not itself a textual formula in that paragraph. Statement 3 is wrong: under paragraph 3, the Governor reports to the President, annually or whenever required by the President.
