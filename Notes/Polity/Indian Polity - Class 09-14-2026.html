1. Constitutional Government: Three Main Organs

India has a written Constitution. Governmental power is principally exercised through the Executive, Legislature and Judiciary. Their functions overlap in limited ways, but each has a distinct core role.

OrganCore roleUnion levelState level
ExecutiveImplements laws and administers government.President, Vice-President, Prime Minister, Council of Ministers and Attorney-General for India.Governor, Chief Minister, Council of Ministers and Advocate-General for the State.
LegislatureMakes laws, authorises taxation and expenditure, and holds the political executive accountable.Parliament: President + Council of States (Rajya Sabha) + House of the People (Lok Sabha), under Article 79.Governor + Legislative Assembly; a Legislative Council also exists in some States, under Article 168.
JudiciaryAdjudicates disputes, interprets law and protects constitutional limits.Supreme Court of India.High Courts and subordinate courts.
[Correction] The rough table placed the Attorney-General only as part of a general list and left the State judiciary incomplete. The Attorney-General is a constitutional officer within the Union Executive chapter (Article 76); the Advocate-General is the corresponding State constitutional officer (Article 165). High Courts and subordinate courts form the principal constitutional judicial structure below the Supreme Court.
[Additional] India does not follow an absolutely rigid separation of powers. For example, the President is part of Parliament, Ministers are generally members of the legislature, and higher courts exercise judicial review. Separation of powers is nevertheless recognised as part of the Constitution's basic structure.

2. Map of the Union Executive

Part V deals with the Union. Its Chapter I, Articles 52-78, deals with the Union Executive.

[Correction] The rough note said "Part V (Articles 52-78)." Part V actually extends beyond Article 78 and also contains provisions on Parliament, legislative powers of the President, the Union judiciary, and the Comptroller and Auditor-General. It is specifically Chapter I of Part V that covers the Union Executive in Articles 52-78.

3. President of India: Constitutional Position

  • Article 52: There shall be a President of India.
  • Article 53: The executive power of the Union is vested in the President and is exercised directly or through subordinate officers in accordance with the Constitution.
  • Head of State: the President is the formal constitutional head and represents the Republic of India.
  • Nominal or de jure executive: [Not mentioned in the Constitution] This textbook classification contrasts the President with the Council of Ministers as the real political executive. Under Article 74, the President acts in accordance with ministerial advice, subject to the power to require reconsideration once.
  • First citizen of India: [Not mentioned in the Constitution] This is a conventional description of precedence and symbolic status, not a constitutional title or a special category of citizenship.
[Correction] The rough notes linked "first citizen" directly to India being a republic. A republic explains why the head of State is elected rather than hereditary; it does not create a constitutional rule naming the President the "first citizen." The Constitution also does not use the expressions "de jure head" or "representative of unity, integrity and solidarity." They are explanatory descriptions.
Exam Point: All Union executive action is formally taken in the President's name, but India has a parliamentary executive. The President normally exercises constitutional powers on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers, not as an independently governing executive.

4. Term, Re-election, Qualifications, Conditions and Oath

4.1 Term of Office under Article 56

  • The President holds office for a term of five years from the date of entering office.
  • The President may resign by writing addressed to the Vice-President. The Vice-President must communicate the resignation to the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
  • The President may be removed for violation of the Constitution by impeachment under Article 61.
  • Even after the five-year term expires, the President continues in office until the successor enters office. This prevents a vacancy merely because the successor has not yet assumed office.

4.2 Eligibility for Re-election under Article 57

A person who holds or has held office as President is eligible for re-election, subject to the other provisions of the Constitution. The Constitution prescribes no limit on the number of terms a person may serve as President.

[Additional] Dr. Rajendra Prasad is the only President of India to have served two full terms.

4.3 Qualifications under Article 58

A person is qualified for election as President if that person:

  1. is a citizen of India;
  2. has completed 35 years of age;
  3. is qualified for election as a member of the House of the People (Lok Sabha); and
  4. does not hold an office of profit under the Union Government, a State Government, or a local or other authority subject to the control of either government.
[Additional] Unlike the United States Constitution's "natural born Citizen" condition for its President, Article 58 requires only Indian citizenship. It does not distinguish between citizenship acquired by birth, descent, registration or naturalisation, provided the person remains otherwise qualified.

4.4 Office-of-Profit Exception

For Article 58, a person is not deemed to hold an office of profit merely because the person is:

  • President or Vice-President of the Union;
  • Governor of any State; or
  • a Minister for the Union or for any State.
[Correction] The constitutional exception in Article 58 expressly names the President, Vice-President, Governors and Union or State Ministers. It does not list MPs, MLAs or MLCs as a blanket exception. Membership of a legislature is not by itself an "office of profit," but a legislator may separately hold another disqualifying office. On becoming President, Article 59 deems any seat held in Parliament or a State Legislature vacated on entering office.

4.5 Conditions under Article 59

  • The President cannot be a member of either House of Parliament or of a House of a State Legislature.
  • The President cannot hold any other office of profit.
  • The President is entitled, without payment of rent, to use the official residence and receives emoluments, allowances and privileges determined by Parliament.
  • The President's emoluments and allowances cannot be diminished during the term of office.

4.6 Oath or Affirmation under Article 60

Before entering office, every President, and every person acting as President or discharging the functions of the President, must make and subscribe an oath or affirmation.

  • It is administered by the Chief Justice of India.
  • If the Chief Justice is absent, it is administered by the senior-most available Judge of the Supreme Court.
  • The person swears or affirms to faithfully execute the office of President, to preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and the law, and to devote himself or herself to the service and well-being of the people of India.
Core words to remember: faithfully execute the office; preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and the law; serve the people of India and promote their well-being.
Exam Point: The President's oath is administered by the Chief Justice of India, whereas the Governor's oath under Article 159 is administered by the Chief Justice of the concerned High Court, or in the Chief Justice's absence, the senior-most available Judge of that High Court.

5. Nomination and Security Deposit

The Presidential and Vice-Presidential Elections Act, 1952, not the Constitution itself, provides the principal nomination requirements:

RequirementRule
ProposersAt least 50 electors must subscribe the nomination paper as proposers.
SecondersAt least 50 electors must subscribe it as seconders.
DepositRs. 15,000. It may be deposited with the Returning Officer in cash, or a receipt may be produced showing that the candidate or someone on the candidate's behalf deposited it in the Reserve Bank of India or a government treasury.
ForfeitureA defeated candidate's deposit is returned only if the candidate's valid votes exceed one-sixth of the number of votes necessary to secure election. Otherwise it is forfeited to the Central Government.
[Correction] The rough notes said the deposit must be made "in RBI." Cash payment to the Returning Officer is also permitted, and a treasury/RBI receipt is another route. They also compared the candidate's votes with one-sixth of all valid votes polled. The statutory comparison is with one-sixth of the votes necessary to secure the return of a candidate. Because votes are weighted, the Act speaks in terms of the number of votes credited to the candidate.
Common confusion: The 50 proposers, 50 seconders, Rs. 15,000 deposit and forfeiture rule are statutory requirements. They are not qualifications listed in Article 58.

6. Electoral College under Article 54

The President is elected by an electoral college consisting of:

  1. the elected members of both Houses of Parliament; and
  2. the elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of the States.

For Articles 54 and 55, "State" expressly includes the National Capital Territory of Delhi and the Union Territory of Puducherry. This inclusion was made by the 70th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992.

IncludedExcluded
Elected Lok Sabha membersNominated members of either House of Parliament
Elected Rajya Sabha membersAll members of State Legislative Councils, whether elected or nominated
Elected MLAs of StatesNominated members of State or UT Legislative Assemblies
Elected MLAs of Delhi and PuducherryMembers of other UT legislatures unless Article 54 is constitutionally amended to include them
[Correction] The rough notes broadly included elected MLAs "from State and UTs." Article 54 presently includes State Assemblies and expressly treats only Delhi and Puducherry as States for this purpose. The existence of a legislature in another Union Territory does not, by itself, add its members to the presidential electoral college.
[Correction] Avoid memorising fixed figures such as "543 Lok Sabha and 233 Rajya Sabha electors." Vacancies and the composition applicable to a particular election can change. The constitutional rule is all elected members of both Houses, not a permanently fixed number.
[Additional] Under Article 71(4), an election cannot be challenged merely because a vacancy existed among the members of the electoral college. If a State Assembly is dissolved, there are no sitting elected MLAs from that Assembly to participate until a new Assembly is constituted.

7. Manner of Election under Article 55

The President is elected:

  • indirectly, by the electoral college rather than by the people directly;
  • according to proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote; and
  • by secret ballot.
Ballot: Presidential voting uses marked ballot papers and ballot boxes under the election rules. An elector marks preferences as 1, 2, 3.... The first preference is essential for a valid ballot; later preferences are optional.

7.1 Winning Quota

Because only one office is filled, the quota is:

Quota = (total value of valid votes polled / 2) + 1, disregarding any remainder in the division.
[Correction] The rough formula "total valid votes / (number of seats + 1) + 1" is a general quota form. For the single presidential vacancy, the operative result is a majority of the total value of valid votes: half, plus one. The count uses the value of votes, not merely the number of ballot papers.

7.2 Counting and Transfer: Worked Example

Assume the total value of valid ballots is 1,000. The quota is 501.

CandidateFirst-preference vote valuePosition
A450Below quota
B350Below quota
C200Lowest; excluded

Candidate C is excluded. Each ballot credited to C is examined for its next available preference. Suppose C's weighted ballots transfer 140 to B and 50 to A, while 10 have no continuing preference and become exhausted.

CandidateEarlier totalTransferred valueNew total
A450+50500
B350+140490

With only A and B continuing, the candidate with the greater credited vote value is declared elected under the applicable count rules; in this illustration, A leads with 500. In a real count, the Returning Officer follows the detailed statutory rules for exclusions, continuing candidates, exhausted papers and ties.

First-preference count → quota reached? → Yes: declare elected / No: exclude lowest candidate → transfer ballots at next available preference → repeat under the counting rules
[Correction] "Proportional representation" does not mean that a presidential candidate wins a proportion of seats; there is only one office. In this election it operates through preferential voting and weighted votes. Preferences are transferred after the lowest continuing candidate is excluded; the elector's vote is not cast again.

8. Value of Votes and Federal Parity

Article 55 seeks:

  • uniformity in the scale of representation among different States; and
  • parity between the States as a whole and the Union in the presidential election.

8.1 Value of an MLA's Vote

Value of each elected MLA's vote = State population / (total elected MLAs x 1,000)

Operationally, divide the State population by the total number of elected MLAs, then count one vote for every complete multiple of 1,000 in the quotient. If the remainder is 500 or more, add one.

Illustration: If a State has a population of 20,000,000 and 200 elected MLAs:

20,000,000 / 200 = 100,000; therefore each MLA's vote value = 100.

8.2 Value of an MP's Vote

Value of each elected MP's vote = total value of votes of all elected MLAs / total number of elected MPs of both Houses

Fractions exceeding one-half are counted as one; other fractions are disregarded. Every elected MP has the same vote value in that presidential election, regardless of House or State.

[Correction] The rough MLA formula placed "x 1,000" ambiguously and the MP formula multiplied the MLA value again by the number of MLAs. The numerator for the MP formula is already the aggregate value of all elected MLAs' votes. For MLAs, the value is population divided by elected MLAs and then by 1,000, with Article 55's rounding rule.

8.3 Census Basis

Article 55's Explanation currently requires the word "population" to be read using the 1971 Census figures until the relevant figures of the first census taken after 2026 have been published.

[Correction] It is too broad to say merely that value "is based on the 1971 Census because it was the last census before the National Population Policy of 1976." The controlling reason is the express constitutional freeze. The 42nd Amendment initially froze the reference until after 2000; the 84th Amendment extended it until the first census after 2026 whose relevant figures are published.
Exam Point: MLA vote values are equal within one State but generally differ across States. MP vote value is uniform across both Houses. Larger population alone does not necessarily mean a larger MLA vote value; the number of elected Assembly seats also matters.

9. Election Administration and Disputes

  • The Election Commission of India conducts the presidential election under Article 324 and the governing election laws.
  • The Commission appoints a Returning Officer. By convention for successive presidential elections, the Secretaries-General of the Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha have served in rotation; this is not a constitutional rule.
  • Under Article 71, doubts and disputes arising out of or connected with the election are inquired into and decided by the Supreme Court, whose decision is final.
  • Under the 1952 Act, an election petition may be presented by a candidate, or by twenty or more electors joined together as petitioners.
[Correction] The rough notes said only "electors and contenders" may question transparency. The precise remedy is an election petition to the Supreme Court, and the statute requires either a candidate or at least twenty electors acting jointly. Ordinary political criticism is different from legally challenging the validity of the election.
[Correction] The Returning Officer is not invariably "Secretary of Lok Sabha / Rajya Sabha." The Election Commission makes the appointment for the election; the alternating parliamentary practice is a convention and uses the office of Secretary-General.

10. Key Facts for Prelims

  • Part V concerns the Union; Chapter I, Articles 52-78, concerns the Union Executive.
  • Parliament consists of the President, Rajya Sabha and Lok Sabha under Article 79.
  • President: Article 52; Union executive power: Article 53; election: Articles 54-55; qualifications: Article 58.
  • The President's term is five years under Article 56, but the incumbent continues until the successor enters office; Article 57 permits re-election without a constitutional term limit.
  • "De jure head" and "first citizen" are conventional descriptions, not constitutional expressions.
  • Article 58 requires Indian citizenship, age 35, Lok Sabha eligibility and absence of a disqualifying office of profit.
  • Under Article 60, the Chief Justice of India, or the senior-most available Supreme Court Judge in the Chief Justice's absence, administers the President's oath or affirmation.
  • The electoral college includes elected MPs and elected MLAs of States, Delhi and Puducherry.
  • Nominated MPs, nominated MLAs and all Legislative Council members are excluded.
  • The election uses proportional representation by STV and secret ballot.
  • Nomination requires 50 proposers and 50 seconders; the deposit is Rs. 15,000.
  • MLA vote value depends on State population and elected Assembly strength; MP vote value is uniform.
  • Article 55 presently uses 1971 Census figures until relevant figures of the first post-2026 census are published.
  • Article 71 gives presidential-election dispute jurisdiction to the Supreme Court.
  • A vacancy in the electoral college does not by itself invalidate the election.

11. Mains Points

  • Republican legitimacy with parliamentary government: indirect election gives the President a broad federal mandate while preserving the Council of Ministers' democratic responsibility to Lok Sabha.
  • Federal balance: weighted MLA votes account for population and Assembly strength, while a uniform MP value creates parity between the Union and States as collective units.
  • Neutral constitutional office: secret preferential voting and a geographically broad electoral college are designed to support an office representing the Union rather than one constituency.
  • Constitutional form and political reality: executive power is vested formally in the President, but Article 74 places its ordinary exercise within the cabinet system.
  • Judicial finality: Article 71 channels election disputes to the Supreme Court, limiting prolonged institutional uncertainty while preserving legal review.

13. 30-Second Revision

Structure: Executive implements, Legislature makes law, Judiciary adjudicates. Part V Chapter I = Union Executive, Arts. 52-78.
President: Head of State; executive power formally vested under Art. 53; normally acts on Art. 74 advice. "First citizen" and "de jure head" are not constitutional terms.
Term: Five years from entering office; resignation is addressed to the Vice-President; continues until the successor enters office; re-election is permitted without a constitutional term limit (Arts. 56-57).
Qualification: citizen + 35 + Lok Sabha qualified + no disqualifying office of profit (Art. 58).
Oath: Administered by the CJI, or the senior-most available Supreme Court Judge in the CJI's absence; preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and the law (Art. 60).
Electoral college: elected MPs + elected MLAs of States, Delhi and Puducherry; no nominated members or MLCs.
Method: indirect + PR-STV + secret ballot. Preferences transfer after exclusion of the lowest continuing candidate.
Vote value: MLA = population / elected MLAs / 1,000; MP = total MLA vote value / elected MPs. Census base presently 1971.
Law: 50 proposers + 50 seconders + Rs. 15,000 deposit. ECI appoints RO; Supreme Court decides election disputes under Art. 71.

14. Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

Source note: These questions are directly connected with today's topics. Wording is reproduced from UPSC Civil Services General Studies papers; the official UPSC archive is linked below.

Q1. UPSC CSE Prelims 2023, General Studies Paper I

Consider the following statements in respect of election to the President of India:

  1. The members nominated to either House of the Parliament or the Legislative Assemblies of States are also eligible to be included in the Electoral College.
  2. Higher the number of elective Assembly seats, higher is the value of vote of each MLA of that State.
  3. The value of vote of each MLA of Madhya Pradesh is greater than that of Kerala.
  4. The value of vote of each MLA of Puducherry is higher than that of Arunachal Pradesh because the ratio of total population to total number of elective seats in Puducherry is greater as compared to Arunachal Pradesh.

How many of the above statements are correct?

(a) Only one
(b) Only two
(c) Only three
(d) All four

Answer: (a) Only one.
Explanation: Statement 4 alone is correct. Nominated members are excluded. MLA vote value depends on the ratio of population to elected Assembly seats, so a higher number of seats does not by itself increase each MLA's value. Under the Article 55 calculation, Kerala's MLA vote value exceeds Madhya Pradesh's, while Puducherry's exceeds Arunachal Pradesh's.

Q2. UPSC CSE Prelims 2014, General Studies Paper I

Consider the following statements:

  1. The President shall make rules for the more convenient transaction of the business of the Government of India, and for the allocation among Ministers of the said business.
  2. All executive actions of the Government of India shall be expressed to be taken in the name of the Prime Minister.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only
(b) 2 only
(c) Both 1 and 2
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: (a) 1 only.
Explanation: Article 77(3) supports statement 1. Under Article 77(1), executive action is expressed to be taken in the name of the President, not the Prime Minister.

Q3. UPSC CSE Prelims 2013, General Studies Paper I

Consider the following statements:

  1. The Council of Ministers in the Centre shall be collectively responsible to the Parliament.
  2. The Union Ministers shall hold the office during the pleasure of the President of India.
  3. The Prime Minister shall communicate to the President about the proposals for legislation.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

Answer: (b) 2 and 3 only.
Explanation: Under Article 75(3), the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible specifically to the House of the People, not Parliament as a whole. Article 75(2) provides pleasure tenure, and Article 78 requires the Prime Minister to communicate Council decisions and proposals for legislation to the President.
