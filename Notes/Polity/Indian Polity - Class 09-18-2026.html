Topics: Union Council of Ministers; ministerial responsibility and ranks; Attorney General of India; State executive; Governor's appointment, tenure, conditions, vacancies and immunity.

Labels used: [Correction] explains a substantive correction. [Additional] marks relevant supplementary material. [Verification Required] flags an unresolved claim. [Not mentioned in the Constitution] distinguishes textbook classifications from constitutional wording.

1. Union Council of Ministers

[Additional] Constitutional setting: Part V, Chapter I deals with the Union executive. Article 74 provides for a Council of Ministers (CoM), with the Prime Minister at its head, to aid and advise the President. Article 75 governs appointment, size, responsibility, oaths, membership and remuneration.

1.1 Article 75: Clause-by-Clause

75(1A)The total number of ministers, including the PM, cannot exceed 15% of the total membership of Lok Sabha.Counts the whole Council, not only Cabinet ministers. The denominator is Lok Sabha, not both Houses together.
[Additional] 75(1B)A party member disqualified from either House under paragraph 2 of the Tenth Schedule is also barred from appointment as a minister for the specified period.The bar lasts until the original legislative term would expire or the person is declared elected again before that expiry, whichever is earlier.

[Correction] The rough reference 71(1A) must be 75(1A). The 91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003 inserted the Union and State ministerial-size provisions; the official Constitution records their commencement as 1 January 2004. The Act's title year remains 2003.
[Correction] The six-month rule does not restrict ministers to Lok Sabha membership or dispense with eligibility. It applies to six consecutive months without membership of either House, including a period after an existing minister loses membership. Mere eligibility to vote is not enough to bypass legislative qualifications or disqualifications.
[Correction] For remuneration, retain the original reference to the Second Schedule but qualify it with Article 75(6)'s words until Parliament so determines. Parliamentary law, not a presidential salary decision, governs Union ministers' salaries and allowances.

1.2 Size of the Council: Union, States and Delhi

State15% of that State's Legislative Assembly membership, including the CM, with a proviso prescribing a minimum of 12 ministers, including the CM.Article 164(1A); the same amendment.

Exam Point: 15% - 15% with minimum 12 - 10% is the Union-State-Delhi comparison. Count the PM/CM within the limit, not in addition to it. At the Union there is no fixed actual number or numerical floor, but Article 74 still requires a Council of Ministers.

[Additional] The State minimum is a real exception for small Assemblies: in a hypothetical 60-member Assembly, 15% is 9, but the 12-minister proviso governs. Do not carry this State minimum into Delhi or the Union. Delhi's Article 239AA was inserted by the 69th Amendment Act, 1991, not the 91st Amendment.

1.3 Collective and Individual Responsibility

  • Collective responsibility, Article 75(3): all ministers stand together as one government before Lok Sabha. This includes ministers who belong to Rajya Sabha.
  • "Sink or swim together": loss of Lok Sabha's confidence affects the entire Council, not just the minister whose department triggered the dispute.
  • [Additional] Cabinet solidarity: ministers publicly support the government's settled decisions; a minister unwilling to do so is expected to resign.
  • Individual responsibility, Article 75(2): a minister may be removed individually by the President on the PM's advice without requiring the whole Council to resign.

[Correction] "Ministers are appointed because they have a majority" means the government must command Lok Sabha's confidence; every minister need not belong to a party with a majority on its own. A coalition or minority ministry with outside support is possible. Loss of confidence ends the mandate to govern, but does not require an executive vacuum during the transition.
[Not mentioned in the Constitution] "Sink or swim together", "Cabinet solidarity", "caretaker government" and "individual responsibility" are explanatory or conventional expressions. Article 75(3) expressly says "collectively responsible"; Article 75(2) expresses the pleasure rule. [Additional] "No-confidence motion" is a parliamentary-procedure term, not wording in the Constitution; at the Union level it operates in Lok Sabha.

1.4 Article 88: Participation Is Not a Voting Right

Every Union minister and the Attorney General of India may speak and otherwise participate in the proceedings of either House, a joint sitting, and a committee of Parliament of which the person may be named a member. Article 88 does not itself confer a vote.

PersonParticipation under Article 88Voting position
Minister who is a Lok Sabha MPMay participate in both Houses.Votes in Lok Sabha as an MP, not in Rajya Sabha merely as a minister.
Minister who is a Rajya Sabha MPMay participate in both Houses.Votes in Rajya Sabha as an MP, not in Lok Sabha merely as a minister.
Minister who is not yet an MPMay participate during the permissible non-member period.No vote in either House on the strength of ministerial office.
Attorney GeneralHas the Article 88 participation rights.No vote by virtue of that office or Article 88.
[Correction] "Become a part of the parliamentary committee" is not an automatic entitlement to sit on every committee: Article 88 says a committee of which the person may be named a member, subject to the relevant rules. Voting here derives from House membership, not from Article 88. The rough generalisation "only a member can vote" also has a separate constitutional exception: the Rajya Sabha Chairman's casting vote under Article 100(1), despite not being a member.

1.5 What Does "No Legal Responsibility" Mean?

[Not mentioned in the Constitution] "Legal responsibility of ministers", in this comparative textbook usage, refers to the traditional British explanation that responsible ministers act for the Crown and bear responsibility, often illustrated through ministerial countersignature and the maxim "the King can do no wrong".

India has no general constitutional requirement that every presidential act must be countersigned by a minister to become valid. Article 77 instead provides for executive action in the President's name and authentication under prescribed rules. Article 74(2) protects inquiry into the advice tendered to the President; it does not make governmental action universally immune from review.

[Correction] "Ministers have no legal responsibility because the President has Article 361 immunity" gives the wrong explanation and suggests an immunity ministers do not possess. Article 361 protects the President and Governors, not ministers. Ministers remain subject to applicable civil and criminal law, and unlawful governmental action can be challenged. The relevant contrast is absence of a universal countersignature rule, not absence of legal accountability. The rough claim about every UK document should not be treated as an exceptionless modern rule.

2. Classification of Ministers

[Not mentioned in the Constitution] The Constitution does not prescribe this complete three-tier/four-category classification. It is an administrative and parliamentary arrangement. The two versions in the rough notes are reconciled by separating Ministers of State with independent charge from other Ministers of State.
CategoryRoleRelationship with the Cabinet
Cabinet MinisterA senior minister, normally heading an important ministry; takes part in the government's principal collective policy decisions.A member of the Cabinet, the smaller decision-making body within the CoM.
Minister of State (Independent Charge)Heads an assigned ministry/department without a Cabinet minister placed over that charge.Not a Cabinet member merely because the charge is independent; may be invited when relevant business is considered.
Minister of StateAssists a Cabinet minister and handles responsibilities allocated within the ministry.Not ordinarily a Cabinet member; participation in a meeting may be by invitation.
Deputy MinisterA junior minister assisting senior ministers with assigned administrative, parliamentary and other work; does not head a ministry independently.Not a Cabinet member by virtue of this rank. This category need not be used in every ministry.
[Correction] Do not turn "classification not in the Constitution" into "Cabinet is nowhere mentioned". Article 352(3) expressly defines the Union Cabinet as the Council consisting of the PM and other ministers of Cabinet rank appointed under Article 75. [Additional] This provision, introduced by the 44th Amendment Act, 1978, requires the Cabinet's decision supporting a National Emergency proclamation to be communicated to the President in writing.

[Additional] Council versus Cabinet: the CoM is the larger body covering all ministerial categories; the Cabinet is its smaller policy-making core. All ministers, not just Cabinet members, fall within the numerical ceiling and collective-responsibility rule. Independent charge does not mean independence from the PM or from collective responsibility.

3. Attorney General of India

Article 76 creates the office of the Attorney-General for India (AG), the Union government's principal legal adviser and law officer. The President appoints the AG. [Not mentioned in the Constitution] "First law officer" and "highest law officer" are standard descriptions, not the title used in Article 76.

3.1 Qualifications: Article 76(1) Read with Article 124(3)

The person must be qualified for appointment as a Supreme Court judge: an Indian citizen satisfying any one of these professional alternatives:

RouteRequirement
Judicial experienceAt least five years as a judge of a High Court, or of two or more High Courts in succession.
AdvocacyAt least ten years as an advocate of a High Court, or of two or more High Courts in succession.
Juristic distinctionA distinguished jurist in the opinion of the President.
[Correction] "High Court judge" alone omits the required five years. The alternatives are 5 years as judge OR 10 years as advocate OR distinguished jurist, together with citizenship; they are not cumulative requirements. The person need not already be a Supreme Court judge.

3.2 Tenure, Remuneration and Service Conditions

  • Article 76(4): the AG holds office during the President's pleasure and receives remuneration determined by the President.
  • The Constitution prescribes no fixed numerical tenure or special parliamentary removal procedure for the AG.
  • The Law Officers (Conditions of Service) Rules, 1987 govern remuneration, duties and service conditions. Rule 3 provides a three-year term, with reappointment for a further term not exceeding three years, subject to its exceptions and earlier-termination provisions.
  • [Additional] Rule 3 allows termination on three months' written notice by either side; an ASG post created for less than three years carries that shorter period. The rules must be read with the AG's constitutional pleasure tenure and the applicable appointment order.
[Correction] Separate the two levels: no fixed tenure in Article 76 does not mean there are no service rules or appointment terms. Conversely, the Rules' three-year term is not a guaranteed constitutional tenure. "All terms and removal are decided by the President" is shorthand for a constitutional office operating under Article 76, service rules and appointment conditions.

[Additional] The Constitution does not require the AG to resign automatically whenever the government that appointed the AG resigns. Any such convention must not be substituted for Article 76(4).

3.3 Functions and Rights

  1. Legal advice: advise the Government of India on legal matters referred by the President: Article 76(2).
  2. Representation in court: appear when required for the Government of India in the Supreme Court or High Courts, including cases in which it is a party or otherwise interested: Rule 5(b).
  3. Presidential references: represent the Government of India in a reference made by the President to the Supreme Court under Article 143: Rule 5(c).
  4. Other legal duties: perform legal duties assigned by the President and functions conferred by the Constitution or other law: Article 76(2).
  5. [Additional] Right of audience: in performing these duties, the AG has a right of audience in all courts in India: Article 76(3).
  6. Parliamentary participation: Article 88 gives the speaking/participation rights described in section 1.4, without a vote by virtue of that Article.
[Correction] The AG does not independently "seek advice/reference under Article 143". The President makes the reference; the Supreme Court gives its advisory opinion within Article 143's framework; the AG represents the Union in those proceedings.

3.5 Solicitor General and Additional Solicitors General

The Solicitor General (SG) and Additional Solicitors General (ASGs) assist with the Union's legal advice and litigation. They are law officers covered by the 1987 Rules, including the term and service-condition framework.

[Not mentioned in the Constitution] The offices of Solicitor General and Additional Solicitor General are not created or named by the Constitution. Contrast the expressly constitutional office of Attorney General under Article 76.
[Correction] The rough notation "Additional Solicitor Generals (4)" is not a fixed constitutional or Rule 2 numerical limit. The number depends on sanctioned posts and appointments; no current headcount is asserted here. [Additional] SG/ASG office alone does not carry the AG's Article 88 parliamentary participation right.

4. State Executive: Constitutional Map

[Correction] The State executive is in Part VI, Chapter II, Articles 153-167, not Part V. Part V concerns the Union; Part VI as a whole covers the States and is wider than just these executive Articles.

The main constitutional offices are the Governor, the Chief Minister and Council of Ministers, and the Advocate General.

[Additional] Article map:

ArticlesSubject
153-162Governor: office, executive power, appointment, tenure, qualifications, conditions, oath, contingencies, clemency and extent of State executive power.
163-164Council of Ministers headed by the CM: aid and advice, appointment and related ministerial provisions.
165Advocate General for the State.
166-167Conduct of State government business and the CM's duties towards the Governor.

The Governor is the constitutional head of the State, broadly corresponding to the President at the Union level. [Additional] Article 154 vests State executive power in the Governor. Under Article 163 the Governor ordinarily acts on the State Council's advice, except in constitutionally authorised discretionary matters; the two offices' powers are not identical.

5. Governor: Appointment, Oath and Tenure

5.1 Appointment and Multiple Charges

  • Article 153: there shall be a Governor for each State. Its proviso allows the same person to be Governor of two or more States.
  • Article 155: appointment is by the President, by warrant under the President's hand and seal.
  • The President acts within Article 74, on the Union Council of Ministers' advice headed by the PM. [Additional] Advice may be returned for reconsideration once, but the reconsidered advice is binding.
  • The 7th Amendment Act, 1956 added Article 153's multiple-State proviso, effective 1 November 1956.
  • Article 239(2): the President may also appoint a State Governor as administrator of an adjoining Union Territory. In that role the Governor acts independently of the State Council of Ministers.
[Correction] Article 153 establishes the office; Article 155 governs appointment. The State-plus-UT arrangement is authorised by Article 239(2), not the Article 153 proviso. Article 239 was also substituted through the 7th Amendment; retain the important word adjoining.

5.2 Constitutional Qualifications: Article 157

  1. Citizen of India.
  2. Completed 35 years of age.

[Additional] Article 157 does not require the appointee to be a resident of the State, a legislator, a judge or a member of a particular political party. The Article 158 conditions of office apply separately.

5.3 Sarkaria Commission: Recommendations, Not Extra Eligibility Clauses

The Sarkaria Commission on Centre-State Relations, constituted in 1983, submitted its report in 1988. Chapter IV recommends the following selection safeguards:

  • A person from outside the State.
  • A detached figure, not too closely connected with the State's local politics and not heavily involved in politics, particularly in the recent past.
  • Effective consultation with the State CM; the Commission recommended prescribing the consultation procedure through an amendment of Article 155.
  • [Additional] The person should be eminent in some walk of life: paragraph 4.16.01. The CM-consultation recommendation is in paragraph 4.16.03.
[Correction] "Not an active politician" summarises the recommendation; it is not a constitutional lifetime ban on anyone with political experience. These recommendations are distinct from Article 157's legal qualifications, and consultation does not give the CM a constitutional veto.

The rough observation that appointments have departed from these standards has a verified basis: in Rameshwar Prasad v. Union of India (2006), the Supreme Court criticised the failure to observe the recommended selection criteria. Departure from a recommendation is not automatically violation of Article 157. Avoid treating the rough statement about repeated violations of every recommendation as a separately proved empirical finding.

5.4 Oath: Article 159

  • Every Governor and every person discharging the Governor's functions must take the oath/affirmation before entering upon the office.
  • It is made before the Chief Justice of the High Court exercising jurisdiction over the State, or, in the Chief Justice's absence, the senior-most available judge of that High Court.
  • [Additional] The oath includes faithfully executing the office, preserving, protecting and defending the Constitution and the law, and serving the people's well-being.
  • [Additional] Its form is in Article 159 itself, not the Third Schedule. Do not add the Union minister's separate oath of secrecy to this office.
Question from the rough notes: Who administers the oath when one person governs multiple States?
Apply Article 159 to each State office. The person takes the State-specific oath before the Chief Justice of the High Court having jurisdiction over that State, or the prescribed senior-most available judge in the Chief Justice's absence. If the States share a common High Court, the same Chief Justice may administer the respective oaths. There is no special rule transferring this task to the President or the Chief Justice of India merely because the person has multiple charges.

5.5 Term, Resignation and Removal: Article 156

ClausePosition
156(1)Holds office during the pleasure of the President.
156(2)May resign by a signed writing addressed to the President.
156(3)Ordinary term: five years from entering office, subject to the preceding provisions.
156(3) provisoContinues after the term expires until the successor enters office.

A Governor may leave earlier through resignation, removal, death or appointment/transfer to another State. [Additional] The Constitution provides no presidential-style impeachment process for a Governor and no constitutional limit on reappointment.

[Additional] B. P. Singhal v. Union of India, 7 May 2010: withdrawal of presidential pleasure is not an arbitrary power. The President need not communicate reasons or give a prior show-cause opportunity, but removal must rest on valid grounds, not whim; it is subject to limited judicial review.
[Correction] "Pleasure is more important than five years" means there is no guaranteed five-year tenure. It does not mean arbitrary removal is lawful. Likewise, normal term expiry does not itself create an unfilled office: the holdover proviso maintains continuity.

6. Governor: Conditions of Office and Vacancies

6.1 Conditions under Article 158

Serving as Governor of more than one State under Article 153, or as administrator of an adjoining UT under Article 239(2), is constitutionally authorised; it is not invalid merely because Article 158(2) forbids another office of profit.

[Correction] The rough lines alternate between Parliament and the President as the salary authority. Parliament determines the entitlements by law; the President determines allocation among States under Article 158(3A) and has rule-making functions under the governing Act. These are different powers. Article 158(3A)'s express State-sharing formula should not be extended automatically to State-plus-UT charges.

6.2 Salary Funding and Post-Retirement Benefits

  • Article 202(3)(a): the Governor's emoluments and allowances, and other expenditure relating to the office, are charged on the Consolidated Fund of the State.
  • [Additional] Article 203(1): charged expenditure may be discussed in the State legislature but is not submitted to the Assembly's vote.
  • The governing statute is the Governors (Emoluments, Allowances and Privileges) Act, 1982, with its rules.
  • [Additional] Section 12A: an ex-Governor is entitled, subject to rules, to one Personal Assistant's secretarial assistance on a reimbursement basis for the remainder of life. The benefit is unavailable during specified periods of reappointment as Governor, membership of Parliament/State legislature or holding another government office of profit.
[Verification Required] The rough claim "the Governor's post-retirement benefits come from the Consolidated Fund of India" is too broad to certify from the sources checked. Article 202 verifies the serving Governor's State-funded expenditure, while Section 12A verifies a specific ex-Governor entitlement; neither alone proves that all retirement benefits are charged on the Union fund. Check the applicable benefit rule and budget head before using that blanket funding statement in an answer.

7. Governor: Immunity and Judicial Review

7.1 Article 361: Four Separate Protections

ClauseProtectionImportant limit
361(1)The Governor is not personally answerable to a court for official powers/duties or acts done or purportedly done in their exercise.Does not prevent appropriate proceedings against the Union or State government or review of the challenged action.
361(2)No criminal proceedings whatsoever may be instituted or continued against the Governor in any court during the term.Covers personal conduct too; this is a time-bound procedural protection, not a declaration that an offence becomes lawful.
[Additional] 361(3)No court may issue process for the Governor's arrest or imprisonment during the term.Do not confuse the bar on court process with an acquittal or permanent immunity from liability.
361(4)A civil proceeding claiming relief against the Governor for an act in a personal capacity may be instituted during the term only after two months have expired following the prescribed written notice.Applies to personal acts before or after entering office. It is not the notice rule for a challenge to an official governmental action.

[Additional] The civil notice must state the nature of proceedings, cause of action, claimant's name/description/residence and relief claimed, and be delivered to the Governor or left at the office.

[Correction] Replace "complete immunity for official acts" with personal non-answerability for official acts, while preserving review of their legality. The criminal protection lasts during the term and includes a bar on continuing proceedings; the two-month civil notice concerns personal-capacity relief. These are distinct rules.

7.2 Rameshwar Prasad v. Union of India (2006)

Detailed judgment: 24 January 2006; (2006) 2 SCC 1. The case concerned dissolution of the Bihar Legislative Assembly following the Governor's reports. The Court distinguished immunity of the officeholder from reviewability of the action.

  • The Governor's personal immunity under Article 361(1) remains complete, including when official action is challenged as mala fide.
  • A court can nevertheless examine the challenged action for mala fides (bad faith), lack of relevant material or ultra vires exercise (beyond lawful power).
  • The Union or State government defending the action must meet the challenge; immunity does not require the court to accept an unlawful act.
  • [Additional] The Court held the dissolution unconstitutional but did not restore the Assembly, given the election process already underway. The operative order was made on 7 October 2005; the detailed reasons followed in January 2006.
[Correction] The rough case summary should not imply that alleging mala fides removes the Governor's personal immunity. The action can be scrutinised without making the Governor personally answerable. This distinction is the central exam point.

Key Facts for Prelims

  • 75(1): President appoints PM; other ministers on PM's advice.
  • 75(1A): Union CoM, including PM, at most 15% of Lok Sabha; no numerical minimum.
  • 164(1A): State CoM, including CM, 15% of Assembly, subject to minimum 12; Delhi: 10%, Article 239AA(4).
  • 75(3): collective responsibility to Lok Sabha. 75(4): office and secrecy oaths, Third Schedule. 75(5): six consecutive months.
  • 76: AG appointed by President; Supreme Court judge qualifications; pleasure tenure; remuneration determined by President.
  • 124(3): Indian citizen + 5-year HC judge / 10-year HC advocate / distinguished jurist.
  • 88: minister/AG participation rights; no vote by virtue of Article 88. SG/ASG not covered merely by holding those offices.
  • 352(3): Union Cabinet is expressly mentioned; the complete ministerial-rank classification is not prescribed.
  • Part VI, Chapter II: State executive, 153-167. Governor: 153 office; 155 appointment; 156 tenure; 157 qualifications; 158 conditions; 159 oath; 160 contingencies.
  • Governor: Indian citizen, 35 years; ordinary five-year term, subject to pleasure and holdover.
  • 158(3A): President apportions shared Governor's emoluments among States. 202(3)(a): serving office expenditure charged on State Consolidated Fund.
  • 361: personal official-act immunity; no court criminal proceedings/arrest process during tenure; personal civil suit requires two months' notice.

Mains Points

[Additional] Analytical applications of the provisions above:

  • Responsible government: Article 75(3) connects executive power to the elected House. Collective responsibility creates a united government; individual removal supports ministerial discipline.
  • Size versus effectiveness: the 91st Amendment sets an outer limit, not an ideal team size. Assess workload, ministerial competence, representation, coordination and the PM's ability to lead a team.
  • Constitutional law versus convention: distinguish enforceable Article 157 qualifications from Sarkaria selection recommendations, and the Article 352 Cabinet definition from the conventional rank hierarchy.
  • Government lawyer and public duty: the AG combines advice and litigation. Constitutional standing, professional independence and conflict restrictions should support legality, not merely defend every executive preference.
  • Governor and federal trust: an impartial appointment process, meaningful consultation and restrained use of powers reduce Centre-State friction. Appointment by the Union should not turn a constitutional head into a partisan actor.
  • Immunity with accountability: Rameshwar Prasad separates personal protection from judicial scrutiny of action; B. P. Singhal limits arbitrary removal. Neither constitutional dignity nor the pleasure doctrine extinguishes the rule of law.

PYQ 1. UPSC CSE Prelims 2022, General Studies Paper I

Consider the following statements:

  1. The Constitution of India classifies the ministers into four ranks viz. Cabinet Minister, Minister of State with Independent Charge, Minister of State and Deputy Minister.
  2. The total number of ministers in the Union Government, including the Prime Minister, shall not exceed 15 percent of the total number of members in the Lok Sabha.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Correct answer: (b) 2 only. Article 75(1A) prescribes the 15% ceiling, including the PM. The Constitution does not prescribe the four-rank classification; Article 352(3)'s mention of Cabinet does not make statement 1 correct.

Question source: Rau's IAS: 2022 Polity PYQs.

PYQ 2. UPSC CSE Prelims 2022, General Studies Paper I

  1. Attorney General of India and Solicitor General of India are the only officers of the Government who are allowed to participate in the meetings of the Parliament of India.
  2. According to the Constitution of India, the Attorney General of India submits his resignation when the Government which appointed him resigns.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Correct answer: (d) Neither 1 nor 2. Article 88 covers ministers and the AG, not the SG merely by virtue of that office. Article 76(4) provides pleasure tenure, not automatic constitutional resignation when the government changes.

PYQ 5. UPSC CSE Prelims 2015, General Studies Paper I

There is a Parliamentary System of Government in India because the

  1. Lok Sabha is elected directly by the people
  2. Parliament can amend the Constitution
  3. Rajya Sabha cannot be dissolved
  4. Council of Ministers is responsible to the Lok Sabha

Correct answer: (d) Council of Ministers is responsible to the Lok Sabha. Article 75(3) supplies the defining relationship of executive responsibility to the elected House. The other institutional features do not by themselves establish parliamentary government.

PYQ 6. UPSC CSE Prelims 2014, General Studies Paper I

Consider the following statements regarding a No-Confidence Motion in India:

  1. There is no mention of a No-Confidence Motion in the Constitution of India.
  2. A Motion of No-Confidence can be introduced in the Lok Sabha only.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Correct answer: (c) Both 1 and 2. The expression is [Not mentioned in the Constitution]; parliamentary procedure gives effect to Article 75(3). PYQ 7. UPSC CSE Mains 2019, General Studies Paper II

"The Attorney-General is the chief legal adviser and lawyer of the Government of India." Discuss.

Suggested answer outline: Article 76 creates a constitutional legal office, filled by the President from persons qualified for Supreme Court judgeship. Its advisory role is to guide the Union on referred legal questions and assigned legal duties. Its litigation role includes representing the Union in the Supreme Court/High Courts and in Presidential references under Article 143; Article 76(3) provides audience in all courts. Article 88 permits parliamentary participation without an office-based vote. Conclude that professional independence, conflict restrictions and assistance from the SG/ASGs enable sound legal representation while protecting constitutional legality.

Sources: Official UPSC 2019 GS II paper; Rau's IAS: 2019 Mains transcription.

PYQ 8. UPSC CSE Mains 2014, General Studies Paper II

The size of the cabinet should be as big as governmental work justifies and as big as the Prime Minister can manage as a team. How far the efficacy of a government then is inversely related to the size of the cabinet? Discuss.

Suggested answer outline: Begin with the PM-led executive under Articles 74-75 and distinguish the Cabinet from the full CoM. Article 75(1A), inserted by the 91st Amendment, caps the whole Council, not a separate Cabinet-only number. Excessive size can fragment portfolios, increase coordination costs and encourage patronage. Too few ministers can overload individuals and neglect specialised work. Effectiveness therefore depends on appropriate size, clear responsibilities, competence, teamwork and parliamentary accountability, not a simple inverse mathematical relationship. Link the conclusion to collective responsibility under Article 75(3).

Question source: 2014 GS II: full Cabinet-size question.
