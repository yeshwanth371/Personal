1. Steps Taken for Financial Inclusion (continued)

1.A Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (PMJDY)

PMJDY is the National Mission for Financial Inclusion. Its objective is to promote bank-account access for all: any individual with no bank account can open a zero-balance savings account (Basic Savings Bank Deposit account) with no extra charges. It is implemented by the Department of Financial Services (DFS), Ministry of Finance.

  • Account holders are entitled to insurance support (see corrected details below).
  • The scheme allows eligible account holders an overdraft (OD) of up to Rs 10,000 - that is, they can withdraw up to Rs 10,000 more than their balance.
  • Business Correspondents (Bank Mitras) make it easier for account holders to operate their accounts in places without a branch; together with Aadhaar and mobile phones this forms the JAM trinity.
  • About 56% of PMJDY account holders are women.

[Correction] PMJDY was announced on 15 August 2014 and launched on 28 August 2014 (not 2015). It completed 12 years on 28 August 2026.

[Correction] The insurance point mixes two schemes. PMJDY itself gives a free accident insurance cover through the RuPay debit card - Rs 1 lakh, raised to Rs 2 lakh for accounts opened after 28 August 2018 - and no premium is paid. "Life insurance of Rs 2 lakh for a premium" is a separate scheme, PM Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana (PMJJBY), whose premium is Rs 436 a year (not Rs 463). PMJDY accounts are the platform for enrolling in PMJJBY, PMSBY and APY (see Section 1.E). [Additional] Early accounts (opened in the first phase, up to January 2015) also carried a life cover of Rs 30,000, subject to conditions.

[Correction] Business Correspondents are engaged by banks under RBI's BC model (permitted since 2006), not appointed by the government. The JAM trinity (Jan Dhan - Aadhaar - Mobile) was named in the Economic Survey 2014-15; it is the delivery rail for Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT), not only a way to operate accounts. [Additional] The OD is not automatic: it is available to eligible holders (account operated satisfactorily, age 18-65); up to Rs 2,000 is available without conditions.

[Correction] Update the data. As on 19 August 2026: 59.09 crore accounts (not 56 crore); deposits Rs 3,16,514 crore (about Rs 3.17 lakh crore) (not Rs 2.9 lakh crore); 55.7% (32.92 crore) women; 77.8% (45.95 crore) in rural and semi-urban areas; 41.29 crore RuPay cards; average deposit Rs 5,356 per account (PIB, 27 August 2026). The DFS progress report shows 59.31 crore accounts and Rs 3.20 lakh crore by 16 September 2026 (see 26 September notes).

[Correction] "Bank account holders rose from 19% of the population in 2015 to 83% by 2026" could not be verified from any official or World Bank source - [Verification Required]. The standard measure is the World Bank Global Findex share of adults (15+) with an account: about 53% in 2014, 80% in 2017, 78% in 2021 and about 89% in 2025 (as cited by PIB, 12 August 2026). Use these, and note that the base is adults, not total population. "Assured savings bank account" should read basic savings bank account.

[Additional] Benefits of PMJDY

  1. Financial identity for the excluded: about 78% of accounts are rural/semi-urban and 56% belong to women - groups historically outside formal banking.
  2. DBT and reduced leakage: subsidies, scholarships, pensions, PM-KISAN and wages reach beneficiaries directly; duplicate and ghost beneficiaries are weeded out.
  3. Safe saving habit: deposits are protected by DICGC insurance (Rs 5 lakh per depositor per bank) and earn interest; household saving moves from cash and gold into financial saving that banks can lend.
  4. Gateway to risk protection: accident cover with RuPay; low-cost PMJJBY, PMSBY and APY enrolment by auto-debit.
  5. Credit history: account transactions build a record that helps access OD, MUDRA and SHG loans, reducing dependence on moneylenders.
  6. Women's empowerment: independent accounts give women control over transfers - for example, Rs 500 a month for three months was credited to women PMJDY accounts under PM Garib Kalyan Yojana during the COVID-19 lockdown (April-June 2020).
  7. Digital payments: RuPay cards and bank accounts are the base on which UPI and Aadhaar-enabled Payment System (AePS) operate.
  8. Shock absorber: accounts allowed quick cash transfers during COVID-19 without physical queues.

[Additional] Challenges of PMJDY

  1. Dormant / inoperative accounts: a significant share of accounts is not operated for two years; some are duplicates opened under target pressure [Verification Required: latest official inoperative share].
  2. Low balances and low usage: the average deposit (Rs 5,356) is small; credit, OD and insurance usage remain low - access has outpaced usage (the RBI FI-Index gives Usage the highest weight, 45%).
  3. Last-mile weaknesses: Business Correspondents earn low commissions, face cash-handling risks and connectivity problems; biometric failures exclude the elderly and manual workers.
  4. Financial and digital literacy gaps: limited understanding of OD, insurance claims and cyber safety; risk of fraud and "mule accounts" misused for cyber crime.
  5. Gender gap in control: some women's accounts are operated by male relatives.
  6. Bank costs: zero-balance accounts are costly for public sector banks, which carry most PMJDY accounts.

Exam point: PMJDY solved access (supply side). Inclusive growth needs usage and quality - credit, insurance, pension and literacy. Cause-effect: account → DBT → less leakage + more savings → credit history → enterprise and risk protection → lower vulnerability.

1.B Kisan Credit Card (KCC)

KCC was introduced in 1998 with the objective of reducing farmers' dependence on moneylenders. It is a revolving credit limit through banks for crop production (seeds, fertiliser, pesticides), post-harvest and marketing expenses, household consumption needs, working capital for farm assets and allied activities.

  • KCC has been extended to animal husbandry (including dairy) and fisheries farmers (2018-19).
  • Small and marginal farmers hold about 76% of agricultural credit accounts under the scheme.

[Correction] Two limits are confused. (i) The loan limit eligible for interest subvention under the Modified Interest Subvention Scheme (MISS) was raised from Rs 3 lakh to Rs 5 lakh (Budget 2025-26). (ii) Collateral-free lending is only up to Rs 2 lakh per borrower (raised from Rs 1.6 lakh with effect from 1 January 2025) - not up to Rs 5 lakh.

[Correction] KCC loans are not interest-free. Short-term crop loans carry 7% interest; an extra 3% Prompt Repayment Incentive for timely repayment makes the effective rate 4% a year. [Additional] Interest is not charged for up to one year on restructured loans after natural calamities (extendable to five years for severe disasters); some States add their own subvention to make crop loans zero-interest [Verification Required: State-specific schemes].

[Correction] Data and implementation. 7.72 crore KCCs are operative with outstanding loans of about Rs 10.2 lakh crore (PIB, 11 March 2026) - consistent with "7.7 crore". For allied sectors, 39.22 lakh animal-husbandry and 4.82 lakh fisheries KCC applications have been sanctioned (PIB, 11 March 2026); the class figure of "30 lakh dairy farmers" could not be matched to an official series - [Verification Required]. KCC is delivered by banks (commercial, regional rural and cooperative) under RBI/NABARD guidelines; the Department of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare funds MISS and monitors through the Kisan Rin Portal (September 2023). "Diary" = dairy.

1.D Pradhan Mantri MUDRA Yojana (PMMY)

Objective: to provide assured, collateral-free credit to non-corporate, non-farm micro and small enterprises (manufacturing, trading, services, and activities allied to agriculture such as dairy, poultry and bee-keeping). Loans are given by banks, regional rural banks, small finance banks, NBFCs and MFIs; MUDRA (Micro Units Development and Refinance Agency) Ltd refinances them.

1.E Insurance Schemes of the Government

Concepts of insurance penetration and density, and PMFBY, are in the 28 September notes, Section 1.2.

1. Pradhan Mantri Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana (PMJJBY) - life cover

A subsidised (low-premium) life insurance scheme. The beneficiary pays a small annual premium for a life cover of Rs 2 lakh on death due to any cause.

[Correction] Premium is Rs 436 a year (not Rs 463; revised from Rs 330 from 1 June 2022). Eligibility is age 18-50 with a bank or post office account and consent to auto-debit (cover up to age 55). Having another life policy does not bar enrolment - "those without any other life insurance" is not a condition. Launched 9 May 2015; Ministry of Finance (DFS). Enrolments: 27.78 crore gross (30 June 2026).

2. Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana (PMSBY) - accident cover

A subsidised accident insurance scheme. The beneficiary pays Rs 20 a year for cover of Rs 2 lakh on accidental death or total permanent disability, and Rs 1 lakh on partial permanent disability (loss of one limb or one eye).

[Correction] The correct name is Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana ("PM Jeevan Suraksha Yojana" does not exist). Age 18-70; premium Rs 20 (raised from Rs 12 from 1 June 2022). PMSBY covers persons only - there is no compensation for damage to assets. Asset losses are covered by general insurance; crop losses by PMFBY. Total permanent disability includes loss of both eyes, both hands or feet, or one eye and one hand/foot. Enrolments: 58.66 crore gross (30 June 2026).

3. Ayushman Bharat - Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (AB PM-JAY) - health cover

A free (fully government-funded) health insurance/assurance scheme under Ayushman Bharat, launched in 2018-19. It covers the bottom 40% of families and, since 2024, all senior citizens aged 70 and above, irrespective of income. Beneficiaries get cashless treatment of up to Rs 5 lakh per family per year for secondary and tertiary hospitalisation.

[Correction] Spelling: "Jan Arogya", "beneficiaries". Launched 23 September 2018; Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, implemented by the National Health Authority. Coverage is about 12 crore families (about 55 crore people), identified from SECC 2011 and State lists - "13 crore families" should be checked against the latest State additions [Verification Required]. The 70+ extension (Ayushman Vay Vandana card) began on 29 October 2024, not 2025; older members of already-covered families get an additional top-up of Rs 5 lakh for themselves.

[Correction] Update the data: Ayushman cards 43.52 crore (28 February 2026), not 24 crore. "53 crore people treated" is wrong - the official measure is hospital admissions: 12.69 crore admissions worth Rs 1.92 lakh crore authorised as on 30 June 2026 (PIB, 4 August 2026). Empanelled hospitals: about 37,400 public and private (PIB, August 2026), not 31,000; the class split "20,000 government / 11,000 private" is outdated - [Verification Required] for the current split. [Additional] PM-JAY does not cover outpatient (OPD) or primary care; primary care is the other pillar of Ayushman Bharat - Ayushman Arogya Mandirs (formerly Health and Wellness Centres). Benefits are portable across States, with no cap on family size or age.

1.F Pension Schemes in India

More schemes (PM-SYM, NPS, UPS, IGNOAPS) and the contributory vs non-contributory distinction are in the 28 September notes, Section 1.3.

1. Atal Pension Yojana (APY)

A contributory pension scheme with a guaranteed minimum pension of Rs 1,000 to Rs 5,000 a month after age 60, mainly for unorganised-sector workers who are not covered by any other pension fund.

[Correction] APY is not limited to non-agricultural workers - any citizen aged 18-40 with a savings account can join (farmers included). Since 1 October 2022, income-tax payers are not eligible. Contribution depends on entry age and chosen pension. Launched 9 May 2015 (with PMJJBY and PMSBY); administered by the Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA) under DFS. Subscribers: 9.29 crore (30 June 2026), about 49% women.

2. Pradhan Mantri Kisan Maan-dhan Yojana (PM-KMY)

All small and marginal farmers with no other pension support are eligible. Farmers contribute Rs 55 to Rs 200 a month depending on entry age, and can join between 18 and 40 years. After 60 years, they receive Rs 3,000 a month as pension.

[Correction] Eligibility is limited to small and marginal farmers (cultivable land up to 2 hectares), not "all farmers". The Centre matches the farmer's contribution equally (Rs 55-200). Launched 12 September 2019; Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare; LIC is the pension fund manager.

[Correction] The death-benefit note is right for APY but not for PM-KMY. APY: on the subscriber's death, the spouse gets the same pension; after both die, the accumulated corpus goes to the nominee. PM-KMY: the spouse gets a family pension of 50% (Rs 1,500 a month); after both die, the corpus is credited back to the pension fund - there is no payout to a nominee. If a PM-KMY subscriber dies before 60, the spouse may continue the scheme or exit with the contributions plus interest. "I both" = In both; "theyare" = they are.

2. MGNREGA (Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act, 2005)

[Correction] "MNREGA" = MGNREGA. The rough notes use the past tense correctly: MGNREGA has been replaced by the Viksit Bharat - Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin) [VB-G RAM G] Act, 2025 (Presidential assent 21 December 2025; in force from 1 July 2026) - see Section 2.5. MGNREGA remains important for exams as the model of a rights-based, demand-driven employment guarantee.

2.1 Design of MGNREGA

  • Introduced as a pilot in the most backward districts in FY 2006 and then extended all over India. Implemented by the Ministry of Rural Development.
  • The Act guaranteed at least 100 days of wage employment in a financial year at a pre-determined (notified) wage.
  • The State government was responsible for the flow of funds to beneficiaries and for approving projects.
  • The Gram Panchayat was responsible for identifying (registering) beneficiaries and the projects to be implemented.
  • It was mandatory to pay wages within 15 days of completion of work.
  • If a worker demanded work and the panchayat could not provide it, the worker was eligible for an unemployment allowance fixed by the State government.

[Correction] Timeline: NREGA was notified on 7 September 2005 and launched on 2 February 2006 in 200 of the most backward districts (not 100); 130 more districts were added from 1 April 2007; it covered all rural districts from 1 April 2008 (not 2007). It was renamed Mahatma Gandhi NREGA on 2 October 2009.

[Correction] The guarantee was 100 days per rural household per financial year (not per working member), shared among adult members who volunteer for unskilled manual work - any rural household was eligible, not only BPL. Wage rates were notified by the Centre for each State under Section 6(1), indexed to CPI for Agricultural Labourers (CPI-AL). Men and women were paid equal wages, and at least one-third of beneficiaries had to be women.

[Correction] Roles and money: the Centre paid 100% of unskilled wages and 75% of material and skilled-wage costs; the States paid 25% of material costs and the unemployment allowance. The Gram Sabha recommended works and conducted social audits; the Gram Panchayat registered households, issued job cards and executed at least 50% of works by cost; the block Programme Officer and District Programme Coordinator sanctioned and supervised. Unemployment allowance was due if work was not given within 15 days of the application: at least one-fourth of the wage for the first 30 days and one-half thereafter. Delayed wages attracted compensation of 0.05% of unpaid wages per day after the 16th day.

2.2 Benefits of MGNREGA

  1. It acted as an efficient social security scheme in rural areas, protecting people from distress due to sudden events like the pandemic and natural disasters (drought, floods). It is counter-cyclical: demand rises automatically when other work disappears.
  2. MGNREGA acted like a wage floor, which helped stabilise and raise overall rural wages and the bargaining power of landless labourers.
  3. It benefited vulnerable groups more than proportionately: about 58% of person-days went to women, with large shares for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes.
  4. Corruption was reduced as wages were transferred through DBT into bank and post office accounts.
  5. [Additional] Rural assets: most works were water conservation, irrigation, land development and rural connectivity - natural-resource management that raises farm productivity.
  6. [Additional] Reduced distress migration and supported rural demand (consumption multiplier).
  7. [Additional] Women's empowerment: equal wages, work near home, and income in their own accounts.

[Correction] COVID-19 example: in FY 2020-21 the Budget Estimate (BE) was Rs 61,500 crore, enhanced to Rs 1,11,500 crore (PIB, 27 July 2022) - not "BE 73,000, RE 1,06,000". Rs 73,000 crore was the BE for the next year, FY 2021-22 (see Figure 2). Also: (i) the "wage floor" effect is real, but MGNREGA wages were below State minimum agricultural wages in many States, so it was a floor for the poorest, not a statutory minimum wage. (ii) Women's share of person-days was about 58-59% in recent years; SC and ST shares were each in the high teens to about one-fifth - the class figures (21% SC, 15% ST) are [Verification Required] for the specific year. (iii) DBT was strengthened through the National Electronic Fund Management System (NeFMS) and the Aadhaar-Based Payment System (ABPS), mandatory from 1 January 2024; geo-tagging of assets (GeoMGNREGA) added transparency.

2.4 Challenges of MGNREGA

  1. Wages were not paid on time, which reduced workers' confidence in the scheme.
  2. Workers' demand was deliberately not registered to avoid paying unemployment allowance.
  3. It created a shortage of agricultural workers during sowing and harvesting seasons.
  4. Corruption was observed through fake job cards and inflated or incorrect work measurements.
  5. The budget benefited better-administered (often richer) States, because they designed better projects and generated more demand, so inter-State inequality in benefits increased.
  6. MGNREGA did not provide adequate protection for workers in case of accident or illness.
  7. [Additional] Low average employment: households typically got around 50 days, far below the 100-day guarantee [Verification Required: year-wise average].
  8. [Additional] Fund shortfalls and supply-side rationing when allocations ran out mid-year; pending liabilities carried into the next year.
  9. [Additional] Technology exclusion: app-based attendance (NMMS) and ABPS failures delayed or denied wages; job-card deletions.
  10. [Additional] Poor asset quality and incomplete works in some States.

[Correction] Point 6 is overstated. Schedule II of the Act provided free medical treatment for workers injured at the worksite and ex gratia payment on death or permanent disability (Rs 25,000 in the original Schedule, or as notified by the Centre) [Verification Required: current amount], plus worksite facilities (drinking water, shade, first aid, crèche). The real gap was weak enforcement and no cover for illness unrelated to work. Point 3 is debated: evidence of farm-labour shortage is mixed, since households averaged far fewer than 100 days; the new Act responds with a 60-day pause in peak seasons. Point 5 should say "better-administered" States (e.g., Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh, Rajasthan drew more funds relative to their poverty than Bihar or Uttar Pradesh) - a commentary-based view, not an official finding.

[Additional] Debate (for Mains): supporters say the Act raises the guarantee, protects farm-labour supply, makes funding predictable and focuses on durable assets. 3. Model Answer: Examine the Role of PM Jan Dhan Yojana in Promoting Inclusive Growth (250 words)

Introduction. Inclusive growth means growth whose benefits reach all sections, especially the poor, women and rural households. Launched on 28 August 2014, PMJDY - the National Mission for Financial Inclusion - opened 59.09 crore zero-balance accounts with deposits of Rs 3.17 lakh crore (August 2026).

Role in inclusive growth

  1. Access for the excluded: 56% of account holders are women and 78% of accounts are rural/semi-urban; adult account ownership rose from about 53% (2014) to about 89% (Global Findex 2025).
  2. JAM and DBT: direct transfers remove middlemen and ghost beneficiaries, making welfare spending efficient.
  3. Savings and safety: DICGC-insured deposits replace cash and informal saving; average deposit rose to Rs 5,356.
  4. Risk protection: RuPay accident cover; gateway to PMJJBY (27.78 crore), PMSBY (58.66 crore) and APY (9.29 crore).
  5. Credit: transaction history enables overdraft, MUDRA and SHG loans, reducing dependence on moneylenders.
  6. Resilience and empowerment: COVID-19 cash transfers to women's accounts; base for UPI and digital payments.

Limitations

  1. Dormant and duplicate accounts; low balances.
  2. Access has outpaced usage of credit and insurance.
  3. Weak Business Correspondent network, biometric failures and cyber fraud (mule accounts).
  4. Low financial and digital literacy, especially among women.

Way forward. Re-KYC and activation drives; better BC remuneration; financial literacy through the National Strategy for Financial Education; credit products using account data; stronger fraud protection.

Conclusion. PMJDY has built the foundation of financial inclusion; deepening usage will turn access into inclusive growth.

Exam point: "Examine" means probe both role and limits with evidence. Use the access - usage - quality framework of the RBI FI-Index (70.0 in March 2026) as the analytical spine.

Key Facts for Prelims

  • PMJDY: launched 28 August 2014; DFS, Ministry of Finance; OD up to Rs 10,000; RuPay accident cover Rs 2 lakh (accounts after 28 August 2018); 59.09 crore accounts, Rs 3.17 lakh crore (19 August 2026); 55.7% women.
  • JAM trinity: named in Economic Survey 2014-15. Business Correspondents allowed by RBI since 2006.
  • KCC: 1998; extended to animal husbandry and fisheries 2018-19; collateral-free up to Rs 2 lakh (from 1 January 2025); MISS limit Rs 5 lakh; 7% interest, effective 4% on prompt repayment; 7.72 crore operative KCCs.
  • PM SVANidhi: 1 June 2020; MoHUA + DFS; loans Rs 15,000 / 25,000 / 50,000; 7% interest subsidy; cashback up to Rs 1,600; extended to 31 March 2030, outlay Rs 7,332 crore.
  • PMMY: 8 April 2015; DFS; MUDRA Ltd (SIDBI subsidiary) refinances; Shishu ≤ 50,000; Kishor to 5 lakh; Tarun to 10 lakh; Tarun Plus to 20 lakh; CGFMU guarantee.
  • PMJJBY: Rs 436, age 18-50, Rs 2 lakh life cover. PMSBY: Rs 20, age 18-70, Rs 2 lakh / Rs 1 lakh accident cover; no asset cover.
  • AB PM-JAY: 23 September 2018; NHA; Rs 5 lakh per family per year; secondary and tertiary care; all 70+ since 29 October 2024.
  • APY: age 18-40; Rs 1,000-5,000 a month; income-tax payers excluded from 1 October 2022; PFRDA.
  • PM-KMY: 12 September 2019; small and marginal farmers (up to 2 ha); Rs 55-200 a month, Centre matches; Rs 3,000 a month at 60; spouse 50% family pension.
  • NREGA: notified 7 September 2005; launched 2 February 2006 in 200 districts; all districts 1 April 2008; renamed MGNREGA 2 October 2009.
  • MGNREGA: 100 days per household; at least one-third women; unemployment allowance if no work in 15 days; wages within 15 days.
  • MGNREGA 2.0: 8 May 2012; Mihir Shah Committee; 30 new works.
  • VB-G RAM G Act 2025: assent 21 December 2025; in force 1 July 2026; 125 days; 60:40 (90:10 NE/Himalayan); 60-day seasonal pause; admin ceiling 9%.

Mains Points

  • Financial inclusion ladder: account (PMJDY) → insurance (PMJJBY, PMSBY, PM-JAY) → pension (APY, PM-KMY) → credit (KCC, MUDRA, SVANidhi). Each step reduces vulnerability and builds productive capacity.
  • Graduation design: SVANidhi tranches and MUDRA categories reward repayment with larger credit - building credit history and formalising informal enterprises.
  • Access vs usage: near-universal accounts, but dormant accounts, low balances and low insurance penetration (3.7% of GDP) show the next challenge is usage and quality.
  • Collateral-free credit and guarantees: CGFMU and Rs 2 lakh collateral-free KCC limit address the main barrier for the poor - lack of collateral - but raise NPA risks if appraisal is weak.
  • Health shocks: PM-JAY reduces catastrophic out-of-pocket spending, but excludes OPD costs, which form a large share of household health spending.
  • MGNREGA as automatic stabiliser: allocation rose about 81% in 2020-21; rights-based design, social audit and self-targeting (only those willing to do manual work at the notified wage apply).
  • Federal and fiscal angle of VB-G RAM G: higher guarantee vs 60:40 sharing and normative allocations - a trade-off between predictability and demand-driven rights.

Q1. UPSC CSE Prelims 2015 (GS Paper I) - PMJDY

'Pradhan Mantri Jan-Dhan Yojana' has been launched for

Q2. UPSC CSE Prelims 2016 (GS Paper I) - MUDRA

'Pradhan Mantri MUDRA Yojana' is aimed at

  1. bringing the small entrepreneurs into formal financial system
  2. providing loans to poor farmers for cultivating particular crops
  3. providing pensions to old and destitute persons
  4. funding the voluntary organizations involved in the promotion of skill development and employment generation

Answer: (a). Collateral-free credit for non-corporate, non-farm micro enterprises (Section 1.D).

Q3. UPSC CSE Prelims 2016 (GS Paper I) - Atal Pension Yojana

Regarding 'Atal Pension Yojana', which of the following statements is/are correct?

  1. It is a minimum guaranteed pension scheme mainly targeted at unorganized sector workers.
  2. Only one member of a family can join the scheme.
  3. Same amount of pension is guaranteed for the spouse for life after subscriber's death.
  1. 1 only
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: (c). Any eligible member can join; the spouse gets the same pension, then the nominee gets the corpus (Section 1.F).

Q4. UPSC CSE Prelims 2020 (GS Paper I) - Kisan Credit Card

Under the Kisan Credit Card scheme, short-term credit support is given to farmers for which of the following purposes?

  1. Working capital for maintenance of farm assets
  2. Purchase of combine harvesters, tractors and mini trucks
  3. Consumption requirements of farm households
  4. Post-harvest expenses
  5. Construction of family house and setting up of village cold storage facility
  1. 1, 2 and 5 only
  2. 1, 3 and 4 only
  3. 2, 3, 4 and 5 only
  4. 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5

Q5. UPSC CSE Prelims 2011 (GS Paper I) - MGNREGA eligibility

Among the following who are eligible to benefit from the "Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act"?
