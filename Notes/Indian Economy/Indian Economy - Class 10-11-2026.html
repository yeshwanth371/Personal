1. Inflation

1.1 Meaning and Calculation

Inflation is the rate at which the general price level of goods and services rises over a period, reducing the purchasing power of money. It is measured as a percentage and shows how prices change year-on-year (YoY).

[Correction] Inflation is a rate - the percentage change in a price index - and refers to a sustained rise in the general price level; a one-time rise in the price of one commodity is not inflation. YoY compares a month with the same month of the previous year (this removes seasonality). Three distinctions: disinflation = inflation falls but prices still rise; deflation = the general price level itself falls; a change in an inflation rate is quoted in percentage points (pp), not per cent (for example CPI inflation moved from 4.45% in July 2026 to 4.82% in August 2026, a rise of 0.37 pp).

Inflation rate (YoY)

Inflationt (%) = [ (It − It−12) / It−12 ] × 100

where It = price index of the current month and It−12 = index of the same month a year earlier (formula as given in the MoSPI FAQs on the CPI 2024 series).

Worked check with official numbers: CPI-Combined (base 2024=100) was 108.74 in August 2026 and 103.74 in August 2025, so inflation = (108.74 − 103.74) / 103.74 × 100 = 5.00 / 103.74 × 100 = 4.82%, matching the published figure (PIB release of 14 September 2026; indices are unitless numbers, base year = 100). A basket costing Rs 100 in August 2025 costs about Rs 104.82 in August 2026.

2. Measures of Inflation in India: PPI, WPI and CPI

In India inflation is measured through (1) the Producer Price Index (PPI), (2) the Wholesale Price Index (WPI) and (3) the Consumer Price Index (CPI). The class notes left the three entries blank; they are completed below from official sources.

[Correction] The PPI is new: DPIIT launched it on 15 June 2026 alongside the revised WPI, and it is not yet a headline measure. The WPI base moved from 2011-12 to 2022-23 on that date, and the CPI base from 2012=100 to 2024=100 on 12 February 2026 - older books still quote the old bases. [Additional] India also has other CPIs: CPI for Industrial Workers (CPI-IW), Agricultural Labourers (CPI-AL) and Rural Labourers (CPI-RL), all compiled by the Labour Bureau; and the broadest measure, the GDP deflator (covers all domestically produced goods and services).

2.1 Which Index Does the RBI Use?

For monetary policy formulation the RBI uses CPI data. It previously used WPI.

[Correction] Correct in substance; precise position: the RBI earlier gave more weight to WPI as its measure of inflation. After the Urjit Patel Committee (2014) recommended a new monetary policy framework with CPI-Combined as the nominal anchor, the RBI shifted to CPI. A Monetary Policy Framework Agreement between the Government and RBI followed (20 February 2015), and the RBI Act, 1934 was amended in 2016 (Finance Act, 2016) to create flexible inflation targeting (FIT): under Section 45ZA the Central Government, in consultation with the RBI, notifies the inflation target once every five years, and a six-member Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) sets the policy rate to meet it. The target is 4% CPI inflation with a tolerance band of +/- 2 percentage points (2% to 6%). News-agency reports of 25 March 2026 say the Centre notified the same 4% (2-6%) target for 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2031; the gazette notification itself was not opened - [Verification Required]. Not re-opened from primary sources: the 2014 committee, the 2015 agreement date and the 2016 MPC formation - standard RBI/Finance Ministry facts, to be cited as such.

[Additional] Why is WPI inflation (9.92%) about twice CPI inflation (4.82%) in August 2026? Composition: the WPI weights Fuel and Power at 14.11 - YoY inflation 22.93%, with Mineral Oils (weight 8.19) at 38.48% - whereas the CPI basket is dominated by food and services and tracks retail prices. This is a composition and coverage difference, not an error; policy is anchored on CPI.

3. Headline Inflation and Core Inflation

Headline inflation includes the total basket of goods and services (for example, CPI) and shows up immediately in consumers' costs. Core inflation excludes volatile items such as food and fuel (energy).

[Correction] "Headline" describes the all-items index of any price index: there is headline CPI inflation (4.82% in August 2026) and headline WPI inflation (9.92%). The one that reaches households directly is the CPI; WPI changes pass to consumers only partly and with a lag. "Reflects immediately on consumers" is therefore true of headline CPI, not of headline WPI.

[Correction] "Excludes volatile items like food and fuel" is the common textbook definition. 5. Stock vs Flow Variables

The core definitions and the tank analogy are in the 25 September notes (Figure 4 there). This section records today's wording and adds the stock-flow link.

Stock vs flow (class notes, refined)
BasisStock variableFlow variable
MeaningQuantity measured at a specific point in time - a snapshot ("as on 31 March")Quantity measured over a period of time ("per month", "per year")
NatureA level; changes only through flowsA rate of occurrence per period; the net flow equals the change in the related stock
Examples (class)Money supply, debt, wealthIncome, expenditure, saving in a month, GDP

[Correction] "Static in nature" means only that a stock is a level at one date; stocks do change over time, but only because flows add to or subtract from them. A flow is a quantity per unit of time (for example, Rs per year); describing it as the "rate of change of a variable" is shorthand - the precise link is change in stock over a period = inflows − outflows. "Snapsnot" = snapshot.

6. Real Flow and Money (Nominal) Flow

Real flow vs money flow in the circular flow of income
BasisReal / physical flowMoney / nominal flow
MeaningMovement of goods, services and factors of production between sectors; actual movement of resources and productsMovement of money used to pay for goods, services and factors of production; the financial side of the transactions
InvolvesResources and productsExchange of currency or payments
ExamplesLabour services to firms; cars delivered to householdsWages, rent, interest, profit; payments for goods and services

[Correction] "Moment of goods" = movement of goods. Terminology trap: the "nominal flow" here means a flow measured in money terms; it is not the same as nominal GDP (valued at current prices) versus real GDP (adjusted for price changes, at constant prices). "Real flow" in the circular flow means physical; "real" in real GDP means inflation-adjusted. Also, in the circular flow each real flow is matched by an opposite money flow.

[Additional] Real-world leakages from the loop are saving, taxes and imports; injections are investment, government spending and exports. This is why the flows of money in an actual economy do not simply cycle between two sectors; financial markets, the government and the rest of the world are added in later classes.

[Correction] The principle is right but the statement needs precision. Transfers are excluded because they only redistribute existing income; counting them would double-count output already counted when the payer earned it (see 29 September notes). 8. Keywords: Loan or Credit, Concessional Loan and Grant

Loan vs concessional (soft) loan vs grant
InstrumentRepaymentInterest / costTypical example
Loan / creditPrincipal must be paid backInterest at a rate reflecting the market and the borrower's riskBank loan, bond, commercial borrowing
Concessional (soft) loanMust be paid backBelow-market interest and/or longer maturity and grace periodDevelopment-agency lending (for example, the World Bank's IDA "credits"); interest-subvention schemes such as the Kisan Credit Card
GrantNo repaymentNoneGrants-in-aid from the Centre to States (Articles 275 and 282); development grants

[Correction] A loan must be repaid with interest, but the interest need not be "the market rate" - it may be fixed, floating or subsidised; "credit" is broader than a bank loan (trade credit, credit-card credit). A concessional loan is a loan on easier terms than the market offers: lower interest, longer repayment or a moratorium. "Consessional" = concessional.

Grant element of a loan

Grant element (%) = [ (Face value − PV of future repayments) / Face value ] × 100, where PV is discounted at a reference rate.

Illustrative (not an official estimate): Rs 100 lent at 0% interest, repayable after one year; at a 10% reference rate the PV of the repayment is Rs 100 / 1.10 = Rs 90.91, so the grant element is about 9.1%. A commercial loan has a grant element of 0%; a pure grant, 100%.

[Correction] "Grants: no need to pay back" is correct, but a grant can carry conditions (use for a specified purpose, matching contributions, reporting). A grant to a household or institution is a transfer (Section 7); it adds to the recipient's resources without a corresponding liability.

9. Assets and Liabilities

  • Assets: the resources owned by a person or organisation that have economic value and can provide future benefits. Types [Additional]: financial (cash, bank deposits, loans given, bonds, shares) and non-financial / physical (land, buildings, machinery).
  • Liabilities: obligations or debts that a person or organisation owes to others (loans taken, deposits accepted, bonds issued).
  • Net worth = Assets − Liabilities. Assets and liabilities are stocks (as on a date); the interest or income they produce is a flow (Section 5).

[Additional] The class notes define assets and liabilities but do not name the two sides of a claim. The party owed money is the creditor (it holds an asset); the party that owes it is the debtor (it has a liability). A depositor is a creditor of the bank: the deposit is the depositor's asset and the bank's liability. When the bank lends, the bank becomes the creditor and the borrower the debtor.

Key Facts for Prelims

  • Inflation = rate of rise in the general price level, YoY %. Formula: (It − It−12) / It−12 × 100. Inflation moves are in percentage points.
  • CPI (MoSPI/NSO): base 2024=100 from 12 February 2026; 358 items (308 goods, 50 services); HCES 2023-24; food and beverages weight 36.75% (was 45.86%).
  • WPI (DPIIT, Office of the Economic Adviser): base 2022-23 from 15 June 2026; 957 items (earlier 697); goods only; PPI launched alongside; WPI to continue for five years.
  • RBI targets CPI-Combined: 4% (+/- 2 pp); Section 45ZA, RBI Act 1934; six-member MPC; period 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2031 (reported 25 March 2026).
  • CPI-IW, CPI-AL, CPI-RL are compiled by the Labour Bureau; CPI (Rural/Urban/Combined) by the NSO.
  • Headline = all items; core = headline minus food and fuel.
  • Stock = at a point in time; flow = over a period. Money supply, debt, wealth = stocks; income, expenditure, saving, GDP = flows.
  • Real flow = goods, services and factors; money flow = payments. Factor income (rent, wages, interest, profit) is in GDP/GNP; transfers (pensions, scholarships, grants, donations) are not.
  • Loan: repay with interest. Concessional loan: repay on easier terms. Grant: no repayment.
  • Net worth = assets − liabilities.

Mains Points

  • Why CPI anchors monetary policy: it measures what households pay; WPI excludes services and retail margins and can diverge sharply (August 2026: WPI 9.92% vs CPI 4.82%, driven by fuel prices in WPI).
  • Headline vs core trade-off: core reveals underlying demand but, with a large food share in the basket, ignoring food can hide the inflation that poor households feel most; the RBI therefore targets headline while watching core.
  • Statistics as public infrastructure: base-year revisions in 2026 (CPI, WPI/PPI, GDP/IIP) improve relevance; they also create a break in series that complicates comparisons over time.
  • Measurement logic of national accounts: stocks vs flows, real vs money flows, and the exclusion of transfers make GDP a measure of production, not of welfare or redistribution.
  • Concessional finance and grants reduce the debt burden of borrowers (government or developing countries) compared with commercial borrowing.

Q1. UPSC CSE Prelims 2020 (GS Paper I) - CPI vs WPI

Consider the following statements:

  1. The weightage of food in Consumer Price Index (CPI) is higher than that in Wholesale Price Index (WPI).
  2. The WPI does not capture changes in the prices of services, which CPI does.
  3. The Reserve Bank of India has now adopted WPI as its key measure of inflation and to decide on changing the key policy rates.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 only
  3. 3 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: (a). Food weight is higher in CPI (36.75% in the 2024 series; 45.86% in the old 2012 series) than in WPI (Food Index weight about 25); WPI covers goods only; statement 3 is wrong because the RBI adopted CPI (Urjit Patel Committee) - see Section 2 and 2.1.

Q2. UPSC CSE Prelims 2013 (GS Paper I) - Inflation and debtors

Consider the following statements:

  1. Inflation benefits the debtors.
  2. Inflation benefits the bond-holders.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: (a). Inflation erodes the real value of money, so a debtor repays in rupees of lower purchasing power (benefit), while a bondholder receiving fixed interest and principal loses in real terms (Section 1.1).

Q3. UPSC CSE Prelims 2015 (GS Paper I) - CPI-IW

Which of the following brings out the 'Consumer Price Index Number for Industrial Workers'?

  1. The Reserve Bank of India
  2. The Department of Economic Affairs
  3. The Labour Bureau
  4. The Department of Personnel and Training

Answer: (c). The Labour Bureau (Ministry of Labour and Employment) compiles CPI-IW, CPI-AL and CPI-RL; the NSO compiles CPI (Rural/Urban/Combined) (Correction 2).

Q4. UPSC CSE Prelims 2010 (GS Paper I) - WPI and CPI-IW

With reference to India, consider the following statements:

  1. The Wholesale Price Index (WPI) in India is available on a monthly basis only.
  2. As compared to Consumer Price Index for Industrial Workers (CPI-IW), the WPI gives less weight to food articles.

Which of the statements given above is or are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. Both 1 and 2
  4. Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: (c). WPI is released monthly by the Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT; CPI-IW gives food a much higher weight than WPI. (Weights have since been revised; the relative ranking stands.)

Corrections Made

  1. Inflation: a rate, not a level; sustained; disinflation vs deflation; percentage points; YoY formula.
  2. Measures: PPI is new (15 June 2026); WPI base 2022-23; CPI base 2024; other CPIs (Labour Bureau) and GDP deflator.
  3. RBI and CPI: Urjit Patel Committee, FIT, Section 45ZA, MPC, 4% (2-6%) target; 2026-31 period reported by news agencies.
  4. Headline inflation: all-items inflation of any index; reaches consumers directly only for CPI.
  5. Core inflation: food-and-fuel exclusion is a convention; other core measures; spellings.
  6. Economic data: data diagnose and target; they do not themselves reduce unemployment or poverty; base-year revisions.
  7. Stock and flow: stock = level at a date; flow = per period; "static" and "rate of change" refined.
  8. Real and money flow: "movement" spelling; real flow is not real GDP; every real flow has an opposite money flow.
  9. Income: factor income counts in GDP on the income method; GDP vs GNP (NFIA); transfers excluded as redistribution.
  10. Loans: repayable with interest, not necessarily the market rate; credit is broader; concessional terms; grant element.
  11. Grants: may carry conditions; grants are transfers.
  12. Assets and liabilities: class definitions recorded; creditor-debtor terms and depositor-bank-borrower example added.

Spelling fixes in the rough notes: "oncludes" (includes), "moneteray" (monetary), "snapsnot" (snapshot), "moment" of goods (movement), "Scholorships" (scholarships), "Consessional" (concessional).
