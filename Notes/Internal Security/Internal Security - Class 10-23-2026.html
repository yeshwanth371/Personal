1. Border Management: Issues and Challenges

Border management is the whole-of-government effort to secure the border while allowing lawful movement and trade. It is wider than border guarding (the task of the border-guarding force on the line).

1.1 Issues with India's borders (three root problems)

Why India's borders are hard to manage
IssueMeaningEffect
1. Artificial / political bordersLines drawn by treaty, colonial arrangements or Partition, not by natural or ethnic boundaries (for example Radcliffe Line 1947, Durand Line, McMahon Line) [Additional]Divided ethnic and kin groups; cross-border ties; disputes over alignment
2. Troubled neighbourhoodUnsettled boundaries, hostile relations or weak governance in neighboursSafe havens, state-linked or non-state armed groups, infiltration
3. Porous bordersHard to seal because of terrain (mountains, rivers, desert, marsh, forest), length and population on both sidesSmuggling, illegal migration, infiltration, difficulty of fencing

1.2 Challenges at the border

  1. Cross-border terrorism - violence planned, trained or financed from across the border.
  2. Infiltration - illegal entry of armed or unarmed persons.
  3. Ceasefire violations - breaches of the 2003 India-Pakistan ceasefire understanding along the LoC and the International Border [Additional].
  4. Safe haven across the border for left-wing extremists (LWE), militants, terrorists and insurgents (for example camps in the neighbourhood).
  5. Sporadic aggression - occasional clashes, transgressions and face-offs.
  6. Organised crime - drug trafficking, human trafficking, gold smuggling, cattle smuggling, illegal migration, fake Indian currency notes (FICN) and arms smuggling.
  7. Maritime piracy - armed robbery and hijacking of ships.
  8. Illegal, unreported and unregulated (IUU) fishing by foreign vessels, and fishermen straying across maritime boundaries.
  9. Dark shipping - vessels that hide identity or movement: (a) stateless / unregistered vessels with no flag State, and (b) vessels that switch off the Automatic Identification System (AIS) transponder so that they are invisible on tracking systems.
[Correction] "Safe heaven" → safe haven; "neighnourhood" → neighbourhood; "Orgnaised" → Organised. Dark shipping is not only "no registration": the second form is a registered vessel that turns off AIS or spoofs it. Piracy, IUU fishing and dark shipping are maritime challenges that belong with coastal security, not only land borders. [Additional] India enacted the Maritime Anti-Piracy Act, 2022 to give domestic effect to UNCLOS provisions on piracy (commencement date [Verification Required]).

1.3 Components of border management

  • Ensuring the security and sanctity of the borders (guarding, fencing, surveillance, intelligence).
  • Ensuring legal and lawful exchange of people across the borders (immigration, passports, visas, border passes).
  • Maintaining diplomatic relations with neighbouring countries.
  • Coordinating with neighbours for smooth border management (joint patrols, border meetings, hotlines).
  • Ensuring legal economic exchange (trade through Integrated Check Posts, border haats, customs).

Exam point: border management combines security + facilitation + diplomacy + development. A pure "seal the border" approach fails where communities live on both sides.

[Additional] India shares a land border of roughly 15,100 km with seven countries (Bangladesh about 4,096 km, China about 3,488 km, Pakistan about 3,323 km, Nepal about 1,751 km, Myanmar about 1,643 km, Bhutan about 699 km, Afghanistan about 106 km) and a coastline of about 7,500 km. [Verification Required] figures are from memory of MHA Annual Reports; confirm in the latest report.

3. Challenges in Border Management and Initiatives

3.1 Porous borders → CIBMS

Comprehensive Integrated Border Management System (CIBMS) is an MHA initiative for stretches where physical fencing is difficult. It integrates people, weapons, sensors and communication into one command-and-control picture, to fill gaps in the older "fence and patrol" model.

CIBMS: components named in class
#ComponentPurpose
1CCTV camerasVisual monitoring
2Thermal imagers and night-vision devicesDetection in darkness and poor visibility
3Battlefield surveillance radarsLong-range detection of movement
4Underground monitoring sensorsDetect tunnels and digging [Additional]
5Laser barrier (intruder alarm)Alert on crossing the beam
  • The system was at a pilot stage on the India-Pakistan border in the notes (pilots in Jammu are reported; [Verification Required] locations and current status).
  • On the India-Bangladesh border a similar riverine model is BOLD-QIT (Border Electronically Dominated QRT Interception Technique), first tried in the Dhubri riverine area of Assam (about 61 km reported by a coaching source [Verification Required]). It combines sensors, cameras and communication links with Quick Reaction Teams (QRTs) of the BSF.
[Correction] "five layers" in the notes is better read as five technology components; the notes list them after the word "layers". "BOLD - QIT" → BOLD-QIT. "Survelliance / seemlessy / porousity" → surveillance / seamlessly / porosity. Pilots do not mean full coverage; treat roll-out status as [Verification Required].

3.2 Forces with multiple, dissimilar responsibilities

  • The BSF guards both the Pakistan and Bangladesh borders, which differ sharply in threat (hostile, terrain-heavy versus porous, population-dense).
  • The Assam Rifles guards the Myanmar border and is a counter-insurgency force.
  • Assam Rifles has dual control: administrative control with the Ministry of Home Affairs and operational control with the Army (Ministry of Defence); it is headed by an Army officer.

Suggestions (MHA / earlier reviews): (1) One border, one force; (2) settled borders to be managed by a CAPF and disputed borders (LoC, LAC) to be under the Indian Army. [Additional] The one-border-one-force idea is linked to the Kargil Review Committee (1999) and the Group of Ministers (2001) [Verification Required]. Merging Assam Rifles under MHA is a long-pending proposal; the Army opposes losing a trained jungle-warfare force.

3.3 Troubled neighbourhood → diplomacy

  • Diplomatic and legal settlement helps border management. Example: the India-Bangladesh Land Boundary Agreement (1974, protocol 2011) was implemented in 2015 through the 100th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2015, with an exchange of enclaves (111 Indian enclaves to Bangladesh and 51 Bangladeshi enclaves to India [Verification Required]). This removed a long-standing source of disputes.
  • Border haats (regulated local markets) operate on the India-Bangladesh border; the notes also mention India-Myanmar border haats [Verification Required]. They give locals a lawful livelihood and reduce smuggling.

3.4 Limited development at the border

Weak roads, services and jobs push border residents towards smuggling and make them less able to give information to the forces. MHA schemes named in class:

  1. Border Area Development Programme (BADP) - centrally funded; meets basic needs in border blocks and promotes a sense of security among residents [Additional].
  2. Vibrant Villages Programme (VVP) - for villages on the northern border; [Additional] VVP-I (approved Feb 2023) targeted about 662 villages and a later VVP-II is reported to extend to other land borders [Verification Required] (outlay, year).

Exam point: the Indian approach to the border is shifting from "guard the line" to "secure and develop the border community"; the border resident is treated as a partner in security.

4. Security Forces

4.1 Constitutional basis

  • Police and public order are State subjects (State List, Entries 2 and 1 of the Seventh Schedule) [Additional], so the State police is the first line of internal security.
  • Article 355 makes it the Union's duty to protect every State against external aggression and internal disturbance [Additional]; this is the base for deploying CAPFs to States.

4.2 State police and auxiliary forces

  • Unarmed civil police (for example traffic police) handle routine policing; armed police handle riots and serious disturbance: for example the Provincial Armed Constabulary (PAC) of Uttar Pradesh; other States have armed battalions under different names [Additional].
  • Auxiliary forces: Home Guards (volunteers under State Home Guards laws) support the police in emergencies, traffic control and civil defence [Additional].

4.4 Paramilitary forces (as classed in the notes)

  • Assam Rifles - India-Myanmar border and counter-insurgency in the North-East.
  • Indian Coast Guard (ICG) - maritime security, search and rescue (including fishermen in distress), and support to the Customs department; under the Ministry of Defence (Coast Guard Act, 1978 [Additional]).
  • Special Frontier Force (SFF) - covert operations on international borders; under the Cabinet Secretariat.

  • NSG (National Security Guard) - counter-terrorist and anti-hijack operations [Additional] (raised 1984).

5. Challenges Faced by Security Forces

  1. Multiple responsibilities not of similar nature (for example BSF on two very different borders; Assam Rifles on border and counter-insurgency). See Section 3.2.
  2. IPS leadership of CAPFs. A perception among CAPF cadre officers that IPS leadership affects operational efficiency and that the ranks find it hard to connect with leaders on short deputations. Defence of the system: IPS officers bring administrative and security-policy experience and act as a bridge with State police.
  3. Perception of not being treated on par with the defence forces (pay, pension, status, rank recognition).
  4. High attrition, suicides and fratricides, vacancies and overwork.
  5. Technology development not as dynamic as threats (drones, cyber, encrypted communication).

5.1 The IPS-deputation issue (law and court position)

  • The notes say the "CAPF act" recently reserved 100% of DG posts, 67% of ADG posts and 50% of IG posts for IPS officers. This matches the Central Armed Police Forces (General Administration) Bill, 2026, which Parliament passed on 2 April 2026 (Lok Sabha by voice vote after Rajya Sabha): 50% of IG posts and at least 67% of ADG posts to be filled by deputation, and all Special DG and DG posts by deputation only (AIR News, 2 April 2026, opened). Assent and commencement dates were not verified [Verification Required].
  • Reported background: on 23 May 2025 the Supreme Court recognised CAPF Group A executive cadre officers as Organised Group A Services (OGAS) and directed a cadre review and progressive reduction of IPS deputation; the review petition was reported dismissed in Oct 2025 [Verification Required]. Opposition members argued in Parliament that the Bill overrides the judgment; the Minister said it only gives a clear framework and strengthens cooperative federalism (as reported by AIR News). Any legal challenge to the Act is [Verification Required].

Suggestions in the notes: (1) increase the deputation period so that IPS leaders build rapport and understanding of the force; (2) draw deputationists from State cadres where the CAPF is also operating, so they know the ground. Added balance [Additional]: faster cadre review and promotions for cadre officers, and clear criteria for deputation.

[Correction] There is no single "CAPF Act" of the older kind: each force has its own Act (for example BSF Act, 1968; CRPF Act, 1949; CISF Act, 1968). The percentages come from the 2026 General Administration law, which covers the five major CAPFs (BSF, CRPF, CISF, ITBP, SSB) according to a secondary source [Verification Required].

Mains Points

  • Border management is multi-layered: it needs forces, technology (CIBMS), diplomacy (LBA 2015), economic exchange (border haats) and local development (BADP, VVP). Each alone is incomplete.
  • Different borders need different models: hostile and disputed borders (LoC, LAC) are military tasks; settled, open or riverine borders (Bangladesh, Nepal) are policing, trade and migration tasks. One force on two unlike borders (BSF) or on border plus counter-insurgency (Assam Rifles) dilutes focus.
  • Technology complements, not replaces, manpower: CIBMS reduces round-the-clock physical presence but needs maintenance, power, trained personnel and quick-reaction teams; pilots must be evaluated before scaling.
  • Community is a security asset: ending the FMR addresses security and demographic concerns but can disrupt cross-border kin and livelihood networks; calibrated passes and consultation lower friction.
  • Force-leadership debate: IPS deputation gives coordination with States and administrative experience; cadre officers argue for career progression and morale. A balanced approach: transparent, time-bound cadre review, adequate deputation tenure, and judicial and parliamentary clarity on the 2026 Act and the 2025 judgment.
  • Force welfare is operational efficiency: vacancies, leave, counselling and housing directly affect morale and effectiveness; publish periodic data and fill vacancies faster.
  • Way forward: (1) one-border-one-force where practical; (2) complete CIBMS roll-out with independent evaluation; (3) coordinated intelligence and anti-drone capability; (4) diplomacy on disputed alignments; (5) border-area development and livelihood; (6) welfare reform and cadre restructuring with consultation; (7) maritime domain awareness against IUU fishing and dark shipping.

Common Traps / Confusions

  • Border guarding is not border management.
  • IB (International Border) versus IB (Intelligence Bureau) - do not mix.
  • LoC versus LAC versus IB: LoC (India-Pakistan, J&K), LAC (India-China, not delineated), IB (settled border).
  • Assam does not border Myanmar.
  • SFF (Special Frontier Force) is not SSF; it is under the Cabinet Secretariat, not MHA.
  • Assam Rifles: MHA administrative, Army operational; do not say "under MHA" alone.
  • Intelligence (IB, R&AW, DRI, FIU-IND) is not investigation (NIA, ED, NCB, CBI).
  • CAPF is not the Army: CAPFs assist States and guard borders under MHA; the Army is under the Ministry of Defence.
  • Dark shipping includes AIS switch-off, not only unregistered ships.
  • Reported numbers (suicides, resignations, vacancies) must be quoted with period and source.
